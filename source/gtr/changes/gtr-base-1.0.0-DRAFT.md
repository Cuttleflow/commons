This is the first release of the Meridian Corporate Travel wording on the Commons. It is derived from the Meridian Corporate Travel Insurance Policy Wording v1.0.0 (July 2026), which was published outside the Commons. Changes from that wording:

- Domestic medical gap cover is removed. Paying the gap between a Medicare benefit and the fee charged is prohibited for a general insurer under the Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth) and the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (Cth). Medical expenses are covered outside Australia, and in Australia only for an insured person who is not Medicare-eligible. New Medicare and Health Insurance Laws provision.
- Limits follow the Meridian Corporate Travel Tenant Configuration Matrix (CFG-CT-2026): personal liability $2,500,000 (previously $5,000,000 in the wording) and rental vehicle excess $5,000 (previously $7,500). All limits are schedule variables with defaults.
- The kidnap, extortion and crisis response section is optional and applies only if the schedule shows it. Security evacuation and terrorism response benefits apply whether or not it is taken. Ransom is payable only where lawful, with an express carve-out for payments to listed terrorist organisations, and a confidentiality obligation subject to s.54.
- The personal accident section has a full table of events, a 12-month event window, a 14-day waiting period and an 85% of income cap on the weekly benefit, a reduced capital sum for accompanying family members, a disappearance provision and a new accumulation limit (default $5,000,000). The separate salary continuance benefit, which duplicated the weekly benefit, is removed.
- New definitions: Business Trip (with a distance threshold), DFAT Advice, Known Event, Epidemic or Pandemic, Kidnap, Terrorism, Unattended, Medicare-Eligible, Pre-existing Condition (12-month treatment and 3-month symptom tests) and others. Every defined term is used.
- New provisions on limits and excess (one excess per event per person), one benefit for one loss, other insurance and recovery (subject to s.45), who we pay, and a declaration and premium adjustment mechanism with a tolerance (default 25%).
- New exclusions: known events, illegal acts, manual work (written back by Endorsement E05), elective and deferrable treatment. The war exclusion has a 14-day grace period for insured persons caught by an outbreak of war. The pre-existing condition exclusion is subject to s.47.
- New general conditions: an express Insurance Contracts Act savings clause applying s.54 to every obligation, s.48 third-party beneficiary rights, cancellation under s.59, renewal under s.58, fraud under s.56, a standalone sanctions condition naming the regimes, GST apportionment for overseas travel, a fixed governing law from the schedule (default New South Wales), and jurisdiction.
- The endorsement library has 10 endorsements, each with operative text, schedule variables and a platform trigger. E02 now also covers leisure-only trips for named directors, E04 is limited to a traveller's own diagnosis or quarantine, E06 is renamed Long-Term Assignment, and E10 is renamed Employees Based Outside Australia.
- The licence is CC BY 4.0 (previously CC BY-SA 4.0), in line with the rest of the Commons.
- Clause numbers are removed. Each clause is identified by its heading.
