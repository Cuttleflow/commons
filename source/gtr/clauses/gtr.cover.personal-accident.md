If an insured person suffers an injury during a trip, and within 12 months of the accident the injury results in one of the events in this table, we will pay the percentage of the capital sum shown in the schedule against that event. An event that must be permanent only needs to have started within those 12 months. We assess whether it is permanent once 12 months have passed since the accident, and may do so after the 12 months have ended.

| Event | | Percentage of the capital sum |
|---|---|---|
| Event 1 | Death | 100% |
| Event 2 | Permanent total disablement | 100% |
| Event 3 | Permanent paraplegia or quadriplegia | 100% |
| Event 4 | Permanent total loss of sight in one or both eyes | 100% |
| Event 5 | Permanent total loss of use of one or more limbs | 100% |
| Event 6 | Permanent total loss of hearing in both ears | 80% |
| Event 7 | Permanent total loss of hearing in one ear | 20% |
| Event 8 | Permanent total loss of speech | 80% |
| Event 9 | Permanent total loss of the lens of one eye | 50% |
| Event 10 | Third-degree burns to 40% or more of the body surface | 50% |
| Event 11 | Permanent total loss of use of four fingers and the thumb of one hand | 75% |
| Event 12 | Permanent total loss of use of four fingers of one hand | 40% |
| Event 13 | Permanent total loss of use of a thumb: both joints / one joint | 30% / 15% |
| Event 14 | Permanent total loss of use of a finger: three joints / two joints / one joint | 15% / 10% / 5% |
| Event 15 | Permanent total loss of use of all toes of one foot / the big toe, both joints / the big toe, one joint / any other toe, each | 15% / 5% / 3% / 1% |
| Event 16 | Permanent partial disablement not listed above | The percentage of permanent loss of whole-body function, assessed under the Fifth Edition of the American Medical Association Guides to the Evaluation of Permanent Impairment (or the edition shown in the schedule), up to 75% |

Loss of use of a limb includes loss of the hand or foot, and loss of a limb, hand or digit includes its physical separation.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the Fifth Edition of the American Medical Association Guides to the Evaluation of Permanent Impairment is the right default edition, and that Event 16 may rely on it by reference.]

For an accompanying family member, the capital sum is 25% of the capital sum shown in the schedule, and for a dependent child it is the lower of that amount and the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $25,000).

If an insured person suffers temporary total disablement because of an injury during a trip, we will pay the weekly benefit shown in the schedule (default $2,000) for each week of the disablement after the first 14 days, for up to the number of weeks shown in the schedule (default 104 weeks). The weekly benefit is limited to 85% of the insured person's average weekly income from personal exertion in the 12 months before the injury, and is reduced by any workers compensation, transport accident compensation or other statutory income replacement payment they receive for the same disablement. We do not reduce it because of a payment under any personal accident, sickness or income protection insurance policy: [[ref:gtr.pay.other-insurance]] explains how this policy and those policies fit together. Weekly benefits do not apply to accompanying family members. Weekly benefits are paid to the insured person, or to you if you continue to pay their salary and they agree.

[WP REVIEW: confirm a 25% capital sum for accompanying family members, and the dependent child sub-limit, sit within the Insurance Contracts Act standard cover regime for sickness and accident contracts, or whether a s35(2) notice is needed for them.]

If the insured person disappears and cannot be found within 12 months after the disappearance of the conveyance in which they were travelling, we will treat them as having died from an injury, provided the person entitled to the benefit agrees to repay it if the insured person is later found alive.
