If an insured person suffers an injury or sickness during a trip, we will pay the reasonable costs, incurred within 12 months of the injury or the sickness first showing itself, of:

- medical, surgical, hospital, nursing, ambulance and prescribed medication expenses outside Australia and, for an insured person who is not Medicare-eligible, in Australia;
- emergency dental treatment to relieve sudden pain, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $5,000 for each insured person);
- emergency transport to the nearest hospital able to give appropriate treatment, and repatriation to their home when it is medically necessary, as decided below, including a medical escort;
- economy class travel and reasonable accommodation for one member of their immediate family to travel to be with them if they are hospitalised for more than 3 days or are dangerously ill, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $25,000); and
- if the insured person dies during a trip, the return of their remains to their home, or a funeral or cremation where they died.

The most we will pay under this section for each insured person for each trip is the limit shown in the schedule. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, it is $5,000,000. The most we will pay under this section for all insured persons together for one event is the event limit shown in the schedule. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, it is $20,000,000. If the claims for one event are more than the event limit, we reduce each insured person's payment in the same proportion. We pay only reasonable costs.

We must approve medical expenses of more than $1,000, and every evacuation or repatriation, before they are incurred, except in a life-threatening emergency when the insured person or someone with them must contact the emergency assistance service as soon as practicable afterwards. If prior approval was not obtained, [[ref:gtr.cond.insurance-contracts-act]] applies and we may reduce our payment only by the extra cost that approval would have avoided.

Whether and when evacuation or repatriation is medically necessary, and the method of transport, is decided by the treating medical practitioner together with our emergency assistance service. If they do not agree, we will arrange an opinion from an independent medical practitioner at our cost. Where an insured person refuses a repatriation that they recommend as medically necessary, we may reduce what we pay for further medical expenses by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced by the refusal.

For an insured person whose home is outside Australia, medical expenses incurred in the country of their home are not covered. If such a person is returned to their home country instead of to Australia, ongoing medical expenses there are covered up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $50,000).
