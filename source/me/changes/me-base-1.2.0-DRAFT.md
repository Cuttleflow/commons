Remediation following the Claude pre-review of 2 November 2026.

- About This Policy: new statement that the policy covers an allied health practitioner only while acting for the company, and is not written to be the insurance a National Board may require of a practitioner for their own practice. The retail-client statement is replaced by the library-wide wording and marked for review.
- Insured Person: refers to that statement.
- Vicarious Liability for Employees, Practitioners and Contracted Doctors: where the company is liable for a medical practitioner's conduct, we pay the company's whole liability, even if the practitioner is also liable. We may then seek contribution or recovery from the practitioner or their insurer. Allocation says the company's liability for the practitioner's conduct is not allocated away.
- Other Insurance: replaced with the library-wide text. Endorsement E15 now makes cover for an employed medical practitioner excess of the underlying medical indemnity policy identified in the schedule. If none is identified, Other Insurance applies. The Extended Reporting Period no longer depends on replacement insurance.
- Molestation (definition): limited to sexual conduct. Physical or psychological abuse, neglect or ill-treatment of a patient or resident that is not sexual in nature is covered as part of the company's liability. A claim alleging molestation remains subject to the Molestation exclusion and the Molestation Defence Costs extension.
- Inquiry and Regulatory Defence Costs: new paragraph for the legal costs of advice about, making or responding to a mandatory notification under the National Law. The clause pays for the company and insured persons, not for a visiting medical officer's or other medical practitioner's own representation. Section references for mandatory notifications are removed until verified, and marked for review.
- Statutory Liability: names the work health and safety penalty bans in New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland, excludes criminal fines, and applies advance-and-repay to defence costs for insured persons. The Fines, Penalties and Punitive Damages exclusion never covers a criminal fine. Marked for review.
- Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B: new condition for insured persons who are directors or officers.
- Cancellation: pro-rata refund in every case, with no retention for notified claims.
- Insurance Contracts Act and Sanctions: replaced with the library-wide text.
- Control Group: new definition. Severability and Non-imputation, Prior Known Circumstances and Retroactive Date, Intoxicants and Drugs, and Molestation Defence Costs now use it in place of directors, officers and senior managers. Your Duty of Disclosure refers to individual treatment.
- Unauthorised Access: new definition, used in the Cyber Events exclusion.
- Run-off Cover: the premium for months 13 to 84 is the amount in the schedule or, if none, 200% of the last annual premium. If it is not paid within 30 days of our notice, run-off ends at 12 months.
- Court Attendance: now has a default sub-limit of $25,000.
- Endorsement E15: the mental health paragraph now separates a scope limit (detention authorised by the mental health legislation) from an obligation to comply with it.
- Endorsement E16: the restrictive practices and communicable disease paragraphs now separate scope limits from obligations that operate on the section 54 basis.
- Default set for underwriter confirmation: Run-off premium for months 13 to 84 of 200% of the last annual premium where the schedule shows none; Court Attendance sub-limit of $25,000; Molestation limited to sexual conduct.
