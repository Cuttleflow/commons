We will pay your defence costs in defending a prosecution, civil-penalty proceeding or formal enforcement proceeding first brought against the company or an insured person during the policy period and notified to us during the policy period, under any Australian statute in connection with your healthcare operations, including proceedings under work health and safety legislation, privacy legislation, the Aged Care Act (including civil-penalty proceedings brought by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission), and food safety, radiation safety, and drugs and poisons legislation.

We will also pay fines and civil pecuniary penalties imposed on the company or an insured person in such a proceeding, but only to the extent the law allows them to be insured, and never a criminal fine.

We do not pay any fine or penalty that the law does not allow to be insured. In particular, we do not pay a penalty imposed under work health and safety law in a State or Territory whose law prohibits insurance of it (including New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland), and we do not pay a criminal fine. In those jurisdictions this extension provides defence costs only for work health and safety proceedings.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the list of jurisdictions that prohibit insurance of WHS penalties; library sources differ on WA and Victoria.]

Penalties are also not covered where the conduct was deliberate, dishonest, fraudulent or reckless, or where payment would be against public policy. Certain penalties, including penalties at the quantum available under the Aged Care Act and privacy legislation, may be uninsurable at law in whole or in part, and we pay only what the law permits.

[[ref:me.cond.corporations-act-199b]] applies to this extension for an insured person. Where we advance defence costs in a civil-penalty or criminal proceeding against an insured person, we advance them as they are incurred, and the insured person must repay them if a Final adjudication, or a court's finding, establishes conduct that this policy or the law does not allow to be covered.

[WP REVIEW: confirm what public policy and s 199B of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) allow for civil-penalty and criminal proceedings against insured persons, including directors and officers of the company.]

The sub-limit for this extension applies. This extension does not cover medical practitioners.
