The company, as the party proposing this contract, has a duty of disclosure under sections 21, 21A, and 22 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). This is a commercial (non-consumer) contract of insurance: the duty is to disclose to us, before the contract was entered into, every matter known to the company that is relevant to our decision to accept the risk and on what terms. A matter is known to the company if it is known to a member of the control group (see [[ref:sa.def.control-group]]).

As between us and each insured person, disclosure-related consequences are assessed individually — a contractual mechanism consistent with [[ref:sa.cond.severability]] — so that the company's non-fraudulent failure to disclose does not itself deprive an innocent insured person of cover (see [[ref:sa.cond.non-avoidance]]). This is a contractual mechanism, not an assertion that each insured person separately owes the statutory duty of disclosure under the Act.
