An endorsement applies only if its code is shown in the schedule. These endorsements can be attached to this wording or to another industrial special risks wording, including Mark IV and Mark V forms. The rules below form part of every endorsement.

**Defined terms.** In these endorsements, Base Wording means the industrial special risks policy wording named in the schedule. Where the Base Wording does not define the terms below, they have these meanings in the endorsements:

- we, us and our mean the insurer or insurers named in the schedule, each liable only for its own share and not jointly;
- you and your mean the insured named in the schedule;
- Situation means a location listed in the schedule, and any location added under an endorsement;
- Material Damage cover means the part of the Base Wording that covers physical loss, destruction or damage to property, and Business Interruption cover means the part that covers loss from interruption of or interference with the business;
- Damage means physical loss, destruction or damage;
- Limit of Liability means the limit shown in the schedule under LOLS or, if LOLS does not apply, the sum insured or limit that applies under the Base Wording;
- Event means one occurrence, or a series of occurrences arising from one originating cause, unless an endorsement sets an hours clause.

**Rules.**

1. Where an endorsement refers to a provision of the Base Wording by what it does, it means the provision or provisions shown against that endorsement in the schedule's base-clause references. If none are shown, it means every provision of the Base Wording that does that thing. [WP REVIEW: is the fallback certain enough to be enforced?]
2. A definition in an endorsement applies only within that endorsement.
3. If an endorsement conflicts with the Base Wording, the endorsement applies to the extent of the conflict. If the schedule shows a value for an endorsement variable, that value applies. If it does not, the default stated in the endorsement applies; if there is no default, the endorsement does not apply until we show the variable in the schedule.
4. A sub-limit in an endorsement is part of, not in addition to, the Limit of Liability, unless the endorsement or the schedule says it is in addition. No endorsement increases a limit unless it says so.
5. Nothing in an endorsement limits your rights under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where you do not comply with something an endorsement asks of you, we may refuse or reduce a claim only as section 54 of that Act allows.
6. All other terms of the policy continue to apply.

Endorsements shown as built in repeat provisions this wording already contains. They are for use with other base wordings and are not attached to this wording.
