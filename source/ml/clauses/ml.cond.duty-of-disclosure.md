The company, as the party proposing this contract, has a duty of disclosure under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). This is a commercial (non-consumer) contract of insurance. The duty is to tell us, before the company enters into this policy, and before it renews, extends, or varies it, everything the company knows, or could reasonably be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision to insure and on what terms. The company knows what a member of the control group knows.

The company does not need to tell us about matters that reduce the risk, that are common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we have told the company we do not need to know.

As between us and each insured person, disclosure and knowledge are assessed individually, as a matter of contract, under [[ref:ml.cond.severability]]. This is a contractual mechanism. It does not mean that each insured person separately owes the statutory duty of disclosure.
