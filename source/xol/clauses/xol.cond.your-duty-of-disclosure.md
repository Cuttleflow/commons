This is a contract of insurance for a business. It is not a consumer insurance contract. Before you enter into this policy with us, you have the duty of disclosure in section 21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): you must tell us every matter that you know, or that a reasonable person in the circumstances could be expected to know, is relevant to our decision whether to accept the risk of the insurance and, if so, on what terms. You have the same duty before you renew, extend, vary or reinstate this policy. For a company that is an insured, what it knows is what a member of its control group knows (see [[ref:xol.def.control-group]]).

You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk we insure you for, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive your duty to tell us about.

If you do not tell us something you are required to, we may reduce the amount we will pay you if you make a claim, or cancel this policy, or both, only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows. If your failure is fraudulent, we may refuse to pay a claim and treat this policy as if it never existed.

**Severability.** Disclosure is assessed separately for each insured. The non-disclosure or misrepresentation, or the knowledge or conduct, of one insured is not imputed to another insured. Where the followed policy gives an insured greater protection than this paragraph, because it promises not to avoid the policy, not to reduce a claim, or not to impute one insured's knowledge or conduct to another, this policy gives the same protection to the same insured. [[ref:xol.cover.follow-form-excess-layer]] says that this policy always follows those terms.
