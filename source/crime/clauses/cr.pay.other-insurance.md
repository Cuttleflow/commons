If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy, we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows. This does not apply where the schedule or an endorsement states that this policy is excess of a specified underlying policy, in which case this policy pays only after the limit of that specified policy is exhausted. The crime priority rule below is the one other exception.

**Crime priority rule.** Crime insurance is primary for loss of money, securities or property through social engineering fraud or funds transfer fraud, including computer-enabled theft of them (see [[ref:cr.cover.social-engineering-fraud]], [[ref:cr.cover.funds-transfer-fraud]] and [[ref:cr.cover.computer-crime]]). Where such a loss is also covered, or purportedly covered, under a cyber policy you hold, this policy pays first, whatever the cyber policy says about being excess to or contributing with this policy. The cyber policy then pays only the part of the loss above what this policy pays, up to the limit and sub-limit of the cyber policy. This applies whether or not we wrote the cyber policy. If the cyber policy also states that it is excess, we and the cyber insurer share the loss in proportion to our respective limits. This rule does not apply to a cyber policy that is written to attach in excess of this policy, such as a dedicated excess or difference-in-conditions layer.
