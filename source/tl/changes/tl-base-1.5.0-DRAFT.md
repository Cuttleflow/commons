Remediation following the Claude pre-review of 2 November 2026.

- About This Policy: the section table now shows each section by its heading only, and the cyber trigger reads "Claims-made for claims; discovery for first-party cyber loss". The notice about claims-made cover now says that first-party cyber covers respond to cyber loss first discovered in the policy period. The distribution paragraph moves to the new Who This Policy Is For clause.
- Who This Policy Is For (new): states that the wording is written for businesses and professionals, that some buyers may be retail clients, and that the issuer must then give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available.
- Loss: liquidated damages are no longer excluded. Amounts that the Outstanding Fees, Refund of Fees, Contractual Liability and PCI DSS Liability covers expressly provide are loss, despite the exclusions for your fees, restitution and merchant services costs.
- Cyber loss: now means the costs and losses you incur or suffer that the Cyber Protection section covers, measured before the retention and limit. It no longer means an amount we pay. It states when a cyber loss is first discovered. The stale reference to the Cyber Liability Extension is removed.
- Control group: now means your chief executive officer, chief financial officer, general counsel, head of risk or insurance, head of information security and directors, or a person in an equivalent role, with your most senior officer as the fallback. It applies to all sections.
- Extended reporting period (definition and extension): applies to claims only. A cyber loss must be first discovered in the policy period.
- Technology Professional Liability Insuring Agreement: replaces "arises solely from" with "to the extent the claim arises from". A claim that is partly outside cover is dealt with under Allocation.
- Data Restoration, Business Interruption, System Failure Business Interruption, Hardware Replacement, Reputational Harm and Cyber Crime: each now states that the event or loss must be first discovered in the policy period. The internal drafting note in Cyber Crime is removed.
- Cyber Extortion: you must tell us about the threat and get our written consent before paying a ransom. The payment is checked against the Sanctions condition and is not made if it would be an offence under the Criminal Code relating to terrorism financing. You must make any ransomware payment report the Cyber Security Act 2024 (Cth) requires, and give us a copy. We do not pay a civil penalty for failing to report. You must take reasonable steps to confirm the threat is genuine and help us and the incident response panel.
- Notification: a notified extortion threat now points to the Cyber Extortion consent requirement.
- Regulatory Defence and Fines, Regulatory Penalties and PCI DSS Liability: each now says we do not pay a fine or penalty that the law does not allow to be insured, and the first two name work health and safety penalties in New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland and criminal fines.
- Prior or Pending and Known Defect: the knowledge test now turns on what a member of the control group knew, and Prior or Pending applies to cyber loss as well as claims.
- Continuous Cover: awareness is now that of a member of the control group.
- Severability and Non-Imputation and Innocent Non-Disclosure: now covers knowledge and conduct as well as disclosure. One insured person's knowledge or conduct is not imputed to another when applying the Conduct, Prior or Pending, Known Defect and Continuous Cover clauses. A control group member's knowledge, and an executive officer's conduct, is imputed to the named insured and its subsidiaries.
- Retroactive Date: applies to claims. It does not apply to a cyber loss, which depends on discovery in the policy period and is subject to Prior or Pending.
- Non-Accumulation: a claim that could fall under more than one section is paid under the section with the highest limit. The worked example now uses the combined limit for the Technology Professional Liability and Cyber Protection sections and the separate limit for the Public and Products Liability section.
- Other Insurance: replaced. We do not refuse or reduce a payment because of other insurance, and we may seek contribution from the other insurer. A policy that the schedule or an endorsement says is excess of a named policy pays only after that policy's limit is exhausted. The cyber boundary rule and the rateable contribution rule are removed.
- Cancellation: replaced. You may cancel at any time and we refund the unexpired premium pro rata, less non-refundable government charges. We may cancel only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows, by the written notice that Act requires. The 30-day notice period is removed. A cancellation does not affect claims or notified circumstances that arose before it.
- Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): the s 54 paragraph is replaced with a statement that section 54 applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation, with the reduction and causation rules. The clause is renamed to match.
- Sanctions: moved out of the exclusions and the Exclusion Applicability Matrix into a general condition. It now covers any payment, claim or benefit that would expose us to a sanction under Australian law, or the law of the United Kingdom, the United States or the European Union to the extent it applies to us.
- Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B (new): states the limits on a company insuring its officers, and that this policy does not cover an insured person for a liability that section 199B prevents the company from insuring.
- Endorsement Library: now for use with version 1.5.x of this wording.
- General Conditions: a repeated word is removed from the threading note.
