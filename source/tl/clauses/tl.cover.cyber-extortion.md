We will reimburse reasonable costs to deal with, contain, or mitigate an extortion threat first discovered during the policy period, and the ransom you pay in response to it, on these terms:

(a) **Consent.** You must tell us about the extortion threat as soon as reasonably practicable. You must not pay or agree to pay a ransom without our prior written consent, which we will not unreasonably withhold. We may require the payment to be made through the incident response panel shown in the schedule.

(b) **Sanctions and terrorism-financing check.** Before we consent, the proposed payment and the person to be paid are checked against [[ref:tl.cond.sanctions]]. We do not consent to, and do not pay, a ransom if making it would expose us to a sanction, prohibition or restriction described in that condition, or would be an offence under the Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth) relating to the financing of terrorism or dealing with terrorist property. [WP REVIEW: confirm the sanctions screening step and the Criminal Code terrorism-financing carve-out for ransom payments.]

(c) **Reporting.** Where the Cyber Security Act 2024 (Cth) requires a report of a ransomware payment, you must make that report within the time the Act allows. This applies whether you make the payment or someone else makes it on your behalf. You must give us a copy of the report. We do not pay any civil penalty for failing to make a report. [WP REVIEW: confirm the ransomware payment reporting obligation, threshold and timing under the Cyber Security Act 2024 (Cth), and that a penalty for failing to report is not insurable.]

(d) **Assistance.** You must take reasonable steps to confirm that the threat is genuine, and give us and the incident response panel the information and help we reasonably ask for, including in dealing with police and regulators.
