**Who issues this policy.** The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

**Who this policy is for.** This wording is written for businesses and professionals. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement.

**Note for issuers.** Schools, clubs, employers and other organisations buy this policy for individuals. Where the policyholder or a covered person may be a retail client, the issuer must wrap this wording in a Product Disclosure Statement and prepare a Target Market Determination before the policy is sold.

**The policyholder's duty when it applies.** What the policyholder must tell us depends on the kind of contract this is.

- If this policy is a consumer insurance contract (taken out wholly or mainly for the personal, domestic or household purposes of the people it covers), the policyholder has a duty under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to us before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated.
- Otherwise, the policyholder has the duty of disclosure in s.21 of that Act. Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, the policyholder must tell us every matter it knows, or that a reasonable person in its circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure and on what terms. It need not tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive.

If the policyholder does not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows. Because covered persons claim under s.48 of that Act, a claim by a covered person can be affected by what the policyholder did or did not tell us. Covered persons do not have to tell us anything when they join, unless the schedule or an endorsement asks for a statement from them.

**Terms that differ from standard cover.** The Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) sets standard cover for sickness and accident contracts. This policy differs from standard cover. In particular:
- it does not cover a pre-existing condition (see [[ref:gpa.excl.pre-existing-conditions]]);
- it does not pay weekly sickness benefits for a sickness that first shows itself in the first 30 days after weekly sickness benefits first start (this does not apply on renewal, to a sickness that results from an injury, or to a person who transfers from the policyholder's previous group policy) (see [[ref:gpa.excl.initial-sickness-period]]);
- it does not pay weekly sickness benefits for disablement caused by pregnancy, childbirth or miscarriage, except for a complication that is not a normal part of pregnancy or childbirth, which it covers only from the 26th week of pregnancy (see [[ref:gpa.excl.pregnancy]]);
- it does not cover disablement caused by cosmetic or elective treatment (see [[ref:gpa.excl.elective-treatment]]);
- it does not cover an injury that happens while a covered person is taking part in a hazardous pursuit, in professional sport, in flying other than as a passenger, or in military service, or an injury to the extent caused by a covered person's alcohol or drug use, criminal act or malicious act (see [[ref:gpa.excl.hazardous-pursuits]], [[ref:gpa.excl.professional-sport]], [[ref:gpa.excl.flying]], [[ref:gpa.excl.military-service]], [[ref:gpa.excl.intoxication]] and [[ref:gpa.excl.criminal-acts]]);
- it does not cover intentional self-injury, war or nuclear risks (see [[ref:gpa.excl.intentional-self-injury]], [[ref:gpa.excl.war]] and [[ref:gpa.excl.nuclear]]);
- weekly benefits start only after the waiting period, stop at the end of the benefit period, are limited to a percentage of income, and stop at age 65 unless the schedule shows otherwise;
- weekly sickness benefits for a mental illness are limited to 26 weeks for any one sickness (see [[ref:gpa.pay.mental-illness]]);
- weekly benefits are reduced by workers compensation, other statutory payments and employer pay for the same disablement (see [[ref:gpa.pay.offsets]]);
- weekly benefits already paid for an injury are deducted from most capital benefits for the same injury (see [[ref:gpa.pay.weekly-and-capital]]);
- capital benefits are reduced where the total of capital benefits across our policies for the same life is above the accumulation limit (see [[ref:gpa.pay.accumulation]]);
- capital benefits are reduced from age 70, and no benefit is payable for an injury after age 75 (see [[ref:gpa.pay.age-limits]]);
- cover outside Australia is limited to 90 consecutive days on any one trip (see [[ref:gpa.obl.residence-and-travel]]);
- all benefits are subject to the aggregate limits (see [[ref:gpa.pay.aggregate-limits]]).

**Copies for covered persons.** The policyholder must make a copy of this wording and the relevant part of the schedule available to each covered person.

**Cooling-off period.** The policyholder may cancel this policy within 21 days of the date it starts, and within 21 days of each renewal, by telling us in writing. If no claim has been made, we will refund the full premium paid for that period, less any non-refundable government charges we cannot recover.

**Financial Claims Scheme.** If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, covered persons may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.
