**Who issues this policy.** The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

**Who this policy is for.** This wording is written for farm businesses that buy it for the business. Its sections are farm property, livestock, business interruption, theft, liability, machinery breakdown, transit and tax audit covers, which are not among the kinds of insurance the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) and its regulations treat as retail when bought for a small business. Most buyers will therefore be wholesale clients, and this wording is not a Product Disclosure Statement. The farmhouse and its contents, a registered vehicle of up to 2 tonnes carrying capacity, and personal accident and illness cover are retail kinds of insurance. They are not insured by this policy: a linked policy that insures them has its own Product Disclosure Statement and Target Market Determination.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that a hobby farm or lifestyle block that is not carried on as a business, insured for its farm buildings, farm contents and fencing, is not buying home building or personal and domestic property insurance under regs 7.1.12 and 7.1.13 of the Corporations Regulations 2001 (Cth), so that the policy stays a wholesale product for that buyer.]

**Who is insured.** You are the person, partnership, company or trust named in the schedule. [[ref:frm.cover.liability]] says who else is insured under [[ref:frm.section.liability|heading]].

**Your duty when you apply.** Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). You must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.

If this policy is a consumer insurance contract under that Act, you instead have a duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to us, and the Act sets out what we may do if you do not.

**Your enterprises matter.** We set the cover, the premium and the endorsements by the enterprises the farm business carries on and the share of farm income each brings in. Tell us every enterprise, any contracting you do for others, whether you spray chemicals, take in other owners' livestock on agistment, have paying guests or visitors who ride horses, and whether you sell produce direct to the public. Tell us during the policy period when the farm business changes, as [[ref:frm.obl.tell-us-about-changes]] requires.

**Flood and bushfire.** Flood is not covered unless [[ref:frm.end.e01]] is shown in the schedule. Damage caused by bushfire is covered, and a special excess may apply under [[ref:frm.end.e11]]. Cover for bushfire and flood may not start while an emergency warning is in force for the farm location, as [[ref:frm.cond.emergency-warnings]] explains.

**What this policy does not replace.** This policy does not provide workers compensation insurance, which each state and territory requires an employer to hold through its own scheme. It does not insure the farmhouse, your vehicles on the road, crops while they are growing, land, pasture or water, or the cost of drought.

**Cooling-off period.** You may cancel this policy within 21 days of the date it starts, and within 21 days of each renewal, by telling us in writing. If you have not made a claim, we will refund the full premium paid for that period, less any non-refundable government charges we cannot recover. After that, you may still cancel under [[ref:frm.cond.cancellation]].

**Commission and fees.** The schedule or the quote shows any commission or fee paid to your broker in connection with this policy.

**Financial Claims Scheme.** If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, you may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.
