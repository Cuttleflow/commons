**Who issues this policy.** The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

**The statutory basis of this policy.** This wording is drafted to meet the requirements for decennial liability insurance in s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW) and cll.55A to 55C of the Strata Schemes Management Regulation 2016 (NSW). A policy is decennial liability insurance for the purposes of that Act only if the Secretary accepts its form. The Secretary has not accepted this wording. An insurer that adopts it must obtain that acceptance before a developer relies on the policy instead of lodging a building bond.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the current text of s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW), as amended by the Fair Trading and Building Legislation Amendment Act 2026 (NSW), and of cll.55A to 55C of the Strata Schemes Management Regulation 2016 (NSW), against this wording, including the definitions of relevant defect and building element.]

[WP REVIEW: Building Commission NSW guidance says that from 1 December 2026 a developer of a Class 2 building of three storeys or fewer may take out decennial liability insurance instead of lodging a building bond, while s.211AA and the same guidance refer to buildings of more than three storeys. Confirm which buildings may use decennial liability insurance.]

**Who this policy is for.** The developer of a new residential apartment building in New South Wales takes out this policy for the benefit of the owners corporation. This wording is designed for distribution to developers that are wholesale clients within the meaning of s.761G of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). It is not a Product Disclosure Statement, and no Target Market Determination has been prepared for it.

[WP REVIEW: confirm whether decennial liability insurance over a residential strata building is a home building insurance product, and so a retail product under s.761G(5) of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) where the developer is a small business; whether the owners corporation's rights as the insured make it a retail product; and whether AFCA membership is required for claims by the owners corporation.]

**The developer's duty of disclosure.** Before the developer enters into this policy, s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires it to tell us every matter it knows, or that a reasonable person in its circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure and on what terms. It does not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. Only the developer owes this duty. The owners corporation does not, and a failure by the developer does not reduce the owners corporation's cover: [[ref:dli.cond.protection-of-the-owners-corporation]] explains this.

**The owners corporation's right to claim.** The owners corporation is the insured under this policy and may claim under it directly. It does not need the developer's consent or help to claim.

**Terrorism.** Where this policy is an eligible insurance contract under the Terrorism Insurance Act 2003 (Cth), [[ref:dli.excl.terrorism]] has no effect for loss arising from a declared terrorist incident, and our liability may be reduced by any reduction percentage declared under that Act.

**Financial Claims Scheme.** If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, the owners corporation may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.
