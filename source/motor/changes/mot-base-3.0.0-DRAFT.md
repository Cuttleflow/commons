This is the first Commons release of the Meridian Commercial Motor wording. It replaces version 2.0.0 (July 2026) and Small Fleet Motor version 1.0.0 (2026/SFMO/0001), both published outside the Commons. The cover is that of version 2.0.0 except where this note says otherwise. Changes from version 2.0.0:

- Clause numbers are removed. Each clause is identified by its heading, and cross-references name the clause they point to.
- The exclusions that applied to both vehicle cover and liability are moved into one General Exclusions section. The unlicensed driver and alcohol and drugs exclusions no longer apply where you did not know of, or consent to, the driver's state; version 2.0.0 relied on s 54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) for that result.
- A general condition states that the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) applies and that s 54 governs every condition and exclusion. Version 2.0.0 cited s 54 only in the late notification clause.
- Governing law is the law of the State or Territory shown in the Schedule at inception, with submission to the jurisdiction of its courts. Version 2.0.0 followed the insured's principal place of business, which can move.
- The sanctions condition names the Australian and foreign regimes.
- The amounts in the additional benefits, excesses and fleet provisions are Schedule values, listed with their defaults in a Default Schedule Values appendix. The defaults are those of version 2.0.0.
- New definitions: Dangerous goods, Declaration date, Endorsement, Fleet basis, Period of insurance, Substitute vehicle. Total loss now takes the theft period from the Schedule.
- The fleet provisions add duty and levies by garaging state, an automatic-cover value limit, and the annual declaration adjustment as an option beside pro-rata adjustment.
- The insurer may cancel only on the grounds the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) permits, with at least 30 days' written notice.
- Each of the 18 endorsements now has operative text. Version 2.0.0 listed their titles only. Each states when the platform uses it (questions, default rules, reason and underwriter responses); the trigger is a wording note on the web page and is not part of the policy.
- The Private Motor wording (2026/PMV/0001) is built on this wording.
- The licence is now CC BY 4.0, in line with the rest of the Commons. Version 2.0.0 was CC BY-SA 4.0.
