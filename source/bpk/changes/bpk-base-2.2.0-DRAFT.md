Remediation following the Claude pre-review of 2 November 2026. Changes from version 2.1.0:

- Limits and More Than One Section: sections now respond together to different parts of one event (a fire is paid under Property Damage and Business Interruption). The one-section rule applies only where the same part of a loss is covered twice. A loss of funds covered by both Crime and Cyber is paid under Crime where Crime is insured. For any other overlap the section giving the greater amount pays. The same loss is never paid twice.
- Excess: for one event under more than one section, you bear the highest of the Excesses of the sections that pay, once.
- Other Insurance: replaced. We do not refuse or reduce a payment because of other insurance, and we may seek contribution. This Policy is excess only of a specified underlying policy named in the Schedule or an Endorsement. The separate Crime and Cyber rule now sits in Limits and More Than One Section.
- Relationship With Comprehensive Crime: now points to Limits and More Than One Section for priority.
- Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) (previously headed The Insurance Contracts Act): replaced with a full statement that section 54 applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation.
- Sanctions: replaced with a standalone condition naming the Australian regimes and, to the extent they apply to us, the UK, US and EU regimes. United Nations resolutions are no longer named separately. Wording Partner review tag added.
- Cancellation: restated. You may cancel at any time and receive a pro-rata refund. No refund applies to insured property for which we have paid a total loss. We may cancel only as the Insurance Contracts Act allows.
- Business Interruption Exclusions: the first exclusion no longer removes loss covered by the vicinity-damage limb of Interruption of the Business or by the Business Interruption Additional Benefits.
- Cyber (general exclusion): does not apply to Damage caused by Breakdown that the Equipment section covers. The Exclusion Applicability Table says the same.
- Known Defects: now tied to loss or Damage caused by a Defect, with a new definition of Defect and a new definition of Control group. It turns on what a member of the Control group knew. It does not apply to Public and Products Liability, and the Exclusion Applicability Table says so. The Tax Audit and Cyber prior-matters exclusions also use the Control group.
- E07 Product Recall Costs: now has its own promise to pay the costs of recalling Your products, within the recall sub-limit, with stated exclusions. Repair, replacement and refund of the products stay excluded.
- E11 Voluntary Workers' Personal Accident: defines voluntary worker, voluntary work and Disablement, and states how the Endorsement fits with the Employee definition, the Injury to Employees exclusion and workers' compensation.
- Management Liability: new condition on sections 199A and 199B of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), replacing the separate statement of section 199B. Defence costs advanced in a civil penalty or criminal proceeding must be repaid if a Final adjudication or court finding establishes conduct that cannot be covered. The fines and penalties position is restated in Statutory Insurability and at the Statutory Liability and Cyber Regulatory promises. It names New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland and excludes criminal fines. Wording Partner review tags added.
- Who This Policy Is For: restated to say that some buyers may be retail clients, in which case a Product Disclosure Statement and Target Market Determination are needed.
- The Endorsement Library and Exclusion Applicability Table: table cells now show their content in the Word file.
