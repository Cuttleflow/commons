This is the first Commons release of the Meridian Business Package wording. It replaces version 2.0.0 (October 2026), which was published outside the Commons. Version 2.0.0 restructured the earlier v1.0.0 into the eleven sections below; this release keeps that cover and changes how the wording is written. Changes from version 2.0.0:

- Clause numbers are removed. Each clause is identified by its heading, and cross-references name the clause they point to. The general exclusions and general conditions are no longer coded (GE1 to GE14, GC1 to GC18), and sections are named rather than numbered.
- Definitions that applied to one section only (Breakdown, Claim, Computer system, Cyber event, Data, Discovery, Dishonest act, Employment claim, Extended reporting period, Extortion threat, Final adjudication, Insured person, Loss, Occurrence, Personal injury, Property damage, Responsible person, Single loss, Theft, Third party, Unauthorised access, Wrongful act and Your products) are moved into the one definitions list. Each says where it applies. Claim keeps its two meanings, one for the Cyber section and one for the Management Liability section, in one definition.
- The insuring clauses of the Comprehensive Crime, Cyber and Management Liability sections, and the exclusions of the Public and Products Liability and Cyber sections, are separate clauses, so that other clauses and endorsements can refer to them.
- Cyber Data Restoration now responds to a Cyber event first discovered during the Period of Insurance, and Cyber Extortion to an Extortion threat first made during it. Version 2.0.0 stated no time trigger for either; the aggregation clause already treated them as discovery-based.
- The exclusion applicability matrix (Appendix B in version 2.0.0) is rewritten as a table naming, for each general exclusion, the sections it does not apply to and the sections it applies to as modified. Its effect is unchanged.
- The Underinsurance (Property) clause carries a Wording Partner review tag on the notice of average required by s 44 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).
- Each endorsement now states when the platform uses it (questions, default rules, reason and underwriter responses). The trigger is a wording note on the web page and is not part of the policy.
- The licence is now CC BY 4.0, in line with the rest of the Commons. Version 2.0.0 was CC BY-SA 4.0.
