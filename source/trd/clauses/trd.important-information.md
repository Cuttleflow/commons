**Who issues this policy.** The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

**Who this policy is for.** This wording is written for trade businesses that buy it for the business. Its sections are liability, business property, contract works, tax audit, payment fraud and business interruption covers, which are not among the kinds of insurance the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) and its regulations treat as retail when bought for a small business. Most buyers will therefore be wholesale clients, and this wording is not a Product Disclosure Statement. A linked motor policy or a personal accident and illness policy may be a retail product, and has its own Product Disclosure Statement and Target Market Determination.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that Tools and Portable Equipment cover bought by a sole trader for tools used in the trade is not personal and domestic property insurance under reg 7.1.12 of the Corporations Regulations 2001 (Cth), so that the package stays a wholesale product.]

**Who is insured.** You are the person, partnership, company or trust named in the schedule. Each section says who else it insures.

**Your duty when you apply.** Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). You must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.

If this policy is a consumer insurance contract under that Act, you instead have a duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to us, and the Act sets out what we may do if you do not.

**Your trade matters.** We set the cover and the endorsements by the trade you carry on. Tell us every trade and activity in the business, and whether you work at height, excavate, do hot work, waterproofing, roof work, gas fitting or tree work, use subcontractors, or do residential building work for which the law requires statutory insurance. Tell us during the policy period when the business changes, as [[ref:trd.obl.tell-us-about-changes]] requires.

**What this policy does not replace.** This policy does not provide workers compensation insurance, which each state and territory requires an employer to hold through its own scheme. It is not statutory home warranty insurance (also called home building compensation, domestic building insurance or home warranty), which the law requires for residential building work above a value set by each state and territory. It does not insure advice or design you give for a fee, or your vehicles on the road.

**Cooling-off period.** You may cancel this policy within 21 days of the date it starts, and within 21 days of each renewal, by telling us in writing. If you have not made a claim, we will refund the full premium paid for that period, less any non-refundable government charges we cannot recover. After that, you may still cancel under [[ref:trd.cond.cancellation]].

**Commission and fees.** The schedule or the quote shows any commission or fee paid to your broker in connection with this policy.

**Financial Claims Scheme.** If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, you may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.
