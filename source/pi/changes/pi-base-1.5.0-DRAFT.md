Remediation following the Claude pre-review of 2 November 2026.

- Endorsement Library: only E01 is now listed as available, because it has its operative terms in the Run-Off Cover extension. E02, E04, E05, E06, E07, E08 and E10 are shown as not yet available in this version, because no operative text exists for them. E03 is replaced by a sentence that profession wordings are published separately. E09 is removed, because the wording has no prior and pending litigation exclusion for it to adjust.
- Compulsory Insurance Requirements condition: now says how a requirement is identified, who is paid (you, or the claimant where the requirement gives a direct right), and that payments reduce the limit of liability and use the one reinstatement. A term of the policy, including the retroactive date and the exclusions, is read down only to the extent the requirement does not permit it. The condition does not increase the limit of liability. Flagged for legal review. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
- Licences, Registrations and Accreditations condition: whether the licence or registration was held is tested when the service was provided, and a later lapse does not remove cover for work already done. Flagged for review.
- Professional Standards Schemes condition: flagged for review of the approved schemes and their caps.
- Notification of Circumstances and Tell Us About Circumstances: the test is now facts that might give rise to a claim, matching section 40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where that section gives you greater protection, it applies. The listed notice contents are now things the notice should include, as far as you are able.
- Claims Against You: a formal complaint or proceeding before a professional disciplinary body is now stated to be a claim for which we pay defence costs only, within a default sub-limit. It does not pay fines, penalties or costs orders in favour of the body. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
- Inquiry Costs, Statutory Liability and Avoiding a Potential Claim: each is now tied to the retroactive date. Inquiry Costs also says that a disciplinary proceeding is dealt with under Claims Against You.
- Retroactive date: if no date is shown in the schedule, the date the policy first started (or the earliest of an unbroken series of policies we issued) applies. A date that an extension expressly sets for a claim applies in place of the schedule date. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
- Statutory Liability extension: now has a default sub-limit. The fines wording names the work health and safety jurisdictions that prohibit insurance of penalties and excludes criminal fines. The exclusion for deliberate or reckless conduct now turns on a Final adjudication, with defence costs advanced and repaid if the conduct is established. It refers to the new Corporations Act condition for directors and officers. Flagged for review.
- Sub-Limits provision: default sub-limits are now stated for disciplinary defence costs, Statutory Liability, First-Party Dishonesty, Public Relations Costs and Court Attendance. First-Party Dishonesty, Public Relations Costs and Court Attendance now refer to them. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
- War and Terrorism exclusion (renamed from War, Terrorism, and Sanctions): sanctions are removed from it. A new Sanctions condition limits payment where it would expose us to Australian sanctions law, or the sanctions laws of the United Kingdom, the United States or the European Union that apply to us. Flagged for review.
- Financial Services exclusion: now limited to financial services and credit activities as those terms are used in the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) and the National Consumer Credit Protection Act 2009 (Cth), instead of any activity regulated by ASIC, APRA or an equivalent regulator. It confirms that audit, review, insolvency and disclosure-document work is not removed. Flagged for review.
- Run-Off Cover extension: now starts only when the company permanently ceases its business activity, not when any insured person stops practising. It states what the automatic cover does.
- New definition: Control group. Prior Known Circumstances, Continuous Cover and Your Duty of Disclosure now turn on what a member of the control group knew, and on each insured person's own knowledge, and one insured person's knowledge is not imputed to another.
- Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition: replaced by a statement that nothing in the policy limits the Act, and that section 54 applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation, not only the claims conditions.
- Other Insurance provision: replaced. We do not refuse or reduce payment because of other insurance, and we may seek contribution. This does not apply where the schedule or an endorsement makes the policy excess of a named policy.
- Cancellation condition: replaced. You may cancel at any time and we refund the unexpired premium pro rata, less non-refundable government charges. We may cancel only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows. Notified claims and circumstances are not affected.
- Governing Law condition and Schedule: the governing law is now a schedule item fixed at the start of the policy period. The schedule also lists any compulsory insurance requirement that applies to you.
- New: Who This Policy Is For, which states that some buyers may be retail clients and that the issuer must then give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available.
- New: Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition, for insured persons who are directors or officers. Flagged for review.
