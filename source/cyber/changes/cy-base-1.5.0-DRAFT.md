Remediation following the Claude pre-review of 2 November 2026.

- Cyber Extortion: we reimburse a ransom only if you had our prior written consent; the paragraph that reimbursed an unconsented ransom where delay would have caused greater loss is removed. Consent now also depends on the payment not breaching the terrorism-financing offences in the Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth). A new paragraph deals with reporting a ransomware payment under the Cyber Security Act 2024 (Cth): tell us before you pay, we help with the report, and we do not pay a penalty for failing to report.
- How to Respond to a Cyber Incident: a new step about reporting a ransomware payment.
- Sanctions: moved out of the exclusions and replaced with the library's standard general condition.
- War and State-Backed Cyber Operations: "civil unrest" and "any similar belligerent act" are removed from limb (a). A new carve-back keeps cover for your computer systems and data located outside the state that suffered the harm, if they are not themselves used by or for a state.
- Retroactive Date, Prior Known Events and Media Liability: the retroactive date now also applies to the wrongful act or publication that gives rise to a media claim.
- What Is Covered, Your Own Losses: a single trigger for first-party cover. Incident Response Costs, Business Interruption, Data and System Restoration and Dependent Business Interruption all respond to a cyber event, privacy breach or dependent event that you first discover during the policy period. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
- Social Engineering Fraud: a missed verification step no longer removes cover. We may reduce payment only to the extent the failure caused or contributed to the loss.
- Other Insurance: replaced with the library's standard text, plus one crime priority rule. Crime insurance is primary for loss of funds through social engineering or funds transfer fraud, and this policy pays only the excess over it.
- Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): replaced with the library's standard section 54 text.
- Cancellation and Extended Reporting: you may cancel at any time and receive a pro-rata refund, without deduction for amounts paid or reserved. We may cancel only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows. The automatic extended reporting period is unchanged.
- Control group: a new definition. Prior Known Events, Your Duty of Disclosure and Deliberate and Dishonest Acts now turn on what a member of the control group knew or did, and one insured's knowledge or conduct is not imputed to another.
- Regulatory Fines and Penalties: redrafted. It is limited to fines imposed in Australia or New Zealand, it names the penalties that cannot be insured (work health and safety penalties in the States and Territories that prohibit insurance of them, criminal fines and penalties for failing to report), it no longer says that civil penalties are generally insurable, and defence costs are advanced and repayable on final adjudication. It no longer reaches a pure failure to comply with a privacy law.
- Related events: a new definition used in Claim, Insured loss, Widespread event and E12 Aggregate Limit Reinstatement.
- Territory and Jurisdiction: the courts clause is no longer exclusive, and a service of suit paragraph is added for an insurer not authorised in Australia.
- Who This Policy Is For: a new statement about retail clients, the Product Disclosure Statement and the Target Market Determination.
- Endorsement Library: the endorsements are now for use with version 1.5.x.
