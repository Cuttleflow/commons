Which Act applies depends on how the goods are carried, not on whether the transit is an import, an export or an inland transit.

- A [[ref:mcg.def.sea-transit|heading]], including coastal carriage between Australian ports, is marine insurance. The Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) governs it, and the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) does not apply to it by force of its own terms.
- A [[ref:mcg.def.non-sea-transit|heading]], that is carriage by air, post, courier, road or rail, is governed by the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Each Act applies to this policy only for the part to which it applies. A remedy that one Act gives us for a Sea Transit does not reach a Non-Sea Transit.

To the extent the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) applies, nothing in this policy excludes, restricts or modifies any right you have under it. [[ref:mcg.cond.insurance-contracts-act]] says how section 54 applies, including to Sea Transits.

The obligations in this policy, including those in the Obligations section, in an endorsement and in the Institute Clauses that this policy applies, are obligations and conditions of cover. They are not warranties. What follows if you do not meet one is set out in [[ref:mcg.cond.insurance-contracts-act]]. The warranties that the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) implies by law are not changed by this policy.
