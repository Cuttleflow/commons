Allied Health Insurance

Combined professional indemnity, public liability and products liability for individual allied health practitioners, written on a claims-made basis to the National Boards' registration standards.

About This Policy

This policy is a contract of insurance between you, the allied health practitioner named in the schedule, and us, the insurer named in the schedule. In return for your payment of the premium, we agree to insure you on the terms set out in this document, the schedule and any endorsements we issue to you. Read the three documents together: they form one contract.

The policy has two operative sections, and both are written on a claims-made and notified basis:

  • Section A: Professional Indemnity covers your civil liability for malpractice and for certain related civil liabilities arising from your professional services, together with extensions that respond to the realities of allied health practice in Australia: regulatory inquiries, Ahpra and National Board proceedings, coronial inquests, Medicare and NDIS audits, data breaches and more.
  • Section B: Public Liability covers your civil liability for bodily injury and property damage arising from the conduct of your practice, for example a client who slips in your waiting room. Products liability, your civil liability arising from goods you supply in connection with your professional services, is provided by the Products Liability extension, so one set of terms and one policy period governs everything.

Because the whole policy is claims-made, it covers only claims first made against you and notified to us during the policy period. When the policy period ends, so does your right to notify, subject to s.40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) and the continuous cover, run-off and extended reporting provisions of this policy. Before you enter into this contract, your insurer or its distributor must give you written notice of the effect of s.40(3), as s.40(1) of that Act requires. The Notification of Circumstances section explains how notifying a circumstance protects you.

This policy is designed to align with the professional indemnity insurance registration standards issued by the National Boards under the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law. It gives retroactive cover back to the start of your continuous series of cover (see the definition of Retroactive Date), one automatic reinstatement (see the Automatic Reinstatement provision), and run-off cover when you cease practice (see the Run-off Cover on Retirement or Cessation of Practice extension). It covers the professional services described in your schedule, within the scope of practice of your registration or association membership. A National Board's standard may require more than the defaults in this policy, for example a longer run-off period, which Endorsement E06 provides. [WP REVIEW: check each National Board's current professional indemnity registration standard against the retroactive cover, run-off period and reinstatement in this policy, and against the Sexual Misconduct, Intoxicants and Obstetrics exclusions; verify the National Law section references for the insurance requirement.] It is written for individual allied health practitioners: practitioners registered under any National Board other than the Medical Board of Australia, and practitioners in self-regulated disciplines (such as counsellors, social workers, dietitians and exercise physiologists) who hold the accreditation or membership their recognised professional association requires. It does not cover medical practitioners for their own clinical practice, because their cover is separately mandated under the Commonwealth medical indemnity framework. Privately practising midwives participating in the Commonwealth contribution scheme for midwife professional indemnity are not insured under this product. This product is written to be an eligible insurance contract for the purposes of the Allied Health High Cost Claims Indemnity Scheme established under the Medical Indemnity Act 2002 (Cth).

This wording is written for businesses and professionals. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement. We have written this policy in plain English. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section. Where this policy refers to legislation, the reference includes that legislation as amended, replaced or re-enacted from time to time, together with its regulations and any corresponding law of a State or Territory. If anything in this document is unclear, contact us before you rely on it.

Definitions

These words have the meanings below wherever they appear in this policy, the schedule or an endorsement. The singular includes the plural and the plural includes the singular.

Aged Care Act

The Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth) and any regulations, rules or legislative instruments made under it, together with the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission Act 2018 (Cth), each as amended or replaced from time to time.

Allied Health Practitioner

A natural person who, at the time of the relevant act, error or omission:

  • (a) is registered under the National Law with any National Board other than the Medical Board of Australia, including, without limitation, practitioners registered in the professions of physiotherapy, chiropractic, osteopathy, podiatry, psychology, nursing (registered nurses, enrolled nurses and nurse practitioners), midwifery, paramedicine, dentistry (dentists, dental hygienists, dental therapists, oral health therapists and dental prosthetists), optometry, pharmacy, occupational therapy, medical radiation practice, Chinese medicine, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health practice; or
  • (b) practises a health discipline for which registration under the National Law is not required or not available, including, without limitation, counselling, social work, dietetics, exercise physiology, speech pathology, audiology, sonography and orthoptics, and holds the current membership, accreditation or certification of the recognised professional association for that discipline that the association requires for practising members.
Bodily Injury

Physical injury to a person, and includes death, sickness, disease, disability, shock, fright, mental anguish and mental injury resulting from that physical injury or occurring independently of physical injury.

Circumstance

Any fact, matter, act, error, omission or event of which you are aware and which might give rise to a claim against you, whether or not any person has yet asserted or threatened a claim.

Claim

Any of the following made against you or, where the context requires, received by you:

  • (a) a written demand for compensation, damages, restitution or non-monetary relief;
  • (b) civil legal proceedings, including a cross-claim, counterclaim, third-party claim or arbitration, seeking compensation, damages, restitution or non-monetary relief;
  • (c) a written notification, complaint, referral or proceeding concerning your conduct or your professional services made to or commenced by the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (Ahpra), a National Board, the Health Ombudsman (Queensland), the Health Care Complaints Commission (New South Wales), or any other state or territory healthcare complaints entity;
  • (d) a complaint or proceeding concerning your conduct or your professional services made to or commenced by the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission or the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, including a proceeding brought by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission that includes a claim for compensation on behalf of, or for the benefit of, an individual;
  • (e) a coronial inquest or inquiry at which your conduct or your professional services may be examined, where you are required or invited to give evidence or produce documents; and
  • (f) any other complaint or proceeding under a statute regulating health services, health complaints, privacy, discrimination or consumer protection, in which compensation, damages, restitution, disciplinary action or non-monetary relief is sought against you or in respect of your conduct, including an application, claim or proceeding for statutory compensation, or for a compensation order, under the Aged Care Act.

A claim of the kind described in paragraphs (c) to (f) is treated as a claim only to the extent this policy expressly covers inquiries, defence costs or civil liability arising from it. It is first made when you first receive written notice of it, as the definition of Inquiry provides for an inquiry.

Control Group

For a practice entity named under Endorsement E03: its chief executive officer, chief financial officer, general counsel, head of risk or insurance, and directors, partners and principals, or a person performing an equivalent role.

Defence Costs

The reasonable legal costs, disbursements and associated expenses (including the reasonable fees of expert witnesses, investigators and adjusters) incurred with our prior written consent, which we will not unreasonably withhold or delay, in the investigation, defence, settlement or appeal of a claim, or in representation at an inquiry where this policy covers that inquiry.

Defence costs do not include your own time, wages, salary or lost income (except as the Court Attendance Costs extension provides), or our own internal costs. We will advance defence costs as they are incurred and before final resolution of the claim, subject to the How Much We Will Pay section and to repayment under the Repayment Where Cover Is Not Established condition if it is finally established that you were not entitled to cover.

Documents

Deeds, wills, agreements, maps, plans, records, photographs, X-rays, scans, clinical images, written or printed books, letters, certificates, clinical notes and records, and written or printed documents of any nature, and electronic data and records (including health records stored in practice-management or electronic health record systems). Documents do not include bearer bonds, coupons, bank notes, currency notes, money or other negotiable instruments.

Employee

A natural person employed by you under a contract of service or apprenticeship, or engaged by you as a trainee or under a recognised work-experience or vocational placement arrangement, while acting within the scope of their duties in connection with your practice. Employee does not include an independent contractor practising their own health discipline in their own right, other than a locum.

Endorsement

A written variation to this policy issued by us, identified as an endorsement, and recorded in or attached to the schedule.

Excess

The amount shown in the schedule that you must bear for each claim (or each series of claims treated as one claim under the Multiple Claims Aggregation condition) before we pay. Final Adjudication

A consent order, settlement, compromise or admission of fact that does not include a determination of the relevant conduct is not a final adjudication.

Good Samaritan Act

Your provision of assistance, care or first aid, without fee, reward or expectation of either, to a person in need of emergency medical assistance at the scene of a medical emergency, accident or disaster arising from a civil emergency, and not in the course of any military, naval or air force duty or deployment.

National Law

Section A: Professional Indemnity

Professional Indemnity Cover

We will pay your civil liability for any claim first made against you and notified to us during the policy period, arising from:

  • (a) malpractice, that is any breach of your professional duty of care in the provision of your professional services; or
  • (b) any of the following, committed unintentionally (except where stated) in connection with your professional services:
  • (i) defamation, being libel or slander;
  • (ii) breach of privacy or breach of a duty of confidentiality, including a breach of the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), the My Health Records Act 2012 (Cth), the Health Records and Information Privacy Act 2002 (NSW), the Health Records Act 2001 (Vic), the Health Records (Privacy and Access) Act 1997 (ACT), or corresponding State or Territory law;
  • (iii) breach of the Australian Consumer Law (Schedule 2 to the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth)) or similar consumer protection legislation of the Commonwealth or of a State or Territory, including breach of the consumer guarantees of due care and skill and fitness for purpose as they apply to your professional services;
  • (iv) infringement of intellectual property rights, including copyright, trade marks and moral rights, but not patents; and
  • (v) unlawful discrimination, harassment or vilification under Commonwealth, State or Territory anti-discrimination legislation, other than in your capacity as an employer.

We will also pay your defence costs for a claim covered by this clause. This clause and every extension are subject to the retroactive date, to the What Is Not Covered section, to the How Much We Will Pay section, and to your obligations in the Your Obligations section and the How to Make a Claim section.

How the Extensions Apply

Data Breach Notification Costs

We will pay the reasonable costs you incur, with our prior written consent, to comply with an NDB Notification obligation arising from an eligible data breach (or suspected eligible data breach) of personal information, including health information, held by your practice and first discovered by you during the policy period. Covered costs include the costs of: conducting the assessment required by s.26WH of the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) within the 30-day statutory assessment period; forensic IT investigation; preparing and delivering the statement to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner; notifying affected individuals; and providing affected individuals with credit or identity monitoring for up to 12 months where reasonably required.

You must:

  • (a) notify us as soon as practicable after discovering the breach or suspected breach, and in any event in time for the statutory 30-day assessment to be completed (nothing in this policy prevents or delays your compliance with the Privacy Act); and
  • (b) at the time of the breach, have maintained back-up copies of your electronic records made no less frequently than daily and stored separately from the primary system.

If you do not comply with these obligations, we may reduce the amount we pay by the amount that fairly represents the prejudice we suffer, in accordance with s.54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

The most we will pay under this extension is the sub-limit shown in the Sub-limits provision, unless the schedule states a different amount.

Data Breach Fines and Penalties

We do not pay any fine or penalty that the law does not allow to be insured, and we do not pay a criminal fine.

Loss of Documents

If, during the policy period, you first discover that documents belonging to you, or for which you are legally responsible, have been destroyed, damaged, lost or mislaid in the course of your practice and cannot be found after diligent search, we will pay the reasonable costs of replacing or restoring them (including re-creating electronic records from back-ups and source material), and your civil liability to third parties arising directly from the loss.

  • (b) $250 for each day, or part of a day, of attendance by an employee.

The most we will pay under this extension is the sub-limit shown in the Sub-limits provision. No excess applies. This extension does not apply to attendance by a person as a claimant or complainant.

Continuous Cover

If you first became aware of a circumstance during a period in which you were continuously insured by us under an allied health professional indemnity policy, and you did not notify us of it under the policy in force at that time, we will cover the resulting claim under this policy, even though the Prior Known Circumstances and Prior Notifications exclusion would otherwise apply, provided that:

  • (a) you have been continuously insured by us for this class of risk from the date you first became aware of the circumstance until the date the claim is first made and notified;
  • (b) the failure to notify was not fraudulent; and

Like Section A: Professional Indemnity, this section is written on a claims-made and notified basis: it covers only claims first made against you and notified to us during the policy period, arising from bodily injury or property damage occurring after the retroactive date.

Relationship Between Section A and Section B

Section B: Public Liability covers premises liability and bodily injury or property damage that does not arise from a failure in your professional services, for example a visitor who trips on loose carpet in your waiting room. Section A: Professional Indemnity covers liability arising from your professional services, for example an injury caused by treatment.

Where a single claim arises from both a failure in professional services and a premises or occupier's failure, Section A applies to the professional element and Section B applies to the occupier or premises element. Where it is genuinely doubtful which section applies to a claim or an element of a claim, the section providing the greater cover for that claim or element applies. In no case will the same loss or the same defence costs be paid under both sections, and in no case will more than the limit of liability be payable for one claim.

Claims by more than one participant arising from the same act, error, omission or event are one claim under the Multiple Claims Aggregation condition. Consistent with the Telehealth and Remote Care extension, professional services include telepsychology and remote counselling delivered in compliance with the Australian Psychological Society's ethical guidelines for psychological services via telehealth or, for a counsellor, the telehealth guidance of their professional association.

Scope limit. A provisionally registered psychologist is an insured person only for professional services provided under supervision arrangements that comply with the Psychology Board of Australia's provisional registration requirements. This is a limit on who is covered, not an obligation, and the Your Obligations section does not apply to it.

Additional obligations. These are added to the Your Obligations section and operate on the same basis:

  • (a) A counsellor must maintain the membership, practising certificate, supervision and continuing professional development requirements of the ACA or PACFA throughout the policy period.
  • (b) You must keep contemporaneous clinical records of all consultations, including telehealth consultations, in accordance with your Board's or professional association's record-keeping standards.

Additional exclusions. We will not cover any claim, inquiry, loss, defence costs or other amounts arising from:

  • (a) the prescription of a medicine by you (psychologists and counsellors hold no prescribing authority); this does not apply to advice about, or care coordination involving, a medicine lawfully prescribed by another authorised prescriber; or
  • (b) the provision of change or suppression (conversion) practices prohibited under the legislation of any State or Territory.

Schedule variables: none.

E15 Dental and Oral Health

This endorsement applies where the insured is a dental practitioner (a dentist, dental specialist, dental hygienist, dental therapist, oral health therapist or dental prosthetist) registered with the Dental Board of Australia, or a practice whose professional services described in the schedule are wholly or predominantly dental or oral health services.

Dental practitioners are covered. A dental practitioner registered with the Dental Board of Australia is an allied health practitioner and is not a medical practitioner for the purposes of this policy. The Medical Practitioners exclusion does not apply to, and does not reduce cover for, any dental practitioner.

Covered dental procedures. Professional services include, without limitation, the following when performed within the insured's registration division, specialty and scope of practice under the Dental Board of Australia's scope of practice registration standard:

  • (a) oral surgery, including simple and surgical tooth extractions;
  • (b) the placement and restoration of dental implants;
  • (c) orthodontics;
  • (d) endodontics;
  • (e) periodontics;
  • (f) prosthodontics, including the design, fitting and adjustment of dentures and other dental prostheses (which are also products for the purposes of the Products Liability extension); and
  • (g) oral pathology, including the examination, diagnosis and referral of oral mucosal disease.

Dental sedation and general anaesthesia. The Professional Indemnity Cover clause extends to your civil liability arising from the administration of conscious sedation (including inhalational, oral and intravenous sedation) in connection with your professional services. At the time of the treatment:

  • (a) the sedation must be administered in compliance with the Dental Board of Australia's registration standard and guidelines for conscious sedation (and, where applicable, an endorsement for conscious sedation on the insured's registration);
  • (b) the premises must comply with any applicable State or Territory hospital, day-procedure or health-facility licensing requirements for the level of sedation administered; and
  • (c) the person administering the sedation must hold current sedation-specific training and continuing professional development certification.

If (a) to (c) are not met, we may reduce the amount we pay by the amount that fairly represents the prejudice we suffer, consistent with s.54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Additional obligations. These are added to the Your Obligations section and operate on the same basis:

  • (a) Radiation safety. Where you take, or cause to be taken, dental radiographs (including intraoral radiography, orthopantomography and cone-beam computed tomography), you must hold and comply with the radiation licence, registration or accreditation required by the radiation safety legislation of the relevant State or Territory, and provide evidence of it to us on request.
  • (b) Infection control. You must comply with the Dental Board of Australia's guidelines on infection control and the current Australian Dental Association infection control guidelines applicable to your practice.

Additional exclusions. We will not cover any claim, inquiry, loss, defence costs or other amounts arising from:

  • (a) Cosmetic facial injectables: the administration of botulinum toxin, dermal fillers or other cosmetic facial injectables for purely aesthetic purposes (that is, otherwise than for the treatment of temporomandibular disorders, bruxism, hyperhidrosis, dental or facial pain, or another clinical indication within the insured's scope of practice), unless the insured has declared that activity to us and Endorsement E07 applies. Where a facial injectable is administered for a clinical indication, the Prescription-only Medicines Outside Authority exclusion continues to apply to the prescribing and supply of the substance.
  • (b) Specialist oral and maxillofacial surgery: any surgical procedure that requires specialist registration as an oral and maxillofacial surgeon, unless the insured holds that specialist registration and it is noted in the schedule. Surgical procedures within the general dental scope of practice, including surgical extractions, remain covered.
  • (c) General anaesthesia elsewhere: dental treatment performed under general anaesthesia, unless the general anaesthesia was administered in an accredited day surgery or hospital facility by a person lawfully authorised to administer it. This is an exclusion of a kind of risk, not an obligation. Treatment under general anaesthesia administered in such a facility by such a person is covered.

Schedule variables: specialist registration (where held).
