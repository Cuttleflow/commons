Personal Accident and Sickness Insurance

Capital and weekly benefits for one named person, for injury and, if chosen, sickness, sold through brokers in Australia.

About This Policy

This policy pays fixed benefits if you suffer an injury, or (if the schedule shows sickness cover) a sickness, during the policy period. It pays a lump sum for death and for the permanent disablements listed in the Capital Benefits clause, and a weekly benefit while you are temporarily disabled and cannot work.

It is a benefit policy, not an indemnity policy. The capital benefits are fixed amounts that do not depend on your financial loss. The weekly benefits replace part of your income and are measured against it, as the How Much We Pay Each Week provision sets out.

It is not workers compensation, life insurance or private health insurance, and it does not replace any of them. It cannot pay any medical expense for which a Medicare benefit is payable (see the What the Law Does Not Allow Us to Pay provision).

This policy, together with your schedule, your proposal and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms your contract of insurance with us. Please read them together and tell us if anything is incorrect. Where the schedule and this wording differ, the schedule applies. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

Your duty when you apply. What you must tell us depends on the kind of contract this is.

  • If you take out this policy wholly or mainly for personal, domestic or household purposes, it is a consumer insurance contract. You have a duty under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to us before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated. Answer every question in the proposal honestly and completely. If you do not take reasonable care, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.
  • Otherwise, you have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of that Act. Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.

[WP REVIEW: confirm presenting both the s20B and s21 regimes in one wording, with classification by purpose, is acceptable where the purchaser's purpose is mixed.]

Terms that differ from standard cover. The Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) sets standard cover for sickness and accident contracts. This policy differs from standard cover. In particular:

  • it does not cover a pre-existing condition (see the Pre-existing Conditions exclusion);
  • it does not pay weekly sickness benefits for a sickness that first shows itself in the first 30 days of cover, unless the schedule shows otherwise (see the First 30 Days of Sickness Cover exclusion);
  • it does not cover normal pregnancy or childbirth (see the Pregnancy exclusion);
  • it does not cover the hazardous pursuits listed in the Hazardous Pursuits exclusion, or service in the armed forces (see the Military Service exclusion);
  • weekly benefits start only after the waiting period, stop at the end of the benefit period, are limited to a percentage of your income, and stop at age 65 unless the schedule shows otherwise;
  • weekly sickness benefits for a mental illness are limited to 26 weeks for any one sickness (see the Mental Illness provision);
  • weekly benefits are reduced by other payments you receive for the same disablement (see the Other Payments for the Same Disablement provision);
  • capital benefits are reduced from age 70, and no benefit is payable for an injury after age 75 (see the Age Limits provision).

Cooling-off period. You may cancel this policy within 21 days of the date it starts, and within 21 days of each renewal, by telling us in writing. If you have not made a claim, we will refund the full premium paid for that period, less any non-refundable government charges we cannot recover. After that, you may still cancel under the Cancellation condition.

Financial Claims Scheme. If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, you may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.

Schedule

(Completed separately for each policy. It contains your specific details.)

The schedule shows:

  • The insured person's name and date of birth, and their occupation and occupation class
  • The policy period, with its start and end dates
  • The scope of cover: 24 hours a day, occupational only, or non-occupational only
  • The capital sum
  • Whether weekly benefits apply, and if so the weekly benefit, the percentage of income it is limited to (default 85%), the waiting period and the benefit period
  • Whether weekly sickness benefits apply
  • The limits of the additional benefits, where they differ from the defaults in the Additional Benefits section
  • The accumulation limit, if different from the default in the Accumulation Limit provision
  • Any pre-existing condition we have accepted, and any condition excluded by endorsement
  • Any hazardous pursuit we have agreed to cover
  • The premium, including stamp duty and GST
  • The insurer and its licence, and the state or territory whose law governs this policy (see the Governing Law condition)
  • The endorsements that apply, by code, with their variables

Definitions

In this policy, the words below have these meanings wherever they appear, whatever their capitalisation. The singular includes the plural.

Accident

A sudden, unexpected and identifiable event that happens to you during the policy period and within the scope of cover.

Benefit Period

The longest period for which we pay weekly benefits for any one injury or sickness, shown in the schedule as 52, 104 or 260 weeks. The benefit period starts on the first day after the waiting period ends.

Capital Sum

The amount shown in the schedule as the capital sum.

Endorsement

A written change to this policy that we issue and that is shown in the schedule. The available endorsements are listed in the Endorsement Library.

Hazardous Pursuit

Any activity listed in the Hazardous Pursuits exclusion.

Income

Your average weekly income from personal exertion, before tax, over the 12 months before the disablement started, or over the time you have been working if that is shorter.

  • If you are an employee, it is your salary or wages, including regular overtime, commissions, shift allowances and bonuses, and the employer superannuation contributions made for you.
  • If you are self-employed or a working director of your own company, it is the income your personal exertion generated for the business, after deducting the expenses of earning it.

Income does not include investment income, or income that continues to be paid to you while you are disabled.

Injury

A bodily injury caused solely and directly by an accident, and not by a sickness or a gradual process. Injury includes a resulting medical condition that first shows itself within 12 months of the accident.

Insured Person

The person named in the schedule as the insured person.

Medical Practitioner

A person registered to practise medicine in Australia, or a person with an equivalent qualification and registration in the country where you are treated, who is not you, a member of your family, your business partner or your employee.

Mental Illness

A mental or psychiatric disorder, including anxiety, depression, stress, burnout and post-traumatic stress disorder, diagnosed by a psychiatrist or a clinical psychologist.

Occupation Class

The class from 1 to 5 shown in the schedule for your occupation, which we used to set the premium.

Permanent

Lasting at least 12 months from the date of the injury and, in the opinion of a medical practitioner we accept, beyond hope of improvement at the end of that time.

Permanent Total Disablement

Disablement from an injury that has lasted 12 months and that, in the opinion of a medical practitioner we accept, will permanently prevent you from working in any occupation for which you are reasonably suited by education, training or experience.

Policy Period

The period shown in the schedule.

Pre-existing Condition

A condition, illness or injury:

  • for which you received treatment, medication or advice from a medical practitioner in the 12 months before the date you were first insured under this policy; or
  • whose symptoms you were aware of, or a reasonable person in your circumstances would have been aware of, in the 3 months before that date.

A condition that you told us about before we insured you, and that we accepted in writing and show in the schedule, is not a pre-existing condition.

Scope of Cover

The times when this policy covers you, shown in the schedule:

  • 24 hours a day: at all times, anywhere in the world, subject to the Living and Travelling Overseas obligation;
  • occupational: while you are working in the occupation shown in the schedule, and while travelling directly between your home and your place of work;
  • non-occupational: at all times except while you are working, and except while travelling directly between your home and your place of work.

If the schedule shows no scope of cover, 24 hours a day applies.

Sickness

An illness or disease that first shows itself during the policy period. Sickness does not include an injury.

Temporary Partial Disablement

Disablement from an injury or sickness that follows a period of temporary total disablement and that prevents you from doing one or more of the important duties of your usual occupation, or from working your usual hours, while you can do some of them.

Temporary Total Disablement

Disablement from an injury or sickness that wholly prevents you from doing every important duty of your usual occupation, for which you are under the regular care of a medical practitioner and following their advice.

Waiting Period

The number of days, shown in the schedule, at the start of a period of temporary total disablement for which we do not pay a weekly benefit.

We, Us, Our

The insurer named in the schedule.

Weekly Benefit

The amount shown in the schedule as the weekly benefit.

You, Your

The insured person. The insured person is also the policyholder.

Weekly Benefits for Injury

If the schedule shows weekly benefits, and you suffer an injury during the policy period and within the scope of cover that results in temporary total disablement starting within 12 months of the accident, we will pay you the weekly benefit after the waiting period, for as long as the temporary total disablement continues, up to the benefit period.

If temporary total disablement is followed by temporary partial disablement, we will pay you the weekly benefit less what you earn in each week of the temporary partial disablement, for the rest of the benefit period. If a medical practitioner certifies you can return to work in a reduced capacity, and you choose not to, we will pay 25% of the weekly benefit for up to 4 weeks.

How much we pay each week is set by the How Much We Pay Each Week provision.

Weekly Benefits for Sickness

This clause applies only if the schedule shows weekly sickness benefits.

If you suffer a sickness during the policy period that results in temporary total disablement, we will pay you the weekly benefit after the waiting period, for as long as the temporary total disablement continues, up to the benefit period.

Temporary partial disablement from a sickness is paid on the same terms as in the Weekly Benefits for Injury clause.

The scope of cover does not limit this clause: a sickness is covered whenever it first shows itself during the policy period.

Additional Benefits

These benefits are paid in addition to the capital sum and the weekly benefit, unless a clause says otherwise. Their limits apply to the insured person, and are shown in each clause unless the schedule shows a different amount.

Exposure and Disappearance

If you suffer an event in the Capital Benefits table because you were exposed to the elements after an accident, we will treat it as caused by injury.

If you disappear after the conveyance you were travelling in is wrecked, sinks or disappears, and you are not found within 12 months, we will pay the Event 1 benefit. Before we pay, your legal personal representative must give us a signed undertaking to repay it if you are later found alive.

Funeral Expenses

If we pay the Event 1 benefit, we will also pay the reasonable funeral, cremation or burial expenses incurred, and the cost of returning your remains to your home in Australia. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the limit is $15,000 for any one injury.

Dependent Children

If we pay the Event 1 benefit, we will also pay a benefit for each child under 18 years of age, or under 25 years of age and studying full time, who was wholly or mainly dependent on you. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the benefit is $10,000 for each child, and no more than $50,000 in total.

Spouse or Partner Retraining

If we pay the Event 1 or Event 2 benefit, we will also reimburse the cost of a course of training your spouse or partner takes within 2 years to gain or improve qualifications for paid work. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the limit is $15,000.

Coma

If an injury causes you to be in a coma for more than 7 consecutive days, we will pay a daily benefit from the first day of the coma. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the benefit is $100 a day for up to 90 days. This benefit is paid in addition to any other benefit for the same injury.

Broken Bones

If an injury causes you to fracture a bone, and a medical practitioner confirms the fracture by X-ray or other imaging, we will pay the percentage below of the broken bones limit. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the broken bones limit is $7,500.

Non-Medicare Medical Expenses

If an injury requires treatment within 12 months of the accident, we will pay 85% of the reasonable cost of treatment by a medical practitioner, physiotherapist, chiropractor, osteopath, dentist or ambulance service, after any amount you recover or could recover from a private health insurer or any other source. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the limit is $10,000 for any one injury.

This benefit is subject to the What the Law Does Not Allow Us to Pay provision. It does not pay for any treatment for which a Medicare benefit is payable, any gap between a Medicare benefit and the fee charged, or any hospital treatment or general treatment within the meaning of the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (Cth).

Rehabilitation

If you are receiving weekly benefits, we will pay the cost of a rehabilitation or return-to-work program that we agree beforehand is likely to help you return to work sooner. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the limit is $10,000 for any one injury or sickness. Taking part does not reduce your weekly benefit.

Home and Vehicle Modification

If we pay the Event 2, Event 3 or Event 6 benefit, we will also reimburse the reasonable cost of modifying your home or vehicle that a medical practitioner certifies is necessary because of the injury. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the limit is $30,000.

Transport to Work

If, because of an injury, a medical practitioner certifies that you cannot drive yourself or use public transport, but you are able to work, we will reimburse the reasonable cost of transport between your home and your place of work. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the limit is $2,500 for any one injury.

Escalation of Weekly Benefits

If we have paid weekly benefits for the same period of disablement continuously for 12 months, we will increase the weekly benefit by 5% on each anniversary of the first day it was paid, for the rest of the benefit period.

How We Pay Benefits

How Much We Pay Each Week

Each week we pay the lower of:

  • the weekly benefit; and
  • the percentage of your income shown in the schedule. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, it is 85%.

If Endorsement E01 applies, the second limit does not apply, but the Other Payments for the Same Disablement provision still does.

We pay weekly benefits fortnightly in arrears, for each day of disablement at one-seventh of the weekly amount. We pay no weekly benefit for the waiting period.

Recurring Disablement

If you return to work and, within 6 months, you are again disabled from the same or a related injury or sickness, we treat it as a continuation of the earlier disablement. No new waiting period applies, and the benefit period already used counts towards the benefit period.

If you have worked full time in your usual occupation for 6 consecutive months, a further disablement is a new claim.

One Capital Benefit for Each Injury

For any one injury, we pay only one of Events 1 to 18 in the Capital Benefits table, the one with the highest percentage. Where an injury causes more than one of Events 8 to 18, we add their percentages together, up to 100% of the capital sum.

Once we have paid 100% of the capital sum for one or more injuries, your cover under the Capital Benefits clause ends. The rest of this policy continues, and we will refund the part of the premium that relates to capital benefits for the unexpired policy period.

Weekly Benefits Before a Capital Benefit

If we pay a capital benefit for an injury, we deduct from it the weekly benefits we have already paid for the same injury, except where the capital benefit is for Event 1 or for Events 8 to 18. We then stop paying weekly benefits for that injury.

If you die from a cause unrelated to the injury before a capital benefit for Events 2 to 18 is assessed, we will pay the amount we reasonably consider would have been payable.

Other Payments for the Same Disablement

We reduce the weekly benefit by any weekly payment you receive, or are entitled to receive, for the same period of disablement from:

  • a workers compensation or transport accident scheme, or any other statutory compensation scheme;
  • sick leave or other pay from your employer or your business that continues while you are disabled; and
  • any other personal accident, sickness, income protection or disability income insurance,

so that the total of all those payments and our weekly benefit is no more than your income, or no more than the weekly benefit if Endorsement E01 applies.

We do not reduce capital benefits, or the benefits in the Additional Benefits section, for any other payment.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the reduction for other insurance operates as a benefit measure, not as an "other insurance" provision caught by s45 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).]

Mental Illness

For temporary total disablement or temporary partial disablement from a mental illness, we pay weekly sickness benefits for no more than 26 weeks for any one sickness, even if the benefit period is longer. This limit does not apply to a mental illness that results from an injury.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the 26-week limit is supportable under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth) s46 on actuarial or statistical data, and is not an unfair term for a consumer or small business contract.]

Age Limits

We do not pay weekly benefits for any period after your 65th birthday, unless Endorsement E09 applies.

If you are 70 or older on the date of the accident, the capital sum for that injury is 50% of the amount shown in the schedule, and the most we pay for Event 2 is $250,000 unless the schedule shows otherwise.

We do not pay any benefit for an injury or sickness that happens after your 75th birthday.

Accumulation Limit

The most we will pay in capital benefits for any one injury, under this policy and every other personal accident policy we have issued that covers you, is the accumulation limit. Unless the schedule shows otherwise, the accumulation limit is $2,000,000. If the total would be more, we reduce the benefit under each policy in proportion.

What the Law Does Not Allow Us to Pay

The Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth), the National Health Act 1953 (Cth) and the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (Cth) do not allow us to pay:

  • any medical expense for which a Medicare benefit is payable, or any gap between the Medicare benefit and the fee charged; or
  • any amount that would make this policy health insurance business under those Acts.

We will not pay any benefit, or part of a benefit, to the extent that paying it would breach those Acts.

Who We Pay

We pay every benefit to you, except that the Event 1 benefit and the benefits that follow it are paid to your legal personal representative. If you are unable to manage your affairs, we may pay a person legally entitled to act for you. Payment to that person discharges our liability for the amount paid.

What Is Not Covered

The exclusions below apply to every benefit under this policy, unless an exclusion says otherwise.

Intentional Self-Injury

We do not pay any benefit for your intentional self-injury, suicide or attempted suicide.

War

We do not pay any benefit for an injury or sickness caused by war, invasion, act of a foreign enemy, hostilities (whether war is declared or not), civil war, rebellion, revolution or insurrection.

This exclusion does not apply to an act of terrorism, unless you took part in it.

Nuclear

We do not pay any benefit for an injury or sickness caused by ionising radiation, or contamination by radioactivity, from any nuclear fuel, nuclear waste or nuclear weapon.

Alcohol and Drugs

We do not pay any benefit for an injury that happens while:

  • you are driving or in charge of a motor vehicle with a blood alcohol concentration above the legal limit where you are driving;
  • at any other time, your blood alcohol concentration is above 0.10 grams per 100 millilitres; or
  • you are under the influence of a drug that a medical practitioner did not prescribe for you, or that you did not take as prescribed,

unless you prove the alcohol or drug did not cause or contribute to the injury.

We do not pay any benefit for a sickness that results from your addiction to alcohol or a drug.

Criminal and Malicious Acts

We do not pay any benefit for an injury that happens while you are committing, or attempting to commit, a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment, or acting maliciously.

Professional Sport

We do not pay any benefit for an injury that happens while you are taking part in, training for, coaching or officiating in any sport for which you receive, or are entitled to receive, a fee, sponsorship or prize money that is more than your reasonable expenses.

Flying

We do not pay any benefit for an injury that happens while you are in or on an aircraft, other than as a passenger in an aircraft that is licensed to carry passengers and is operated by a person licensed to fly it.

This exclusion does not apply to flying shown in the schedule under Endorsement E05.

Hazardous Pursuits

We do not pay any benefit for an injury that happens while you are taking part in:

  • parachuting, skydiving, hang-gliding, paragliding, BASE jumping or ballooning;
  • a race, rally or speed trial in or on any motor vehicle or motorcycle, other than a road vehicle event that tests regularity and is not timed for speed;
  • scuba diving below 30 metres, or scuba diving without a qualification from a recognised training body unless under the supervision of a qualified instructor;
  • mountaineering above 4,000 metres; or
  • rodeo, bull riding, or horse racing (including harness racing and steeplechase).

This exclusion does not apply to a pursuit shown in the schedule under Endorsement E05.

Military Service

We do not pay any benefit for an injury that happens while you are serving full time, or on operational deployment as a reservist, in the armed forces of any country or of an international organisation.

Pre-existing Conditions

We do not pay weekly sickness benefits for disablement caused by a pre-existing condition. We do not pay any benefit for an injury to the extent that a pre-existing condition caused or contributed to the disablement or event.

This exclusion stops applying to a pre-existing condition once you have been insured with us continuously under this policy, and the policies it renews, for 24 months.

This exclusion is subject to s.47 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): we will not rely on it for a condition that you were not aware of, and that a reasonable person in your circumstances could not have been expected to be aware of, when you were first insured.

First 30 Days of Sickness Cover

We do not pay weekly sickness benefits for a sickness that first shows itself in the first 30 days after weekly sickness benefits first start under this policy. This exclusion does not apply on renewal, or to a sickness that results from an injury.

Pregnancy

We do not pay weekly sickness benefits for disablement caused by pregnancy, childbirth or miscarriage, except a complication that a medical practitioner certifies is not a normal part of pregnancy or childbirth. We pay weekly sickness benefits for such a complication as a sickness, from the first day of the 26th week of pregnancy at the earliest.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the 26th-week start is supportable under the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth) s41, or remove it.]

Elective Treatment

We do not pay any benefit for disablement caused by cosmetic or elective surgery or treatment, unless it is needed because of an injury or sickness covered by this policy.

Your Obligations

Each obligation is subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition.

Tell Us About Claims

Tell us as soon as reasonably practicable after an accident, injury or sickness that may lead to a claim. For weekly benefits, give us a medical certificate covering the start of the disablement, and further certificates when we reasonably ask for them.

Give us the information and documents we reasonably need to assess the claim, including evidence of your income and of other payments under the Other Payments for the Same Disablement provision, at your own cost.

Medical Care and Examination

Get treatment from a medical practitioner as soon as reasonably practicable after an injury or the start of a sickness, and follow their reasonable advice.

You must attend an examination by a medical practitioner or other health professional we choose, as often as we reasonably require while a claim is open. We pay for it. If you die, we may ask for a post-mortem examination at our expense, where the law allows it.

Tell Us About Changes

Tell us during the policy period, as soon as reasonably practicable, if:

  • you change your occupation, or your duties change so that a higher occupation class would apply; or
  • you start taking part regularly in a hazardous pursuit, or in any activity listed in the proposal that you did not tell us about.

We will tell you whether the change affects your cover or premium.

Living and Travelling Overseas

You are covered while you are outside Australia, for up to 90 consecutive days on any one trip, unless Endorsement E10 allows a longer period.

If you leave Australia while you are receiving weekly benefits, tell us before you go. We will continue to pay weekly benefits for up to 90 days while you are outside Australia, and for longer if we agree in writing. We may ask you to return to Australia for a medical examination at our expense.

General Conditions

Insurance Contracts Act

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts or modifies any right you have under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Where you fail to do something this policy requires, or do something it does not allow, after the policy is entered into, s.54 of that Act applies. We will not refuse a claim only because of that act or omission. We may reduce our payment by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced. We may refuse the claim only where the act or omission could reasonably be regarded as capable of causing or contributing to the loss, and then only as that section allows.

Where you have not told us about a change under the Tell Us About Changes obligation, and the change meant a higher premium would have applied, we may reduce a claim to the proportion that the premium paid bears to the premium we would have charged.

[WP REVIEW: confirm a premium-proportion reduction for an untold change of occupation or pursuit is a fair measure of prejudice under s54(1).]

Cancellation

You may cancel this policy at any time by telling us in writing. We will refund the premium for the unexpired policy period in proportion, unless you cancel within the cooling-off period in the Important Information section.

We may cancel this policy only on a ground allowed by the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), by giving you written notice as s.59 of that Act requires. We will refund the premium for the unexpired policy period in proportion.

Cancellation does not affect a claim for an injury that happened, or a sickness that first showed itself, before cancellation.

Renewal

Each renewal is a new contract. Before this policy ends, we will tell you whether we offer to renew it and on what terms, as s.58 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires. We are not obliged to offer renewal.

Fraudulent Claims

If a claim is made fraudulently, we may refuse to pay it, as s.56 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows.

Sanctions

We will not provide cover, pay any claim or provide any benefit under this policy to the extent that doing so would expose us, or any of our related bodies corporate, to any sanction, prohibition or restriction under the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth), the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or the trade or economic sanctions laws or regulations of the United Nations, the European Union, the United Kingdom or the United States of America.

GST

The premium includes GST where it applies, under A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth). The benefits under this policy are fixed amounts and are not adjusted for GST or input tax credits.

Privacy

We collect, use and disclose your personal information, including health information, to assess your proposal, administer this policy and manage claims, in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and our privacy policy. Our privacy policy explains how you can access and correct your information and how to complain about a privacy matter.

Complaints and Disputes

If you are unhappy with our service or a decision, contact us at the details in the schedule. We will acknowledge your complaint within one business day and give you our decision within 30 calendar days.

If you are not satisfied with our response, or we have not responded within 30 calendar days, you may take your complaint to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA), a free and independent dispute resolution service: www.afca.org.au, 1800 931 678, info@afca.org.au.

Governing Law

This policy is governed by the law of the Australian state or territory shown in the schedule. If the schedule does not show one, it is governed by the law of the state or territory where you lived on the date this policy started.

Jurisdiction

You and we submit to the jurisdiction of the courts of the state or territory whose law governs this policy, and of any court that may hear appeals from them.

Headings and References

Headings are for convenience only. A reference to legislation includes any legislation that amends or replaces it. A reference to a clause by its heading is a reference to that clause as it appears in this wording.

How to Make a Claim

  1. Contact your broker, or contact us directly at the details shown in the schedule.
  2. Tell us what happened and when, and the name of the medical practitioner treating you (see the Tell Us About Claims obligation).
  3. For weekly benefits, send us a medical certificate and evidence of your income for the last 12 months.
  4. Tell us about any workers compensation, transport accident, sick leave or other insurance payments you receive or have claimed.
  5. We will acknowledge your claim, send you a claim form within 7 days and keep you informed.
