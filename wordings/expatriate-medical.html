Expatriate Medical Insurance

International medical plan for employees of Australian organisations on assignment overseas, and their dependants, built on the Meridian Corporate Travel base wording.

About This Policy

This policy pays the medical, hospital, evacuation and repatriation costs of employees of an Australian organisation who are on assignment outside Australia, and of their dependants who live with them in the host country. It is an annual international medical plan: it covers planned and unplanned treatment in the area of cover, not only emergencies.

You, the organisation named in the schedule, are the policyholder. The people covered are the insured persons described in the Who Is Covered clause. Benefits are paid to the provider, to you or to the insured person, as the Who We Pay provision states.

This policy is not workers compensation, private health insurance or life insurance, and it does not replace any of them. It pays only because the insured persons are Australian residents temporarily employed outside Australia, and their dependants, and it does not pay for treatment in Australia except as Endorsement E12 allows (see the What the Law Does Not Allow Us to Pay provision).

This policy, together with your schedule, your proposal and declarations, and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms your contract of insurance with us. Where the schedule and this wording differ, the schedule applies. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

This wording is the Meridian Corporate Travel base wording restated as an international medical plan for assignees. It is a complete wording in its own right.

Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

Who this policy is for. This wording is written for Australian organisations that send employees on assignment overseas. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement.

Not health insurance. This policy is general insurance. A general insurer may pay hospital and medical costs outside Australia for an Australian resident temporarily employed outside Australia, and their dependants, without carrying on health insurance business. It is not private health insurance, and it does not count for the Medicare levy surcharge or lifetime health cover. An assignee who keeps Australian private health insurance while overseas should ask their health fund about suspending it.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the policy is outside health insurance business under rule 15 of the Private Health Insurance (Health Insurance Business) Rules 2018 (Cth) for assignees and their dependants, including dependants who are not themselves employed, and including treatment in a third country during an assignment.]

Your duty when you apply. This policy is taken out by an organisation for its business, so it is not a consumer insurance contract. You have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. This includes the assignees and dependants you want covered, their ages, the host countries, and any known medical condition you are aware of. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.

Who may claim. An insured person may claim under this policy as a third-party beneficiary, as s.48 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows.

Financial Claims Scheme. If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, an insured person may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.

Definitions

In this policy, the words below have these meanings, wherever they are used and whether or not they start with a capital letter.

Accident

A sudden, unexpected and external event, not intended by the insured person, that happens at an identifiable time and place during a trip.

Area of Cover

The area shown in the schedule in which this policy pays for treatment: Asia (excluding Hong Kong, Singapore and mainland China unless the schedule shows otherwise), worldwide excluding the USA, or worldwide.

Assignment

The period during which an assignee works outside Australia for you, as shown in the schedule or declared to us, together with the time their dependants live with them in the host country.

Dependant

The spouse or domestic partner of an assignee, and their unmarried children under 21 (or under 25 if in full-time study), who live with the assignee in the host country.

DFAT Advice

The travel advice level published on Smartraveller by the Australian Government Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade for a country or region. "Do not travel" is the highest level.

Endorsement

A written change to this policy that we issue and that is shown in the schedule. The available endorsements are listed in the Endorsement Library.

Excess

The amount shown in the schedule for a section that is deducted from each claim under that section. Only one excess applies to all claims by one insured person arising from one event under one section.

Home

The usual place of residence of the insured person in the host country during the assignment.

Host Country

The country outside Australia where an assignee is based for the assignment, as shown in the schedule or declared to us.

Immediate Family

The spouse or domestic partner, parent, parent-in-law, step-parent, child, step-child, grandparent, grandchild, brother or sister of an insured person.

Injury

A bodily injury caused solely and directly by an accident, and not by a sickness or a gradual process. Injury includes a resulting medical condition that first shows itself within 12 months of the accident.

Insured Person

An assignee or dependant described in the Who Is Covered clause.

Medical Practitioner

A person registered and practising as a medical practitioner or dentist in the country where treatment is given, who is not the insured person, a member of their immediate family, or employed by you.

Medicare-Eligible

An insured person who is eligible for a Medicare benefit under the Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth).

Policy Period

The period shown in the schedule, or any shorter period if the policy is cancelled.

Pre-existing Condition

A condition, illness or injury of an insured person: for which they received treatment, medication or advice from a medical practitioner in the 12 months before a trip starts; or whose symptoms they were aware of, or a reasonable person in their circumstances would have been aware of, in the 3 months before the trip starts. A condition that we have accepted in writing, or that is accepted under Endorsement E01, is not a pre-existing condition.

Sickness

An illness or disease that first shows itself during a trip.

Trip

The assignment, and any journey an insured person makes from the host country within the area of cover during it.

We, Us, Our

The insurer named in the schedule.

You, Your

The organisation named in the schedule as the policyholder, and its subsidiaries in Australia.

Who and What Is Covered

Who Is Covered

The insured persons are, while they are on assignment:

  • assignees: your directors, officers and employees who live in Australia and whom you have sent to work outside Australia for a period expected to last more than 3 months and no more than 5 years; and
  • their dependants who live with them in the host country.

Each insured person must be named in the schedule or declared under the Declarations obligation, and must be no older than 74 when the policy starts or is renewed. An assignee who stops being employed by you, or whose assignment ends, stops being an insured person 30 days after that, or when they return to Australia, whichever is earlier. Their dependants stop being insured persons at the same time.

When You Are Covered

Cover for each insured person starts when they leave Australia to start the assignment, or when the policy starts, whichever is later, and ends when the assignment ends and they return to Australia, or when the policy ends, whichever is earlier.

Cover applies in the host country and anywhere else in the area of cover shown in the schedule, including trips from the host country, subject to the Government Travel Advice exclusion and the Sanctions condition. Treatment in the USA is covered only if the area of cover is worldwide or Endorsement E13 applies. Treatment in Australia is not covered, except as Endorsement E12 allows.

How We Pay

Limits and Excess

The most we will pay for each insured person in each policy period is the overall annual limit for the plan, and each benefit limit is part of, and not in addition to, the overall annual limit. A limit shown as a year applies for each policy period.

The excess shown in the schedule applies once for each insured person in each policy period, to outpatient benefits only, unless the schedule shows that it applies to inpatient benefits too. No excess applies to evacuation, repatriation or the return of remains.

What the Law Does Not Allow Us to Pay

The Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth) and the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (Cth) do not allow us to pay any medical expense in Australia for which a Medicare benefit is payable, or any gap between the Medicare benefit and the fee charged, or any amount that would make this policy health insurance business.

We pay for treatment outside Australia only for an insured person who is on assignment, as an Australian resident temporarily employed outside Australia or as their dependant. We do not pay for treatment in Australia, except as Endorsement E12 allows, and then only for costs for which no Medicare benefit is payable. We will not pay any benefit, or part of a benefit, to the extent that paying it would breach those Acts.

Other Insurance

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy, we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows. This applies to every section of this policy, including the medical and evacuation, personal accident and kidnap, extortion and crisis response sections. This does not apply where the schedule or an endorsement states that this policy is excess of a specified underlying policy, in which case this policy pays only after the limit of that specified policy is exhausted.

Personal accident and sickness policies. This policy pays first for an injury or sickness that happens on a trip covered by this policy. We do not reduce any benefit because of a personal accident or sickness policy, including a group personal accident and sickness policy issued to you. If the schedule of such a policy names this policy as a specified underlying policy, that policy pays its weekly benefits and additional benefits only after this policy has paid, and both policies pay their capital benefits in full.

You or the insured person must tell us about any other insurance that may cover the loss when you make a claim.

Recovery

After we pay a claim for an indemnity loss, we may recover the amount from anyone responsible for it, in your name or the insured person's name. You and the insured person must give us reasonable help to do this. We will not recover from an insured person, or from you, except where they acted fraudulently.

Who We Pay

We pay the personal accident capital benefits to the insured person, or if they have died, to their estate. We pay other benefits to whoever incurred the cost, whether you or the insured person, or directly to the provider of the service. Payment to any of them discharges our liability for that amount. All amounts are paid in Australian dollars, at the exchange rate on the date the cost was incurred.

What Is Not Covered

These exclusions apply to every section, in addition to the exclusions in each section.

Pre-existing Conditions

We do not pay any claim under the medical and evacuation, personal accident or travel disruption sections arising from a pre-existing condition of the insured person, unless we have accepted it in writing or Endorsement E01 applies to it.

This exclusion is subject to s.47 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): we will not rely on it for a condition the insured person was not aware of, and that a reasonable person in their circumstances could not have been expected to be aware of, when the trip started.

Government Travel Advice

We do not pay for treatment in a country or region for which DFAT advice was "do not travel" when the insured person went there, unless we agreed in writing before they went to cover it.

This exclusion does not apply to an insured person who was already there, including in the host country, when the advice was raised, for 30 days after it was raised or until they can reasonably leave, whichever is earlier, or to their evacuation from it.

War

We do not pay for treatment of an injury caused by war, invasion, act of a foreign enemy, hostilities (whether war is declared or not), civil war, rebellion, revolution or insurrection, if the insured person took an active part in it. This exclusion does not apply to an insured person injured in an act of terrorism, or in war or civil unrest they took no part in, or to evacuation from it.

Nuclear, Biological and Chemical Events

We do not pay for treatment of an injury or sickness caused by ionising radiation or contamination by radioactivity from any nuclear fuel, nuclear waste or nuclear weapon, or by the use of a nuclear, biological or chemical weapon. This exclusion does not apply to treatment for exposure in medical diagnosis or treatment.

Self-Injury and Intoxication

We do not pay any claim caused by the insured person's intentional self-injury, or by their being under the influence of alcohol or a drug, unless the drug was taken as prescribed by a medical practitioner.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the self-injury exclusion as drafted is consistent with the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth) for injury resulting from a mental illness.]

Illegal Acts

We do not pay any claim caused by the insured person's illegal act, or by their being arrested or detained because of their own illegal act.

Hazardous Activities

We do not pay any claim caused by the insured person taking part in professional sport, motor racing, mountaineering or rock climbing using ropes or guides, skydiving, hang-gliding, paragliding, base jumping, bungee jumping, scuba diving deeper than 30 metres or without a qualified instructor or certification, off-piste snow sports, or flying other than as a fare-paying passenger on a scheduled or licensed charter aircraft, unless Endorsement E03 applies.

Pregnancy and Childbirth

We do not pay for pregnancy or childbirth, or for a pregnancy-related condition, except:

  • under the Premier plan, as the maternity benefit in the International Medical Plan clause allows; and
  • under either plan, for a complication of pregnancy that a medical practitioner certifies as unforeseen, such as ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage or pre-eclampsia.

We do not pay for fertility treatment, assisted reproduction or surrogacy.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the 10-month qualifying period for maternity under the Premier plan and the absence of maternity under the Standard plan are consistent with the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth) and its exemption for insurance based on actuarial or statistical data.]

Treatment We Do Not Pay For

We do not pay for cosmetic treatment, unless it is to restore appearance after an injury or cancer surgery during the policy period; weight-loss treatment or surgery; experimental treatment; treatment for alcohol or drug dependence beyond an initial stabilisation; routine health checks, vaccinations or travel medicine, unless the plan or Endorsement E14 includes them; or treatment an insured person travelled to the host country, or within the area of cover, to obtain.

Your Obligations

Emergencies

In a medical emergency, kidnap, extortion or security event, the insured person or someone with them must contact the 24-hour emergency assistance service on the number shown in the schedule as soon as practicable. In a life-threatening emergency, they should first call local emergency services. These obligations are subject to the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition.

Making a Claim

You or the insured person must tell us as soon as reasonably practicable about any event that may lead to a claim, and give us the information and documents we reasonably need to assess it, including original receipts, medical reports, carrier reports and, for theft or loss of property or money, a police report obtained within 24 hours of discovering the loss or as soon as practicable after that.

You and the insured person must take reasonable steps to prevent or reduce loss, and must not dispose of damaged property until we have had a chance to inspect it. These obligations are subject to the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition.

Declarations

The premium is based on the assignees and dependants you declared for the policy period. You must tell us within 30 days when an assignee or dependant starts or ends an assignment, and give us their name, date of birth, host country and dates. We charge or refund premium for them on a pro-rata basis at the rates used to calculate the premium.

Failure to give a declaration does not affect a claim for an insured person who meets the Who Is Covered clause, subject to the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition.

General Conditions

Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth)

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts or modifies the operation of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where this policy is inconsistent with the Act, the Act prevails.

If this policy would otherwise let us refuse to pay a claim, in whole or in part, because of something you or an insured person did or did not do after this policy was entered into, section 54 of the Act applies. We may not refuse to pay the claim only because of that act or omission. Our liability is reduced by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced. Where the act or omission could reasonably be regarded as capable of causing or contributing to the loss, we may refuse to pay only the part of the claim that the act or omission caused or contributed to. We may not refuse any part that you or the insured person prove the act or omission did not cause. This applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation in this policy, not only to the claims conditions.

Cancellation

You may cancel this policy at any time by telling us. We refund the premium for the unexpired period of insurance on a pro-rata basis, less any non-refundable government charges.

We may cancel this policy only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows, by giving you written notice as that Act requires. We refund the premium for the unexpired period on a pro-rata basis.

Cancellation does not affect cover for a trip that started before the cancellation took effect, until that trip ends.

Renewal

Each renewal is a new contract. Before this policy ends, we will tell you whether we offer to renew it and on what terms, as s.58 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires. We are not obliged to offer renewal.

Fraud

If a claim is made fraudulently, we may refuse to pay it, as s.56 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows.

Sanctions

We do not provide cover, pay any claim or provide any benefit under this policy to the extent that doing so would expose us to any sanction, prohibition or restriction under: - the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth), the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or regulations made under either Act; or - the trade or economic sanctions laws of the United Kingdom, the United States of America or the European Union, to the extent they apply to us.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that naming the UK, US and EU regimes is appropriate for Australian capacity; this is a deliberate library-wide scope choice.]

GST

The premium includes GST where it applies, under A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth). Insurance for travel outside Australia is GST-free to the extent the law allows, and the schedule shows how the premium is divided between overseas and domestic travel. Where you are entitled to an input tax credit for a cost we pay, we pay the cost less that credit. The personal accident benefits are fixed amounts and are not adjusted for GST.

Privacy

We collect, use and disclose personal information about you and insured persons, including health information, to assess your proposal, administer this policy, provide emergency assistance and manage claims, in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and our privacy policy. You confirm you will tell insured persons that you have given us their information and where they can read our privacy policy. Our privacy policy explains how to access and correct information and how to complain about a privacy matter.

Disputes and Complaints

If you or an insured person are unhappy with our service or a decision, contact us at the details in the schedule. We will acknowledge your complaint within one business day and give you our decision within 30 calendar days.

If you are not satisfied with our response, or we have not responded within 30 calendar days, you may take your complaint to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA), a free and independent dispute resolution service, if it falls within AFCA's rules: www.afca.org.au, 1800 931 678, info@afca.org.au.

Governing Law

This policy is governed by the law of the Australian state or territory shown in the schedule. If the schedule does not show one, it is governed by the law of New South Wales.

Jurisdiction

You and we submit to the jurisdiction of the courts of the state or territory whose law governs this policy, and of any court that may hear appeals from them.

Headings and References

Headings are for convenience only. A reference to legislation includes any legislation that amends or replaces it. A reference to a clause by its heading is a reference to that clause as it appears in this wording. A reference to an endorsement by its code is a reference to that endorsement in the Endorsement Library, and it applies only if the schedule shows it.

How to Make a Claim

  1. In an emergency, call the 24-hour emergency assistance number shown in the schedule.
  2. Otherwise, contact your broker, or contact us at the details shown in the schedule, as soon as reasonably practicable (see the Making a Claim obligation).
  3. Send us the claim form with original receipts, medical certificates, carrier and police reports, and evidence of the trip.
  4. Tell us about any other insurance, carrier compensation or card scheme that may cover the loss.
  5. We will acknowledge your claim, and keep you informed, as the General Insurance Code of Practice requires where the insurer subscribes to it.
