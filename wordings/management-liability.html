About This Policy

This is a modular policy. Your schedule shows which sections are operative — you may have all five sections or only some. Each section covers a different type of risk:

  • Directors' and Officers' Liability
  • Company Liability
  • Employment Practices Liability
  • Statutory Liability
  • Crime

In this policy, the liability sections are the Directors' and Officers' Liability, Company Liability, Employment Practices Liability, and Statutory Liability sections.

This policy, together with your schedule and any endorsements, forms your contract of insurance with us. Words shown in bold have special meanings — you will find their definitions in the Definitions section.

If a claim involves more than one section: We will cover it under the section that gives you the most favourable cover. We will not pay twice for the same loss under different sections. The limit of liability, any sub-limit, and the excess of the section selected under this rule are the ones that apply to that claim. Payment under that section reduces only that section's limit of liability and the overall aggregate limit of liability — not the limit of any other section (see the Sub-Limits provision).

Before this policy was entered into, you were given written notice, as required by s.40(1) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), of the effect of s.40(3) (see the Notify Us of Circumstances obligation).

Remediation following the Claude pre-review of 2 November 2026. This release replaces version 1.4.0 (November 2026). Changes from version 1.4.0:

  • Cancellation: you may cancel at any time and receive a pro-rata refund. The deduction for amounts paid or reserved is removed. Our cancellation requires statutory notice and not less than 30 days (the 30-day period is the library's settled position).
  • New definitions of Control group, Discovery and Responsible person. Company knowledge is the knowledge of the control group in the duty of disclosure, severability, continuous cover, prior and pending, prior known matters and environmental clean-up provisions. The Severability and Non-Imputation condition now covers knowledge as well as conduct, and covers the company.
  • Duty of Disclosure: states that the duty is the company's, and that individual treatment of insured persons is contractual.
  • New Non-Avoidance for Insured Persons condition. We will not avoid cover for an insured person except for that person's own fraud, and we waive the section 28(3) reduction in favour of innocent insured persons.
  • Retroactive Date: new operative condition, and each insuring clause now states it. Related wrongful acts that began before the retroactive date are treated as committed before it. The Claim definition now aggregates the same, similar or related wrongful acts and wrongful acts with a common cause. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
  • Continuous Cover: now capped at the lower of the current and previous limits, on the less favourable terms.
  • Company Liability exclusions: the Directors' and Officers' exclusions now apply to the company by reference to the knowledge of the control group.
  • Priority of Payment: now applies when the overall aggregate is short, and gives discharge for good-faith payments.
  • Defence Costs: repayment now also applies where the company or another insurer pays the same costs.
  • Crime section: cover now runs on discovery by a responsible person, and the Crime section's use of "you" means the company only. New clauses for Prior Insurance and Superseded Policies, Single Loss, and Valuation. Recoveries are applied to the company's uninsured loss first. Prior discovery now includes matters notified under a prior policy. Default set for underwriter confirmation.
  • Social Engineering Fraud endorsement: now operates on discovery. Its exclusion of loss covered by a cyber policy is replaced by the crime and cyber priority rule.
  • Third-Party Crime endorsement: now applies the Crime section's discovery, superseded policy, single loss and valuation provisions.
  • Side A Difference in Conditions endorsement: the trigger is restated, financial impairment is replaced by insolvency, the exclusions are fixed and not left to the schedule, and Insured versus Insured and Prior Known Matters now apply. Default set for underwriter confirmation.

Who This Policy Is For

This wording is written for businesses and professionals. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement.

Guidance for issuing insurers and distributors — not an operative policy term. Before distributing this wording, the issuing insurer or its authorised distributor should:

Definitions

These definitions apply throughout this policy, except where a section states its own definition that displaces a general definition for that section (for example, Crime Loss in the Crime section displaces Loss). Some sections have additional definitions specific to that section — you will find these at the start of the relevant section.

Claim

means:

(a) any written demand for compensation made against an insured;

(b) any writ, summons, cross-claim, or other legal proceeding issued against an insured;

(c) any formal investigation, examination, or inquiry by a regulator into the conduct of an insured, where the investigation could result in personal liability or a penalty; or

(d) any extradition proceeding, or the execution of a warrant for arrest, against an insured person in connection with a wrongful act.

Claims are related if they arise from the same wrongful act, or from wrongful acts that are the same, similar, or related, or that have a common originating cause, act, omission, or set of facts or circumstances. Related claims are treated as a single claim, first made when the earliest of them was first made.

Company

means the entity named as the named insured in the schedule, and any subsidiary.

Control group

means the company's chief executive officer, chief financial officer, general counsel, head of risk or insurance, and directors, or a person performing an equivalent role.

Cyber event

means:

(a) any access to, or use of, a computer system by a person who has no authority to access it, or who exceeds the authority they have; (b) any malicious code, ransomware, denial of service attack or other attack on a computer system; (c) any failure, outage or error of a computer system; or (d) any actual or suspected unauthorised acquisition, disclosure, alteration, loss or destruction of data held electronically.

A computer system is any computer, hardware, software, network, cloud service or electronic storage that is used, owned, leased or relied on by the company.

Defence costs

means the reasonable legal costs and expenses incurred with our prior written consent in investigating, defending, or settling a claim. We will not unreasonably withhold or delay our consent. We will advance defence costs as they are incurred, before the final outcome of a claim is determined.

Employee

means any natural person who works for the company under a contract of employment, including permanent, fixed-term, casual, seasonal, and part-time employees. It includes volunteers and interns. It does not include independent contractors.

Endorsement

means a document attached to this policy that changes its terms.

Excess

means the amount shown in the schedule that you must pay as the first part of each claim or Crime Loss. The excess may differ by section. See the Excess provision and the Excess Waivers provision.

Extended Discovery Period

means the period described in the Extended Discovery Period condition in the Crime section, during which you may discover and notify us of a dishonest act that occurred before the end of the policy period, for the purposes of the Crime section.

Extended Reporting Period

means the period described in the Extended Reporting Period condition in the General Conditions section, during which you may notify us of a claim arising from a wrongful act that occurred before the end of the policy period, for the purposes of the liability sections. It does not extend the policy period itself, and does not apply to the Crime section — see the definition of Extended Discovery Period.

Final adjudication

means a judgment, verdict, or order of a court or tribunal of competent jurisdiction that is no longer subject to appeal — whether because the time to appeal has expired, or all appeals have been exhausted, abandoned, or dismissed — and includes a formal admission, guilty plea, or consent order to the same effect. Every reference in this policy to conduct being established, admitted, or determined by final adjudication carries this meaning.

Insured

means:

(a) the company;

(b) any insured person; and

(c) where the context requires, any employee.

Insured person

means any natural person who is, was, or during the policy period becomes:

(a) a director (including a shadow director or de facto director) of the company;

(b) an officer of the company (including the company secretary, chief financial officer, chief executive officer, or any person who participates in making decisions that affect the whole or a substantial part of the company's business);

(c) a member of a committee of management (for associations and not-for-profits); or

(d) an equivalent position in any subsidiary.

Limit of liability

means the amount shown in the schedule for each section, and the overall aggregate limit of liability shown in the schedule, which is the most we will pay across all sections combined during the policy period.

Loss

means the amount an insured becomes legally liable to pay as compensation to a third party, including the claimant's legal costs awarded against the insured, and including defence costs. It does not include taxes or amounts that are not insurable at law. This definition does not apply to the Crime section — see the definition of Crime Loss.

Named insured

means the entity shown as the named insured in the schedule.

Policy period

means the period shown in the schedule.

Regulator

means any government body, statutory authority, regulatory agency, or professional body with the power to investigate, examine, or take enforcement action against an insured. It includes ASIC, the OAIC, the ACCC, the ATO, SafeWork (and state and territory equivalents), the Fair Work Commission, the Fair Work Ombudsman, and any equivalent body.

Retroactive date

means the date shown in the schedule for a section of this policy. The Retroactive Date condition states how it applies. The retroactive date does not apply to the Crime section.

Schedule

means the document titled "Schedule" issued with this policy, as amended by any endorsement.

Subsidiary

means any entity in which the named insured holds more than 50% of the voting rights, or over which the named insured has effective management control, at the time the relevant wrongful act occurs.

We / us / our

means the insurer named in the schedule.

Wrongful act

means any actual or alleged act, error, omission, misleading statement, breach of duty, breach of trust, or breach of any statutory or regulatory obligation.

You / your

means the company and, where the context requires, any insured person or employee. In the Crime section, you and your mean the company only.

Directors' and Officers' Liability

This section must be marked "Included" in the schedule to be operative.

What Is Covered — Directors' and Officers' Liability

Personal Liability of Directors and Officers

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act in their capacity as a director or officer of the company, committed on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), we will pay:

(a) any loss the insured person becomes personally liable to pay; and

(b) defence costs.

Company Reimbursement

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act in their capacity as a director or officer of the company, committed on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), and the company is legally permitted to indemnify the insured person and has done so, we will reimburse the company for the amount it has paid.

Direct Payment

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act in their capacity as a director or officer of the company, committed on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), and the company is legally permitted to indemnify the insured person but has not done so — for example, because the company is insolvent or has refused to indemnify — we will pay the insured person directly.

Non-Indemnifiable Loss

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act in their capacity as a director or officer of the company, committed on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), and the company is not legally permitted to indemnify the insured person (for example, where section 199A of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) prohibits it), we will pay the insured person directly.

This cover is for the insured person. The company's actions, and the company's financial position, do not reduce it. The Non-Avoidance for Insured Persons condition applies to it.

See also the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition, which applies wherever a claim under this policy involves a civil penalty order, compensation order, or related proceeding under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth).

Extensions — Directors' and Officers' Liability

Retired Directors and Officers

If an insured person retires, resigns, or otherwise ceases to hold office during or before the policy period, this section continues to cover claims first made against them for up to 72 months after the end of the policy period, at no additional premium — provided:

(a) the claim arises from a wrongful act committed while they held office; and

(b) no other directors' and officers' insurance is available to them.

Spouse, Estate, and Legal Representative

If an insured person dies, becomes incapacitated, or is made bankrupt, we will cover their spouse, domestic partner, estate, or legal representative for any claim that would have been covered if made against the insured person personally.

We will not cover any wrongful act of the spouse, estate, or legal representative themselves.

ASIC Investigation Costs

We will pay an insured person's defence costs in responding to a formal investigation or examination by ASIC into their conduct as a director or officer, even if no claim has yet been made.

The most we will pay under this extension during the policy period is the ASIC investigation costs sub-limit shown in the schedule. The sub-limit is part of the Directors' and Officers' Liability section limit of liability. The E05 Increased ASIC Investigation Costs Sub-limit endorsement increases it.

Extradition Costs

If an insured person faces extradition proceedings in connection with a wrongful act that would be covered by this section, we will pay their defence costs in resisting extradition.

Loss of Earnings — Attending Proceedings

If an insured person is required to attend court, a tribunal, a regulatory hearing, or an examination in connection with a claim covered by this section, we will pay $500 per day or part-day of attendance.

Public Relations Costs — Directors and Officers

Following a claim covered by this section, we will pay the reasonable costs of engaging a public relations consultant to protect or restore the personal reputation of an insured person.

What Is Not Covered — Directors' and Officers' Liability

Deliberate and Dishonest Acts

We do not cover any claim arising from any deliberately dishonest, fraudulent, or criminal act by the insured person — but only after a final adjudication establishes that the conduct occurred.

Until that point, we will continue to advance defence costs.

We will still cover any insured person who did not personally commit, participate in, or know about the dishonest conduct. This protection applies separately to each insured person.

Personal Profit

We do not cover any claim for any personal profit or advantage gained by the insured person to which they were not legally entitled — but only after a final adjudication establishes that the profit was improperly gained.

Prior Known Matters

We do not cover any claim arising from any wrongful act, fact, matter, or circumstance that the insured person seeking cover knew about before the start of this policy period and that they knew, or ought reasonably to have known, might lead to a claim. The Severability and Non-Imputation condition applies to this exclusion.

This is the prior-knowledge test that applies to claims under this section. The Prior and Pending Matters exclusion in General Exclusions is a general backstop exclusion for other sections and for pending proceedings generally, and does not impose a different knowledge test on a claim under this section.

Insured Versus Insured

We do not cover any claim brought by one insured against another insured — except for:

(a) a claim brought by a liquidator, administrator, receiver, or similar officer appointed to the company;

(b) a claim brought by an employee under the Employment Practices Liability section;

(c) a claim brought by a whistleblower; and

(d) a cross-claim or contribution claim arising from a claim first brought by an independent third party.

Related Party Claims

We do not cover any claim brought by any person or entity that holds:

(a) 15% or more of the issued shares or voting rights of the company or any parent entity (the same threshold used in the Directors' and Officers' Dishonesty exclusion); or

(b) an equivalent executive or managerial interest — meaning a role, such as chief executive officer or an equivalent position, carrying authority to control or materially influence the company's management —

except where the claim is brought by a liquidator, administrator, or receiver of that entity.

Major Transactions

We do not cover any claim arising from an initial public offering, listing on a securities exchange, scheme of arrangement, or any transaction that results in the company becoming a publicly listed entity.

This exposure requires a listed company D&O policy.

Professional Services

We do not cover any claim for errors or omissions in professional advice or services provided by the insured person to a third-party client. This is the domain of professional indemnity insurance.

However: this exclusion does not apply to the insured person's oversight, supervision, or governance of the company's professional services business. For example, if a director is sued for failing to implement adequate quality controls across the firm, that is a governance claim covered by this section.

Bodily Injury and Property Damage

We do not cover any claim arising from death, bodily injury, or physical damage to tangible property — except for:

(a) defence costs incurred in WHS proceedings (covered under the Statutory Liability section if operative); and

(b) mental injury, emotional distress, or psychological harm arising from an employment practices wrongful act (covered under the Employment Practices Liability section if operative).

Company Liability

This section must be marked "Included" in the schedule to be operative.

What Is Covered — Company Liability

Entity Cover

If a claim is first made against the company during the policy period for a wrongful act of the company itself (not arising from professional services provided to third-party clients), committed on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), we will pay:

(a) any loss the company becomes legally liable to pay; and

(b) defence costs.

This covers the company's own liability for matters such as:

  • misleading or deceptive conduct in its own business affairs (not in the provision of services to clients);
  • breach of competition or consumer law in its own business dealings;
  • breach of a duty owed to a shareholder or member; and
  • wrongful conduct in connection with a corporate transaction (merger, acquisition, restructure).

Extensions — Company Liability

Tax Audit Costs

If the ATO or a state revenue authority commences a formal audit of the company during the policy period, we will pay the reasonable costs of your accountant in responding to the audit.

This does not cover any tax, interest, or penalty payable as a result of the audit.

The most we will pay under this extension during the policy period is the Tax Audit Costs sub-limit shown in the schedule. The sub-limit is part of the Company Liability section limit of liability.

Public Relations Costs — Company Liability

Following a claim covered by this section, we will pay the reasonable costs of engaging a public relations consultant.

What Is Not Covered — Company Liability

All exclusions from the Directors' and Officers' Liability section apply to this section, with two changes. A reference to the insured person means the insured seeking cover. For the company, its knowledge or conduct is that of a member of the control group (see the Severability and Non-Imputation condition). This section also has these exclusions:

Professional Services — Company Liability

We do not cover any claim for errors or omissions in professional advice or services provided by the company to third-party clients.

This exposure is covered under professional indemnity insurance.

Product Liability

We do not cover any claim arising from the design, manufacture, supply, distribution, sale, or recall of any tangible product.

Contractual Liability

We do not cover any claim for any liability assumed under a contract that is greater than the liability the company would have at law without the contract.

Cyber Event

We do not cover any claim arising from or in connection with a cyber event.

This exposure is covered under cyber insurance.

Employment Practices Liability

This section must be marked "Included" in the schedule to be operative.

Additional Definitions — Employment Practices Liability

Employment practices wrongful act

means any actual or alleged:

(a) wrongful dismissal, wrongful termination, or constructive dismissal;

(b) breach of an employment contract (written, oral, or implied);

(c) discrimination or harassment (including sexual harassment) on any ground prohibited by law, including age, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, race, disability, religion, pregnancy, marital status, or political opinion;

(d) bullying or workplace intimidation;

(e) failure to provide a safe and healthy workplace (to the extent this gives rise to civil liability — WHS prosecutions are covered under the Statutory Liability section);

(f) wrongful failure to promote, wrongful demotion, or wrongful failure to employ;

(g) wrongful deprivation of a career opportunity;

(h) misrepresentation to an employee or prospective employee about the terms or nature of their employment;

(i) retaliation against a whistleblower, or any adverse action taken against a person who has made a complaint, disclosure, or report under any whistleblower protection legislation;

(j) breach of privacy of an employee's personal information (to the extent the claim relates to the employment relationship);

(k) wrongful denial or reduction of any benefit or entitlement; and

(l) any other civil liability arising from the employment relationship.

What Is Covered — Employment Practices Liability

Employment Practices Claims

If a claim is first made against any insured during the policy period for an employment practices wrongful act committed on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), we will pay:

(a) any loss the insured becomes legally liable to pay as compensation; and

(b) defence costs.

This covers claims brought by current, former, or prospective employees, and by third parties such as unions, the Fair Work Commission, the Fair Work Ombudsman, or the Australian Human Rights Commission.

Extensions — Employment Practices Liability

Fair Work Proceedings

We will pay your defence costs in any proceedings before the Fair Work Commission, Fair Work Ombudsman, or the Australian Human Rights Commission arising from an employment practices wrongful act, even if the proceedings do not constitute a claim as defined.

Workplace Mediation Costs

If a workplace dispute arises that has not yet become a claim, and you engage a qualified mediator to resolve it, we will pay the reasonable mediation costs — with our prior consent.

Employee Counselling Costs

If an employment practices wrongful act causes psychological harm to an employee (whether or not a claim has been made), we will pay the reasonable costs of professional counselling for the affected employee, up to 12 sessions.

What Is Not Covered — Employment Practices Liability

Workers' Compensation

We do not cover any claim that is covered or ought to be covered by any workers' compensation, workplace injury, or accident compensation scheme, whether statutory or contractual.

Employment Entitlements

We do not cover any claim for wages, salary, overtime, leave entitlements, superannuation contributions, or any other amount that you were legally obliged to pay in the ordinary course of the employment relationship — as distinct from compensation for a wrongful act.

We cover damages for wrongful dismissal, but not underpaid wages that triggered the complaint.

Industrial Action

We do not cover any claim arising from any industrial action, lockout, strike, or collective bargaining dispute.

Deliberate Unlawful Employment Practices

We do not cover any claim arising from an employment practices wrongful act that the insured deliberately committed with the knowledge that it was unlawful — but only after a final adjudication establishes this. Until that point, we will advance defence costs.

The severability principle applies: we will still cover any insured who did not personally commit or know about the deliberate conduct.

What Is Covered — Statutory Liability

Statutory Proceedings

If a prosecution, proceeding, or enforcement action is brought against any insured during the policy period under any Australian federal, state, or territory statute in connection with the operations of the company, in respect of conduct occurring on or after any retroactive date shown in the schedule for this section (see the Retroactive Date condition), we will pay:

(a) fines and penalties, but only those that the law allows to be insured and that this clause does not exclude; and

(b) defence costs.

We do not pay any fine or penalty that the law does not allow to be insured. In particular, we do not pay a penalty imposed under work health and safety law in a State or Territory whose law prohibits insurance of it (including New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland), and we do not pay a criminal fine.

We will advance defence costs for WHS prosecutions immediately upon notification, without requiring a formal coverage determination first. WHS prosecutions move quickly and the consequences are severe — we will not delay your defence.

Extensions — Statutory Liability

Environmental Clean-Up Costs

If a regulator orders you to remediate environmental contamination arising from a wrongful act covered by this section, we will pay the reasonable clean-up costs — provided no member of the control group expected or intended the contamination.

Extradition Costs (Regulatory)

If an insured person faces extradition in connection with a statutory offence that would be covered by this section, we will pay their defence costs in resisting extradition.

What Is Not Covered — Statutory Liability

Criminal Conduct

We do not cover fines or penalties arising from conduct that is determined by a final adjudication to be intentionally criminal — but we will advance defence costs until that determination is made.

We will still cover the company and any insured person who did not personally commit, participate in, or know about the criminal conduct. This protection applies separately to each insured person.

Uninsurable Penalties

We do not cover any fine, penalty, or amount that the law does not allow to be insured in the relevant jurisdiction. This includes:

(a) any criminal fine;

(b) any penalty for an offence under work health and safety law (including the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 and State and Territory equivalents) in a State or Territory whose law prohibits insuring it, including New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland; and

(c) any liability that section 199B of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) prevents the company from insuring, as the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition states.

Where it is uncertain whether the law allows a fine or penalty to be insured, we will advance defence costs. We will not pay the fine or penalty unless it is established that the law allows it to be insured.

Tax and Revenue

We do not cover the underlying tax, duty, or revenue liability itself — only the penalties and defence costs are covered.

Workers' Compensation Premiums

We do not cover any workers' compensation premium, levy, or contribution.

Additional Definitions — Crime

Dishonest act

means an act committed by an employee with the clear intention of:

(a) causing the company direct financial loss; and

(b) obtaining a personal financial benefit for the employee beyond their normal salary, wages, bonus, commission, or other agreed compensation.

Crime Loss

means the direct financial loss the company sustains as a direct result of a dishonest act covered under the Employee Dishonesty clause, calculated in accordance with the How We Calculate the Crime Loss provision. It does not include indirect or consequential loss (see the Indirect and Consequential Loss exclusion). This definition applies in place of the definition of Loss for the purposes of this section. References elsewhere in this policy to "claim or Crime Loss" (for example, in the definition of Excess, the Section Limits provision and the Excess provision) include a Crime Loss under this section.

Discovery

means the point at which any responsible person first has reason to believe, on the information then available, that a Crime Loss covered under the Crime section has been or is likely to have been sustained, whether or not the amount, cause, or full circumstances of the loss are then known, and regardless of when the dishonest act occurred. Discover and discovered have matching meanings.

Responsible person

means any director, officer, partner, principal, risk manager, compliance officer, internal auditor, chief financial officer, or head of finance of the company; or, where the company has no natural person holding any of those roles, its most senior officer or manager responsible for its financial affairs at the relevant time.

What Is Covered — Crime

Employee Dishonesty

If the company first discovers, during the policy period, that an employee has caused it a Crime Loss through a dishonest act, we will pay the Crime Loss. This applies whenever the dishonest act occurred, subject to the Discovered Before This Policy exclusion and the Prior Insurance and Superseded Policies — Crime condition.

This covers:

(a) theft of money, securities, or tangible property by an employee;

(b) fraudulent alteration or forgery of documents, cheques, or electronic records by an employee;

(c) fraudulent electronic funds transfer by an employee; and

(d) deliberate destruction of property or data by an employee with the intent to conceal a dishonest act.

The company must notify us within 60 days of discovery. The Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition applies to this time limit.

How We Calculate the Crime Loss

We will calculate the Crime Loss by deducting from the amount the company has lost:

(a) any money or property recovered from the employee or any third party;

(b) any amounts the employee owes the company (whether or not related to the dishonest act); and

(c) the value of any property of the employee that the company holds.

If we pay a Crime Loss and the company later recovers any amount from the employee or any third party, the recovery is first applied to the company's uninsured loss (including the excess and any amount above the limit of liability), and the remainder is applied to reimburse us.

Single Loss — Crime

Single loss means all Crime Losses arising from the same act, or from a series of the same, similar, or related acts, by the same person or group of persons acting together (whether acting alone or in collusion, and whether or not every person involved is identified). A single loss is treated as one Crime Loss for the limit of liability and the excess.

Where a single loss would otherwise span two or more consecutive policy periods issued by us to the company, it is treated as discovered in, and is subject to the limit, excess and terms of, the policy period in which it was first discovered — even if the acts making up the single loss continue, or further amounts are discovered, in a later policy period. This is subject to the Prior Insurance and Superseded Policies — Crime condition where the loss began under a separate, expired prior policy.

Where a single loss falls within more than one insuring clause or endorsement in the Crime section, we will pay it once, under the one with the highest applicable limit, and only one excess applies.

Valuation — Crime

Where the Crime section pays for the loss of money, securities, or property:

(a) securities are valued at their market value on the date of Discovery or, at our election, we may instead pay the reasonable cost of a replacement in kind, or arrange a bond of indemnity;

(b) foreign-currency money is converted to Australian dollars at the exchange rate published by the Reserve Bank of Australia (or, if none is published for that currency, a comparable published rate we reasonably nominate) on the date of Discovery;

(c) where a security or currency has no readily ascertainable market value on the date of Discovery, we will determine its value using the most recent published value before that date, adjusted as reasonably necessary to reflect known intervening events; and

(d) tangible property is valued at the lowest of the cost to repair it, the cost to replace it with property of a similar kind and quality, and its actual cash value, in each case on the date of Discovery.

Extensions — Crime

Reconstitution of Records

If an employee destroys or alters your financial records to conceal a dishonest act, we will pay the reasonable costs of reconstructing those records.

What Is Not Covered — Crime

E06 Side A Difference in Conditions

This endorsement adds a Side A limit of liability, shown in the schedule for this endorsement, for loss of an insured person that the company does not indemnify. It is in addition to, and does not reduce, the Directors' and Officers' Liability section limit of liability or the overall aggregate.

The Side A limit pays an insured person's loss, as described in the Non-Indemnifiable Loss clause and the Direct Payment clause, where the company does not indemnify the insured person because it is not permitted by law to do so, is insolvent (it cannot pay its debts as they fall due, or an administrator, liquidator, receiver or similar officer has been appointed), or refuses to do so. It pays only when:

  • the Directors' and Officers' Liability section limit of liability has been exhausted by payment; or
  • any other directors' and officers' insurance shown in the schedule for this endorsement has been exhausted by payment, or does not pay the loss because the other insurer is insolvent, because the other insurer refuses or fails to pay within 60 days of a written request (unless the refusal is because of a matter that this policy also excludes), or because the other insurance has been cancelled or avoided.

The exclusions in the Directors' and Officers' Liability section apply to the Side A limit, including the Insured Versus Insured exclusion and the Prior Known Matters exclusion, and each conduct exclusion applies only after a final adjudication, as it does in that section. No excess applies. The Non-Avoidance for Insured Persons condition applies to the Side A limit. Where we pay under the Side A limit because another insurer refused or failed to pay, we are subrogated to the insured person's rights against that insurer.

E07 Social Engineering Fraud

The Crime section is extended to cover the company's direct financial loss of money or securities, first discovered during the policy period, that was transferred, paid or delivered by an employee in good faith in reliance on an instruction that:

  • purports to come from a director, officer, employee, customer, supplier or other person with whom the company has an established business relationship; and
  • was in fact sent by a person who impersonated that person, without the knowledge or consent of the company or of the person impersonated.

The Discovered Before This Policy exclusion, the Prior Insurance and Superseded Policies — Crime condition, the Single Loss — Crime provision and the Valuation — Crime provision apply to this endorsement.

Cover under this endorsement is not conditional on the company having followed any verification procedure. Where the company has not followed the verification procedure shown in the schedule for this endorsement, and that failure caused or contributed to the loss, we may reduce our payment to the extent the failure caused or contributed to it, as section 54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows.

The most we will pay under this endorsement during the policy period is the social engineering sub-limit shown in the schedule, which is part of the Crime section limit of liability. The social engineering excess shown in the schedule applies to each loss. All losses arising from one impersonation, or from a series of related impersonations by the same person or group, are one loss.

This endorsement does not cover loss of cryptocurrency or other digital assets. The Other Insurance provision states how this endorsement applies where a cyber insurance policy also covers the loss. For this endorsement, the definition of Dishonest act and the Employee Dishonesty clause do not require a dishonest act by an employee.

Schedule variables: social engineering sub-limit; social engineering excess; verification procedure.

E10 Excess Layer

This policy applies only in excess of the underlying insurance shown in the schedule for this endorsement, and only after the underlying limits have been exhausted by payment of covered loss. Except as this endorsement states, this policy follows the terms of the primary underlying policy for the sections shown in the schedule.

Where the underlying limits are reduced or exhausted by payment, this policy drops down to apply in excess of the reduced limits, or in place of the exhausted limits. This policy does not drop down because an underlying insurer becomes insolvent or fails to pay for any other reason. We are not obliged to defend any claim. We may join the defence or settlement of any claim that is likely to involve this policy.
