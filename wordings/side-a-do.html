Side A Directors' & Officers' Liability

Excess cover reserved for individual directors and officers, above the company’s D&O or management liability insurance.

About This Policy

This policy protects individual directors and officers against loss that their company cannot or will not indemnify. It sits above the company's directors' and officers' liability or management liability insurance (the underlying D&O insurance) and pays only after that insurance has been exhausted by payment.

It is called "Side A" cover because it protects only the individuals, not the company. Its limit is reserved for directors and officers. The company cannot claim under it, and claims against the company that use up the underlying D&O insurance do not reduce it.

For cover of loss the company does not indemnify, this policy follows the terms of the underlying D&O insurance, except where this policy says otherwise. It does not drop down to fill gaps in the underlying D&O insurance unless the schedule shows that Endorsement E04 (Difference in Conditions) applies.

Who this policy is for: Directors and officers who want additional limit reserved for themselves, in particular where the underlying D&O insurance also covers the company (for example, a management liability package), so that claims against the company can use up the limit available to individuals.

This policy, together with your schedule and any endorsements, forms your contract of insurance with us. Words shown in bold have special meanings, set out under Definitions.

Before this policy was entered into, you were given written notice, as required by s 40(1) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), of the effect of s 40(3) (see Notify Us of Circumstances).

Schedule

(Completed separately for each policy — contains your specific details)

  • The company and its directors and officers

  • The policy period

  • The retroactive date

  • The underlying D&O insurance: each policy, its insurer, its limit of liability, and its policy period

  • The attachment point (the total of the limits of the underlying D&O insurance)

  • The limit of liability of this policy

  • Whether Endorsement E04 (Difference in Conditions) applies and, if it does, the DIC retention

  • Governing law and jurisdiction (the Australian state or territory)

  • The premium

  • Any portion of the premium payable other than by the company (see Premium and Corporations Act s 199B)

  • Any endorsements

Definitions

Term Meaning
Attachment point The total of the limits of liability of the underlying D&O insurance, as shown in the schedule.
Claim

(a) any written demand for compensation made against an insured person;

(b) any writ, summons, cross-claim, or legal proceeding issued against an insured person;

(c) any formal investigation, examination, or inquiry by a regulator into the conduct of an insured person, where the investigation could result in personal liability or a penalty against that individual;

(d) any extradition proceeding or warrant for arrest against an insured person in connection with a wrongful act; and

(e) any proceeding commenced by a liquidator, administrator, or receiver against an insured person.

Two or more claims arising from the same, similar, or related wrongful acts are treated as a single claim, first made on the date the earliest of those claims was first made.
Company The entity named in the schedule, and any subsidiary.
Defence costs The reasonable legal costs and expenses incurred by an insured person with our prior written consent in investigating, defending, or settling a claim. We will not unreasonably withhold or delay our consent.
Endorsement A document attached to this policy that changes its terms.
Final adjudication means a judgment or ruling of a court, tribunal, or arbitral body, after the exhaustion of all available rights of appeal or the expiry of the time within which an appeal may be lodged without an appeal being lodged, or a formal admission by the insured person, that establishes the conduct in question. Every reference in this policy to conduct being established, admitted, or determined by final adjudication (including in Dishonesty and Fraud and Personal Profit) carries this meaning, including all appeals.
Insured person

(a) a director (including a shadow director, de facto director, or alternate director) of the company;

(b) an officer of the company (including the company secretary, chief executive, chief financial officer, chief operating officer, general counsel, or any person who participates in making decisions that affect the whole or a substantial part of the company's business);

(c) a member of a committee of management, board of governors, or equivalent governing body; or

(d) any person in an equivalent position in a subsidiary.

It also includes the lawful spouse, domestic partner, estate, or legal representative of an insured person — but only for claims arising from the insured person's wrongful acts, not their own.
Loss

The amount an insured person becomes personally liable to pay, including: (a) compensatory damages (including interest); (b) the claimant's legal costs awarded against the insured person; (c) defence costs; and (d) settlements agreed with our prior written consent.

Loss does not include: (i) any amount the company has actually paid to, or on behalf of, the insured person by way of indemnity or advancement; (ii) taxes, or the return of remuneration to which the insured person was not entitled; (iii) fines, penalties, pecuniary penalty orders, or compensation orders, to the extent the law does not permit us to pay them; or (iv) matters uninsurable at law. [WP REVIEW: confirm the insurability of civil pecuniary penalty orders and compensation orders made against officers, and whether any category should be named expressly. Section 199B of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) governs who may pay the premium, not insurability, and is addressed in Premium and Corporations Act s 199B]
Non-indemnifiable loss

A loss that the company: (a) is not legally permitted to indemnify (for example, where indemnification is prohibited by section 199A of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)); (b) is financially unable to indemnify (because of insolvency, liquidation, administration, receivership, or financial incapacity); or (c) refuses or fails to indemnify (despite being legally permitted and financially able to do so).

See also Presumptive Indemnification, which governs how this definition is applied where the company's ability or willingness to indemnify is uncertain or contested.
Policy period The period shown in the schedule.
Regulator Any government body, statutory authority, regulatory agency, or professional body with the power to investigate or take enforcement action. Includes ASIC, APRA, the ACCC, the ATO, SafeWork, the OAIC, and any equivalent body.
Retroactive date

means the date shown in the schedule. This policy does not cover any claim arising from a wrongful act committed, in whole or in part, before the retroactive date.

Where two or more wrongful acts are treated as a single claim under the definition of Claim, the retroactive date is applied to that claim by reference to the date of the earliest of those wrongful acts.
Schedule The document titled "Schedule" issued with this policy, as amended by any endorsement.
Subsidiary Any entity in which the company holds more than 50% of the voting rights, or over which the company has effective management control, at the time of the relevant wrongful act.
Underlying D&O insurance The directors' and officers' liability or management liability policy or policies listed in the schedule as the underlying D&O insurance.
Underlying insurer The insurer, or each insurer, of the underlying D&O insurance.
We / us / our The insurer named in the schedule.
Wrongful act Any actual or alleged act, error, omission, misleading statement, breach of duty, breach of trust, or breach of any statutory or regulatory obligation, committed by an insured person in their capacity as a director or officer of the company.

What Is Covered

Side A Excess Cover

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act committed on or after the retroactive date, we will pay the insured person's non-indemnifiable loss in excess of the attachment point, up to the limit of liability of this policy.

Exhaustion of the Underlying D&O Insurance

This policy attaches only when the underlying D&O insurance has been exhausted by payment of loss. Payments count towards exhaustion whether they were made: (a) under any insuring clause of the underlying D&O insurance, including cover for the company's own liability and reimbursement of the company; (b) by the underlying insurer; or (c) by or on behalf of an insured person or the company, in place of the underlying insurer, in settlement of loss covered by the underlying D&O insurance. [WP REVIEW: confirm whether payments under (c), for example on a settlement with the underlying insurer below its limit, should count towards exhaustion, and whether we should require written notice of them]

Where the underlying D&O insurance has been partly exhausted by payment, the attachment point is reduced by the amount paid.

Following the Underlying D&O Insurance

Except where this policy states otherwise, this policy follows the terms, conditions, definitions and exclusions of the underlying D&O insurance, as they stand at the start of the policy period, that apply to cover for an insured person's loss which the company does not indemnify. Where this policy and the underlying D&O insurance are inconsistent, this policy applies.

This policy does not follow: (a) any cover for the company's own liability, or for reimbursement of the company; (b) any provision about limits, sub-limits, retentions, excesses, premium, or cancellation; or (c) any change to the underlying D&O insurance made after the start of the policy period, unless we agree to it in writing.

No Drop-Down

Unless Endorsement E04 (Difference in Conditions) applies, this policy does not pay any loss that the underlying D&O insurance does not cover, and does not pay below the attachment point for any reason, including the insolvency of the underlying insurer or its refusal or failure to pay. An amount the underlying insurer does not pay does not reduce the attachment point.

Priority and Independence

This policy is for the exclusive benefit of individual insured persons. The company has no right to claim under this policy and cannot direct, control, or assign the benefits of this policy.

If the company is in liquidation, administration, or receivership: (a) no liquidator, administrator, receiver, or creditor of the company has any right to access, assign, or claim the proceeds of this policy; (b) no court order or statutory charge against the company's assets affects the proceeds of this policy, because the proceeds belong to the insured person, not the company; and (c) we will pay the insured person directly — not through the company.

Presumptive Indemnification

For the purpose of determining whether a loss is a non-indemnifiable loss, it is presumed, unless we prove otherwise, that the company is legally permitted and financially able to indemnify the insured person for the loss. This presumption may be rebutted only by evidence establishing that the company is not legally permitted, or is not financially able, to indemnify the loss (for example, evidence of insolvency, liquidation, administration, receivership, or a deed of company arrangement), or that the company has refused or failed to indemnify.

Uncertainty as to whether the company is legally permitted or financially able to indemnify does not, by itself, entitle us to withhold or delay advancement of defence costs or payment of loss once this policy has attached.

Advancement of Defence Costs

Once this policy has attached, we will advance defence costs as they are incurred, without waiting for the outcome of the claim.

If it is later established that the insured person was not entitled to cover for those defence costs, they must repay them to us. If the company later indemnifies the insured person for the same loss (including from funds recovered in an insolvency, liquidation, administration, receivership, or deed of company arrangement), the insured person must repay us to the extent of the indemnity received, so that there is no double recovery.

What Is Not Covered

In addition to the exclusions of the underlying D&O insurance that this policy follows, we do not cover any claim or loss:

Loss Indemnified by the Company

to the extent the company has actually paid it to, or on behalf of, the insured person by way of indemnity or advancement. This exclusion does not apply to loss the company refuses or fails to pay.

Dishonesty and Fraud

arising from any deliberately dishonest, fraudulent, or criminal conduct of the insured person — but only after final adjudication establishes that the insured person personally committed the dishonest or fraudulent conduct. Until final adjudication, we will continue to advance defence costs. If final adjudication establishes dishonesty, the insured person must repay any defence costs we have advanced for the portion of the claim relating to the established dishonesty.

Personal Profit

for any personal profit, remuneration, or advantage gained by the insured person to which they were not legally entitled — but only after final adjudication establishes that the profit was improperly gained. Until final adjudication, we will continue to advance defence costs, on the same basis as Dishonesty and Fraud.

Prior and Pending Litigation

arising from, or in any way involving, any demand, suit, proceeding, investigation, or claim that was pending, or of which written notice had been given under any prior policy, before the retroactive date — regardless of whether the insured person seeking cover had knowledge of it.

Sanctions

We will not provide any cover, or make any payment, to the extent that doing so would expose us to enforcement action under:

(a) the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth) or the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or any sanctions, regulations, or designated-persons listings made under either Act; or

(b) any United Nations Security Council resolution binding on Australia.

US / Canada Jurisdiction

for any claim brought or maintained in a court in the United States or Canada — unless Endorsement E03 applies.

How Much We Will Pay

Limit of Liability

The most we will pay for all claims and defence costs combined during the policy period is the limit of liability shown in the schedule.

Defence Costs Within the Limit

Defence costs are included within the limit of liability, unless Endorsement E02 applies.

No Excess for Insured Persons

There is no excess payable by any insured person under this policy. Once this policy has attached, we will not require a director or officer to pay out of their own pocket before we respond.

Order of Payment — Priority

If the limit of liability is or may be insufficient to cover all insured persons' losses, we will allocate payments: (a) first, to non-executive directors and independent directors; (b) second, to executive directors and officers; in each case prioritising defence costs before compensatory payments.

Other Side A Insurance

If any other Side A, excess Side A, or difference-in-conditions insurance that is not listed in the schedule as underlying D&O insurance is available for the same non-indemnifiable loss, this policy applies in excess of that other insurance, unless that other insurance is expressly written to apply in excess of this policy. Where both policies are expressed to apply in excess of each other, each will contribute in proportion to its limit of liability. [WP REVIEW: confirm this clause is effective under s 45 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) where the other insurance is not specified in the schedule]

Non-Avoidance

We will not avoid this policy, or any insured person's cover under it, under s 28 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) or otherwise, for non-disclosure or misrepresentation in connection with the application for this policy — except where the specific insured person seeking cover personally committed the non-disclosure or misrepresentation, and did so fraudulently or with the intention to deceive us. Avoidance, where available, is limited to the cover of that insured person only, and does not affect the cover of any other, innocent insured person.

Where a non-disclosure or misrepresentation is not fraudulent, we will not reduce our liability to an innocent insured person under s 28(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) or otherwise, on account of that non-disclosure or misrepresentation. We expressly waive that remedy as against every innocent insured person. This paragraph does not limit our rights under s 28 against the insured person who personally made the fraudulent non-disclosure or misrepresentation.

Your Obligations

Notify Us of Claims

Any insured person may notify us directly of a claim. They do not need to go through the company or the company's broker. Tell us in writing as soon as reasonably practicable after you become aware of a claim that is notified under the underlying D&O insurance and that may reach this policy.

Notify Us of Circumstances

Tell us during the policy period if you become aware of any fact, matter, or circumstance that might reasonably be expected to give rise to a claim. Notified circumstances will be treated as a claim first made during this policy period, in accordance with s 40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Do Not Admit Liability

Do not admit liability or settle without our written consent where the settlement may reach this policy. We will not unreasonably withhold consent.

Cooperate

Cooperate with us and provide all information and documents we reasonably need, including copies of correspondence with the underlying insurer about the claim.

Maintenance of Underlying D&O Insurance

The company must keep the underlying D&O insurance in force, on the terms and limits shown in the schedule, for the policy period, and tell us promptly if it is cancelled, not renewed, or its limits or terms are reduced.

If the underlying D&O insurance is not maintained, we will pay only what we would have paid had it been maintained, and only to the extent permitted by s 54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) (see Insurance Contracts Act — Savings and No Contracting-Out). [WP REVIEW: confirm the effect of s 54 on this clause, and whether an innocent insured person should be protected where the company fails to maintain the underlying D&O insurance]

General Conditions

Duty of Disclosure

Each insured person has an individual duty of disclosure. We assess each person's disclosure separately. A failure by one person does not affect another person's cover (see Non-Avoidance).

Severability

This policy treats each insured person as if they have their own separate policy. The conduct, knowledge, or statements of one insured person are not attributed to any other insured person.

Subrogation and Recovery

When we make a payment under this policy, we take over the insured person's rights of recovery for that loss, including any right to be indemnified, or to have defence costs advanced, by the company.

We will not exercise any right of recovery against an insured person, except to recover amounts that must be repaid under Dishonesty and Fraud or Personal Profit after final adjudication. The insured person must give us reasonable help to exercise these rights, at our cost.

Any amount recovered, after deducting the reasonable costs of recovery, is applied first to repay the insured person for loss they have paid that this policy did not cover because the limit of liability was exhausted, and then to repay us.

Cancellation

By you (the company): The company may cancel this policy at any time by written notice to us. Cancellation does not affect the cover of any insured person who has already notified a claim or circumstance, and does not affect the accrued rights of any insured person under this policy up to the date of cancellation.

By us: We may only cancel this policy on grounds permitted by s 60 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where we are entitled to cancel, we will give each insured person written notice individually as required by s 59. We will refund the premium proportionally.

By an insured person: An individual insured person cannot cancel this policy, and the company cannot cancel it in a way that removes cover for any individual without their written consent.

Discovery Period

If this policy is not renewed, each insured person has an automatic discovery period of 180 days (6 months) to notify claims arising from wrongful acts during or before the policy period. A longer discovery period is available under Endorsement E01.

Governing Law and Jurisdiction

This policy is governed by the law of the state or territory of Australia shown in the schedule. The parties submit to the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of that state or territory, and of any court of appeal from those courts.

Disputes and Complaints

Any insured person may raise a complaint directly with us or with AFCA: www.afca.org.au | 1800 931 678.

Privacy

We handle personal information in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).

Insurance Contracts Act — Savings and No Contracting-Out

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts, or modifies, or purports to exclude, restrict, or modify, the operation of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where any provision of this policy is inconsistent with that Act, the Act prevails to the extent of the inconsistency.

Without limiting the foregoing, we may not refuse to pay a claim, in whole or in part, by reason of an act or omission of an insured person or the company after this policy was entered into, except to the extent permitted by s 54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) — that is, only to the extent that the act or omission has actually prejudiced us, and then only to the extent of that prejudice.

Premium and Corporations Act s 199B

Section 199B of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) prohibits the company or a related body corporate from paying a premium for insurance against an officer's liability (other than for legal costs) arising from conduct involving a wilful breach of duty in relation to the company, or a contravention of section 182 or 183 of that Act. The schedule may show a portion of the premium as payable other than by the company or a related body corporate, for that part of the cover.

The way the premium is paid does not reduce or condition any insured person's cover under this policy. [WP REVIEW: confirm whether this policy needs a premium allocation, and whether cover for those liabilities should depend on the allocated portion being paid by someone other than the company]

GST

Amounts payable under this policy are calculated without regard to any input tax credit to which the company or an insured person is or would be entitled under A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth), unless the schedule states otherwise. Where a payment under this policy is subject to GST, we will gross up that payment so that the recipient is placed in the same after-tax position as if no GST applied, to the extent the recipient is not entitled to an input tax credit for the corresponding acquisition.

Endorsement E04 — Difference in Conditions

This endorsement applies only where the schedule shows that it applies.

Where it applies, we will also pay an insured person's non-indemnifiable loss arising from a claim covered by Side A Excess Cover (apart from the attachment point), in excess of the DIC retention shown in the schedule, where the underlying D&O insurance does not pay that loss for any of the following reasons:

(a) the underlying insurer is insolvent, is placed into liquidation, or is otherwise unable to pay;

(b) the underlying insurer denies cover for the claim, or avoids or cancels the underlying D&O insurance, because of: (i) a breach of the duty of disclosure or a misrepresentation in the application for the underlying D&O insurance, made without the knowledge of the insured person seeking cover; (ii) a failure by the company (not the insured person) to comply with a condition of the underlying D&O insurance; (iii) an exclusion in the underlying D&O insurance that is not in What Is Not Covered; or (iv) a provision of the underlying D&O insurance that attributes the knowledge or conduct of one insured person to another, innocent insured person; or

(c) the underlying D&O insurance is cancelled or not renewed (other than by the company for non-payment of premium), and the company has been unable to obtain replacement cover on substantially similar terms.

For cover under this endorsement, No Drop-Down does not apply, and this policy does not follow the exclusions of the underlying D&O insurance; only the exclusions in What Is Not Covered apply.

This endorsement does not apply where: (a) the insured person seeking cover personally participated in the conduct that caused the underlying insurer to deny or avoid cover; or (b) the company voluntarily cancelled the underlying D&O insurance and the insured person knew of the cancellation but did not tell us.

When we pay under this endorsement, we also take over the insured person's rights against the underlying insurer, under Subrogation and Recovery.
