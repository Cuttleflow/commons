Directors & Officers Liability Insurance

Cover for directors and officers against claims for wrongful acts in their roles — Side A, Side B and, for securities claims, Side C.

About This Policy

It is structured in three parts:

Side A — pays the director or officer directly when the company cannot or will not indemnify them

Side B — reimburses the company when it has indemnified a director or officer

Side C — covers the company itself, but only for securities claims

Australia has one of the most active securities class action environments in the world. This policy is designed for that environment — including the continuous disclosure regime (now with the knowledge/recklessness/negligence fault element), the Corporations Act 2001 personal duties framework, and increasing regulatory focus on individual accountability.

This policy, together with your schedule and any endorsements, forms your contract of insurance. Words in bold have special meanings — see the Definitions section.

Before this policy was entered into, you were given written notice, as required by section 40(1) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), of the effect of section 40(3) (see the Notify Circumstances obligation).

Who This Policy Is For

This wording is written for businesses and professionals. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement.

Distribution and Regulatory Note

For issuing insurers and distributors — not an operative policy term.

This wording is designed for the directors' and officers' liability of Australian listed companies, and of private companies and not-for-profit organisations. It is a commercial product. Some insureds, such as small private companies, may meet the retail client test under section 761G of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) and regulation 7.1.11 of the Corporations Regulations 2001 (Cth).

Before distributing this wording, the issuing insurer or its authorised distributor should:

  • determine, for each prospective insured, whether the client meets the retail client test;
  • where it does, prepare and maintain a Target Market Determination and Product Disclosure Statement that comply with Part 7.8A and Part 7.9 of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) before offering or issuing this product to that client; and
  • where this wording is intended for wholesale clients only, state that restriction expressly in the issuer's own distribution materials and schedule documentation.

Definitions

Claim

means any of the following first made against an insured during the policy period: (a) any written demand for compensation; (b) any writ, summons, statement of claim, cross-claim, counterclaim, or originating process in any civil, criminal, or administrative proceeding; (c) any Securities claim; (d) any Derivative action; (e) any Investigation; (f) any extradition proceeding or warrant for arrest in connection with a wrongful act; and (g) any formal demand by a liquidator, administrator, or receiver for payment of money or return of property. Two or more claims arising from the same, similar, or related wrongful acts, or from the same or related facts, matters, or circumstances, are treated as a single claim first made when the earliest was first made.

Company

means the entity named in Item 1 of the schedule, and any Subsidiary.

Compensatory damages

means the amount an insured becomes legally liable to pay by way of judgment, award, or settlement (with our consent), including pre-judgment and post-judgment interest, and including the claimant's costs awarded against the insured.

Consolidated assets

means the total assets of the company and its Subsidiaries on a consolidated basis, as shown in the company's most recent audited consolidated financial statements.

Control group

means the company's chief executive officer, chief financial officer, general counsel, head of risk or insurance, and directors, or a person performing an equivalent role.

Continuity date

means the date shown in Item 6 of the schedule, being the date from which continuous D&O coverage has been maintained for the purposes of the Continuous Cover condition. The continuity date does not limit which wrongful acts are covered by this policy — that is the function of the Retroactive date, defined below.

Continuous disclosure breach

means any actual or alleged contravention of ASX Listing Rule 3.1 or section 674 of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), or any equivalent foreign provision, regarding the obligation to disclose material price-sensitive information to the market.

Defence costs

means the reasonable fees, costs, and expenses incurred with our prior written consent to investigate, defend, settle, or appeal a claim, including: (a) legal fees of counsel and solicitors; (b) expert witness fees (including financial, actuarial, and forensic experts); (c) e-discovery costs; (d) class certification study expenses; (e) costs of mediation or alternative dispute resolution; and (f) costs of retaining a public relations consultant in connection with a covered claim (subject to the Public Relations extension). We will not unreasonably withhold or delay consent. Where urgency requires immediate legal representation, the insured person may retain counsel and seek our retrospective consent within 14 days.

Derivative action

means a proceeding brought under Part 2F.1A of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), or any equivalent foreign provision, by or on behalf of a shareholder or member seeking relief on behalf of the company for an alleged wrongful act of an insured person.

E-discovery costs

means the reasonable costs of collecting, processing, hosting, reviewing, and producing electronically stored information in connection with a covered claim.

Class certification study expenses

means the reasonable fees and expenses of an expert retained through defence counsel to prepare a report, analysis, or rebuttal of evidence in connection with a class certification motion, a common fund application, a group costs order, or any equivalent procedural step.

Employee

means any natural person employed by the company under a contract of employment.

Employment claim

means a claim by an Employee, former Employee, or applicant for employment with the company, alleging: (a) wrongful, unfair, or constructive dismissal; (b) discrimination, harassment (including sexual harassment), vilification, or victimisation, including an alleged breach of the positive duty under Part IIB of the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth); (c) breach of an applicable employment-related statute (including the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), state and territory anti-discrimination legislation, and equivalent foreign legislation); (d) negligent provision of, or failure to provide, an employment reference; (e) breach of an employment contract; or (f) retaliation against an Employee for a protected disclosure, exercise of a workplace right, or a complaint of a kind described in (b). The Employer's Liability exclusion states the extent to which this policy responds to an Employment claim, and the E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL) endorsement provides fuller cover where operative.

Endorsement

means a document attached to this policy that changes its standard terms.

Entity cover loss

means Compensatory damages and Defence costs that the company becomes legally liable to pay in connection with a Securities claim.

Excess

means the amount shown in Item 4 of the schedule that the company must pay as the first part of each claim under Side B.

Final adjudication

means a judgment, verdict, or ruling by a court, tribunal, or arbitrator of competent jurisdiction, after the exhaustion of all appeals or the expiry of the time to appeal without an appeal being filed, that finally determines the relevant conduct. A consent order, or a settlement, compromise, or admission of fact that does not include a judicial or arbitral determination of the relevant conduct, is not a final adjudication for the purposes of this policy.

Financial institution

means an entity that is authorised, licensed, or required to be licensed by APRA (including as an ADI, general insurer, life insurer, private health insurer, or RSE licensee), or an equivalent entity regulated by a foreign prudential regulator.

Indemnifiable loss

means Loss that the company is legally permitted to indemnify under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), the company's constitution, any deed of indemnity, access and insurance, or applicable law.

Insured

means: (a) the company (for Side C only); and (b) any insured person (for Side A and Side B).

Insured person

means any natural person who is, was, or during the policy period becomes: (a) a director of the company (including executive, non-executive, independent, alternate, shadow, and de facto directors); (b) the company secretary; (c) an officer of the company within the meaning of section 9 of the Corporations Act 2001 — including the CEO, CFO, COO, CRO, CIO, CLO/general counsel, and any person who participates in making decisions that affect the whole or a substantial part of the company's business; (d) a member of any board committee (audit, risk, remuneration, nomination, ESG, or other); (e) any Employee in a managerial or supervisory role, but only for claims alleging a wrongful act in that capacity; (f) an equivalent person in any Subsidiary; and (g) the lawful spouse, domestic partner, estate, heir, or legal representative of any person described in (a)–(f), but only for claims arising from that person's wrongful acts.

Investigation

means a formal investigation, examination, inquiry, or proceeding commenced by a regulator into the conduct of an insured, including: (a) an investigation under Part 3 of the ASIC Act, or an examination under section 19; (b) an APRA investigation or prudential review; (c) an investigation under the Competition and Consumer Act 2010; (d) an ASX investigation or inquiry, or a Takeovers Panel proceeding; (e) an ATO audit or investigation relating to director penalty notices or personal tax obligations; (f) a coronial inquest where an insured person is a person of interest; (g) a Royal Commission, Parliamentary Committee inquiry, or equivalent public inquiry, into a matter that includes the individual conduct of an insured, whether or not the insured is a named party to the inquiry; and (h) an equivalent investigation by a foreign regulator with jurisdiction. Does not include a routine compliance review or general industry inquiry that does not focus on the individual conduct of an insured.

Limit of liability

means the amounts shown in Item 3 of the schedule.

Loss

means Compensatory damages and Defence costs. For Side A and Side B, loss also includes fines and penalties imposed on an insured person in connection with a wrongful act, but only to the extent the law allows them to be insured.

We do not pay any fine or penalty that the law does not allow to be insured. In particular, we do not pay a penalty imposed under work health and safety law in a State or Territory whose law prohibits insurance of it (including New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland), and we do not pay a criminal fine. [WP REVIEW: confirm the list of jurisdictions that prohibit insurance of WHS penalties; library sources differ on WA and Victoria.]

Loss does not include: (i) fines, penalties, or sanctions not insurable at law; (ii) taxes, levies, or statutory charges; (iii) disgorgement of remuneration or equity-based compensation to which the insured person was not legally entitled; (iv) the cost of complying with an injunction or specific performance order; or (v) matters uninsurable at law in the relevant jurisdiction.

For who may pay the premium for cover of some liabilities, see the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition.

Non-indemnifiable loss

means Loss that the company: (a) is legally prohibited from indemnifying (including under section 199A of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth); see the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition); (b) is financially unable to indemnify (due to insolvency, liquidation, administration, receivership, or scheme of arrangement); or (c) refuses or fails to indemnify (despite being permitted and able to do so).

Policy period

means the period shown in Item 2 of the schedule.

Regulator

means and includes: ASIC, APRA, the ACCC, the ATO, the ASX, the Takeovers Panel, AUSTRAC, the OAIC, SafeWork (and state/territory equivalents), the Clean Energy Regulator, the Foreign Investment Review Board, and any equivalent foreign body.

Retention

means the amount shown in Item 5 of the schedule that the company must bear as the first part of each Securities claim under Side C.

Retroactive date

means the date shown in Item 7 of the schedule. This policy covers wrongful acts occurring on or after the retroactive date only, unless Item 7 states “Full Prior Acts”, in which case no retroactive-date restriction applies to which wrongful acts are covered. If Item 7 is blank, the retroactive date is the continuity date or, if Item 6 is also blank, the start of the policy period.

The retroactive date (not the Continuity date) governs the Prior Known Matters exclusion, the Prior and Pending Litigation exclusion, and the Major Transaction — IPO exclusion. Where Item 7 states “Full Prior Acts”, those three exclusions apply by reference to the continuity date or, if Item 6 is blank, the start of the policy period.

Where two or more wrongful acts are treated as a single claim under the definition of Claim, the retroactive date is applied to that claim by reference to the earliest of those wrongful acts.

Schedule

means the document titled “Schedule” issued with this policy.

Securities claim

means a claim that alleges: (a) a Continuous disclosure breach; (b) misleading or deceptive conduct in connection with the company's securities (under section 1041H of the Corporations Act 2001, section 12DA of the ASIC Act, or the Australian Consumer Law); (c) a breach of the prospectus or disclosure provisions of Chapter 6D of the Corporations Act 2001; (d) market manipulation or insider trading (Part 7.10 of the Corporations Act 2001); (e) a breach of equivalent foreign securities laws; or (f) loss suffered by a shareholder or investor in connection with the purchase, sale, or holding of the company's securities. A securities claim does not include a regulatory investigation or proceeding against the company alone, unless that proceeding is also brought against an insured person.

Subsidiary

means any entity in which the company: (a) holds more than 50% of the voting rights; (b) has the right to appoint a majority of the board; or (c) has effective management control, at the time of the relevant wrongful act. A former subsidiary is covered for wrongful acts that occurred while it was a subsidiary.

We / us / our

means the insurer named in the schedule.

Wrongful act

means any actual or alleged act, error, omission, misstatement, misleading statement, breach of duty, breach of trust, breach of fiduciary obligation, or breach of any statutory or regulatory obligation, committed by an insured in their capacity as a director, officer, or manager of the company.

What Is Covered

Side A — Non-Indemnifiable Loss

Personal Liability — Direct Payment

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act, and the claim results in non-indemnifiable loss, we will pay the insured person directly.

The company has no right to access, direct, assign, or benefit from Side A payments. Side A exists exclusively for the protection of individual directors and officers.

When Non-Indemnifiable Loss Arises

Non-indemnifiable loss arises when the company:

(a) Is legally prohibited from indemnifying. Section 199A of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) limits the liabilities and legal costs for which a company may indemnify its officers. See the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition. Loss the company may not lawfully indemnify is non-indemnifiable loss.

(b) Is financially unable to indemnify. The company is insolvent, in liquidation, administration, receivership, voluntary administration, or a deed of company arrangement.

(c) Refuses to indemnify. The company is permitted and able, but refuses or fails to pay.

No Excess on Side A

No excess or deductible applies to Side A, except the DIC retention shown in the schedule for the E08 Side A Standalone / Excess DIC endorsement, which applies only to loss paid under that endorsement because the primary D&O policy does not respond. We will never require an insured person to pay out of pocket before we respond to Side A loss that is not within that retention.

Side B — Company Reimbursement

Reimbursement of the Company

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act, and the company has indemnified or is obligated to indemnify the insured person for indemnifiable loss, we will reimburse the company.

Side B Excess

The company must pay the excess shown in Item 4 of the schedule for each claim under Side B. The excess applies to the combined total of compensatory damages and defence costs.

Side C — Entity Securities Claims

Securities Claims Against the Company

If a securities claim is first made against the company during the policy period, we will pay the company's entity cover loss.

Side C covers only securities claims. The company's broader management liability exposure (employment, regulatory, statutory) is not covered under Side C — see the Employer's Liability exclusion and the E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL) endorsement for cover relating to claims by employees.

Side C Retention

The company must bear the retention shown in Item 5 for each securities claim under Side C. The retention applies to entity cover loss only — it does not apply to an insured person's personal loss arising from the same securities claim (which is covered under Side A or Side B).

Extensions of Cover

Defence and Investigation

Advancement of Defence Costs

We will advance defence costs as they are incurred, without waiting for the final outcome of a claim. We will never require an insured person to self-fund their defence. We advance defence costs in the same way in civil-penalty and criminal proceedings.

An insured person must repay advanced defence costs to the extent that:

(a) it is later established that the claim, or part of it, is not covered, by Final adjudication or by an allocation under the Allocation provision;

(b) a Final adjudication, or a court's finding, establishes conduct that the Dishonesty and Fraud exclusion or the Personal Profit and Illegal Remuneration exclusion excludes, or that the law does not allow to be covered (see the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition); or

(c) the company later indemnifies the insured person for the same defence costs, to the extent of the indemnity received, so that there is no double recovery.

Where the claim involves conduct excluded under the Dishonesty and Fraud exclusion or the Personal Profit and Illegal Remuneration exclusion, repayment is required only after Final adjudication (as defined in the definition of Final adjudication), consistent with the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition.

Investigation Costs

We will pay defence costs incurred by an insured in responding to an investigation, even before a claim has been made. This includes an investigation that is a Royal Commission, Parliamentary Committee inquiry, or equivalent public inquiry within limb (g) of the definition of Investigation — for every insured, not only where the E03 Financial Institutions endorsement is operative.

For the company (Side C), investigation costs are covered only where the investigation relates to matters that could give rise to a securities claim.

Pre-Investigation Advisory Costs

If an insured person receives a written request or notice to attend an interview, examination, or hearing by a regulator — and the request does not yet constitute a formal investigation — we will pay the insured person's reasonable legal costs in obtaining advice and preparing a response.

Class Certification Study Expenses and E-Discovery Costs

In any securities claim or representative proceeding, we will pay class certification study expenses and e-discovery costs as part of defence costs, without a separate sub-limit.

Cooperation With Regulators — Legal Representation

If a regulator requires an insured person to attend an interview, produce documents, or give evidence, we will pay the costs of independent legal representation for the insured person at that interview or proceeding — even if the insured person is attending as a witness rather than a target.

Personal Protection

Retired Directors and Officers

If an insured person ceases to hold office during or before the policy period (for any reason other than disqualification by final court order), this policy continues to cover claims first made against them for 84 months after the end of the policy period, at no additional premium.

Reconciliation with the Discovery Period condition and the E06 Extended Discovery Period endorsement: if this policy is not renewed, or is cancelled, before that person's 84-month period under this clause has ended, the longer of (a) the remainder of that person's 84-month period under this clause, and (b) any discovery period applicable to the policy generally under the Discovery Period condition or a purchased extension under the E06 Extended Discovery Period endorsement, applies to that person. The shorter period does not curtail the longer one.

New Directors and Officers

Any person who becomes an insured person during the policy period is automatically covered from the date they take office.

Spouse, Estate, and Jointly Held Assets

We will cover the lawful spouse, domestic partner, estate, heir, or legal representative of an insured person for claims arising from the insured person's wrongful acts.

We will also cover the personal assets of a spouse or domestic partner that are jointly held with the insured person — including jointly owned real property and joint bank accounts — to the extent those assets are at risk in connection with a claim against the insured person.

Bail and Civil Bond

If an insured person is required to post bail or a civil bond in connection with a covered claim, we will advance the cost.

Preservation of Assets and Living Expenses

If a court order freezes or restrains the personal assets of an insured person in connection with a covered claim, we will pay: (a) their legal costs in seeking to vary or discharge the order; and (b) reasonable living expenses for the insured person and their immediate family during the period of restraint.

Extradition Defence

We will pay defence costs for an insured person resisting extradition in connection with a wrongful act.

Loss of Earnings — Attending Proceedings

$1,000 per day or part-day for an insured person attending court, a tribunal, or a regulatory hearing as a witness or defendant.

Tax Advisory Costs

If an insured person incurs personal tax liability as a direct result of a payment under this policy, we will pay the reasonable costs of tax advice.

Corporate Events

Newly Acquired Subsidiaries

Automatic cover for 90 days from acquisition — provided total assets do not exceed 25% of the company's Consolidated assets and the entity is not a Financial institution or US/Canada-domiciled entity (unless the relevant endorsement is operative). Notify us within 90 days.

If the acquired entity exceeds the 25% threshold, cover is available subject to our prior agreement.

Divested Subsidiaries — Run-Off

If the company divests a subsidiary during the policy period, this policy continues to cover claims arising from pre-divestiture wrongful acts for 72 months.

Change of Control

If any person acquires more than 50% of the company's voting rights during the policy period, this policy continues for the remainder of the policy period — but only for wrongful acts before the change of control. No new cover after the change of control.

We will not cancel this policy following a change of control.

Public Relations

Following a covered claim, we will pay public relations costs to protect or restore the personal reputation of an insured person or the corporate reputation of the company in connection with a securities claim.

Prospectus and Fundraising

We will cover claims arising from any prospectus, product disclosure statement, information memorandum, cleansing notice, or investor presentation issued by the company during the policy period — including claims under Chapter 6D of the Corporations Act 2001.

Subject to the full limit of liability unless the schedule provides a separate prospectus sub-limit.

Reinstatement After Recovery

If we recover amounts from a third party, we will reinstate the limit of liability by the recovered amount (less costs), up to the original limit.

What Is Not Covered

Dishonesty and Fraud

Any claim arising from deliberately dishonest, fraudulent, or criminal conduct — but only after a Final adjudication (including all appeals) establishes that the specific insured personally committed the conduct. Until Final adjudication, we advance defence costs, on the terms in the Advancement of Defence Costs extension. The presumption of innocence applies.

Severability: Applied individually. One person's dishonesty does not affect another's cover.

Personal Profit and Illegal Remuneration

Personal profit or advantage to which the insured person was not legally entitled — but only after Final adjudication so establishes. Includes insider trading or market manipulation profits.

Insured Versus Insured

Claims by one insured against another — except:

(a) claims by a liquidator, administrator, receiver, or equivalent officer;

(b) derivative actions by shareholders;

(c) claims by a whistleblower, meaning a person who makes a disclosure that the law protects as a whistleblower disclosure;

(d) a claim funded, in whole or in part, by a litigation funder, or otherwise funded or actively directed by a person other than the claimant (including by the provision of legal representation, funding of legal costs, or control of the conduct or strategy of the proceeding by that person) — other than a Regulator acting in its regulatory capacity;

(e) an Employment claim by an Employee against an insured person (but not against the company — see the Employer's Liability exclusion);

(f) cross-claims or contribution claims arising from a third-party claim; and

(g) claims by a former insured who no longer holds office.

Prior Known Matters

Claims arising from facts, matters, or circumstances that the specific insured person knew about before the Retroactive date and knew, or ought reasonably to have known, might lead to a claim. For a claim under Side C, the knowledge that counts is the knowledge of a member of the control group (see the definition of Control group). Knowledge assessed individually. Not imputed between insured persons. See the definition of Retroactive date for the date that applies where the schedule states “Full Prior Acts”.

Prior and Pending Litigation

Claims arising from proceedings pending on or before the Retroactive date, or from substantially the same facts. See the definition of Retroactive date for the date that applies where the schedule states “Full Prior Acts”.

Bodily Injury and Property Damage

Death, bodily injury, or physical damage to tangible property — except: (a) mental injury or emotional distress in a Securities claim or Employment claim; (b) defence costs in WHS proceedings; and (c) defence costs in coronial inquests.

Professional Services

Errors or omissions in professional advice or services to third-party clients. Does not apply to governance, oversight, or supervision of the company's professional services business.

Product Liability

Design, manufacture, supply, sale, or distribution of tangible products.

Pollution

Pollution or environmental contamination — except: (a) defence costs in environmental proceedings; and (b) an insured person's personal liability for environmental clean-up, to the extent the claim alleges a failure of board-level oversight of the company's environmental compliance or risk management systems, as distinct from the insured person's own day-to-day operational control of the polluting activity. For example: an allegation that the board failed to implement an adequate environmental management system falls within this carve-back; an allegation that the insured person personally directed or carried out the operational discharge does not.

Nuclear

Loss directly caused by radioactivity, or by radioactive, nuclear, or ionising-radiation material of any kind, including nuclear fuel, nuclear waste, and nuclear weapons material. (See the Sanctions condition for the separate, standalone sanctions payment-limitation clause, which applies to all cover under this policy and is not part of this exclusion.)

US / Canada Jurisdiction

Claims brought or maintained in the US or Canada, or to enforce a US/Canada judgment — unless the E01 United States / Canada Jurisdiction Extension endorsement is operative.

Contractual Liability

Liability assumed under contract that exceeds liability at law — except under a deed of indemnity, access, and insurance for directors or officers.

Employer's Liability

This policy does not cover an Employment claim, except: (a) to the extent cover is preserved for an insured person under paragraph (e) of the Insured Versus Insured exclusion or paragraph (a) of the Bodily Injury and Property Damage exclusion; or (b) to the extent the E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL) endorsement is shown as operative in the schedule, in which case the Relationship to the Employer's Liability Exclusion paragraph of the E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL) endorsement governs the relationship between this exclusion and that endorsement.

This is a management liability / EPL exposure. Broader cover — including cover for the company itself, and cover for third-party discrimination claims — is available only via the E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL) endorsement.

Major Transaction — IPO

Claims arising from the company's initial public offering — unless a separate IPO policy is in place or the IPO occurred before the Retroactive date. Subsequent capital raisings and continuous disclosure obligations post-listing are covered. See the definition of Retroactive date for the date that applies where the schedule states “Full Prior Acts”.

How Much We Will Pay

Limits — Architecture

(a) Each Side has its own per-claim and aggregate limit as shown in Items 3(a)–(f) of the schedule.

(b) The Overall Aggregate (Item 3(g)) is the total most we will pay under this policy, across all Sides combined, in the policy period. It operates as a shared cap: the individual Side aggregates in Items 3(b), (d) and (f) are sub-caps within the Overall Aggregate, not additional to it, and amounts paid or payable under one Side reduce the Overall Aggregate available to the others.

Priority of Payment

If the limit of liability is or may be insufficient — including because the shared Overall Aggregate under paragraph (b) of the Limits — Architecture provision is or may be insufficient — we pay in this order:

First priority: Side A — personal non-indemnifiable loss and defence costs of insured persons.

Second priority: Side B — reimbursement of the company for indemnifiable loss.

Third priority: Side C — entity cover loss for securities claims.

Loss paid under an endorsement sub-limit (for example the E12 Outside Directorship Liability (ODL) endorsement or the E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL) endorsement) ranks with the Side under which that loss would otherwise be paid, and is part of the Overall Aggregate, not in addition to it.

Within a priority, we pay claims in the order in which they are finally agreed or determined, and we pay defence costs as they are incurred. If we pay loss in good faith in that order, we are discharged to the extent of each payment, even if a later claim then cannot be paid in full because the limit has been used up. We will not delay a payment at a higher priority because a claim at a lower priority is, or may be, made.

Defence Costs

Defence costs are included within the limit of liability unless the schedule states they are in addition.

Allocation

If a claim involves covered and uncovered matters or parties, we will agree a fair allocation. The allocation is made by reference to the relative legal and financial exposure of the covered and the uncovered matters and parties. If we cannot agree, a senior counsel agreed by both parties (or appointed by the President of the relevant Law Society) will determine allocation.

We advance defence costs for the covered portion pending allocation.

Other Insurance

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy, we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows. This does not apply where the schedule or an endorsement states that this policy is excess of a specified underlying policy, in which case this policy pays only after the limit of that specified policy is exhausted.

Your Obligations

Notify Claims

Tell us in writing as soon as reasonably practicable. For securities claims, within 10 business days. (See the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition for the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) savings clause that applies to this obligation, including section 54.)

Notify Circumstances

Tell us during the policy period of any circumstance that might reasonably give rise to a claim. Later claims arising from notified circumstances are treated as made during this policy period. This clause implements section 40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) — see also the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition.

Examples: Media alleging non-disclosure. Investigation or ASX inquiry about disclosure practices. Significant share price drop following announcements. Shareholders engaging litigation funders. Personal notice from a regulator. Internal investigation into misconduct.

Do Not Admit Liability

No insured should admit liability or settle without our written consent. Consent not unreasonably withheld. (See the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition for the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) savings clause that applies to this obligation, including section 54.)

Side A of this policy operates as an excess layer above the primary D&O insurance identified in the schedule as the “Primary D&O Policy”. Side A responds when the insured person's non-indemnifiable loss exceeds the limit of the Primary D&O Policy. This policy is excess of that specified policy for the purposes of the Other Insurance provision.

DIC Drop-Down

Side A will drop down and respond from the ground up (or from the DIC retention shown in the schedule) for non-indemnifiable loss if the Primary D&O Policy fails to respond because of: (a) insolvency of the primary insurer; (b) aggregate exhaustion of the Primary D&O Policy; (c) avoidance of the Primary D&O Policy on grounds of non-disclosure or misrepresentation (but not where the insured person seeking cover personally caused that avoidance); (d) coverage denial based on a breach of condition by the company (not the insured person); (e) an exclusion in the Primary D&O Policy that does not appear in this policy; or (f) cancellation or non-renewal of the Primary D&O Policy (other than by the company for non-payment).

The DIC retention applies only to loss paid under this drop-down. It does not apply to loss above the limit of the Primary D&O Policy.

Presumptive Indemnification

When deciding whether loss is non-indemnifiable loss, we presume that the company will indemnify the insured person to the extent the law permits and the company is financially able. The presumption does not apply if the company is insolvent, or has refused or failed to indemnify. Uncertainty about whether the company will indemnify does not entitle us to withhold or delay advancement of defence costs once this endorsement responds. [WP REVIEW: confirm that this presumption, and its limits, are workable and consistent with the Non-indemnifiable loss definition.]

Maintenance of the Primary D&O Policy

The company must keep the Primary D&O Policy in force, on the terms and limits shown in the schedule, for the policy period, and tell us promptly if it is cancelled, not renewed, or its limits or terms are reduced. If it is not kept in force, drop-down under paragraphs (a) to (f) above is available only to the extent the Primary D&O Policy would have responded had it been maintained, and only as section 54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows (see the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition).

Non-Avoidance

Side A under this endorsement is non-avoidable on the terms of the Non-Avoidance (Side A) condition.

Limits

The Side A limit under this endorsement is the Side A limit shown in Items 3(a) and 3(b) of the schedule. It is part of, not in addition to, the Overall Aggregate in Item 3(g). Payments under Side A rank first under the Priority of Payment provision.

E12 Outside Directorship Liability (ODL)

Additional Definitions

Outside entity means a corporation, partnership, trust, or not-for-profit organisation (other than the company or a Subsidiary) in which an insured person serves as a director, officer, trustee, or equivalent, at the specific written request of the company.

Outside position means the office or role held by an insured person in an outside entity, as outside director, officer, trustee, or equivalent.

Outside entity insurance means any directors' and officers' liability insurance, or equivalent insurance, maintained by or for the benefit of an outside entity that responds to a claim against an insured person in their outside position.

Insuring Clause

We will pay non-indemnifiable loss and indemnifiable loss (in each case in accordance with Sides A and B of this policy) that an insured person becomes legally liable for, arising from a wrongful act committed in their outside position, provided: (a) the company requested, in writing, that the insured person hold the outside position; and (b) the claim is first made against the insured person during the policy period.

Cover under this endorsement is subject to all the terms, conditions, exclusions, and limits of this policy that would otherwise apply to a claim against an insured person, except as varied by this endorsement.

Sub-limit

Cover under this endorsement is subject to the sub-limit shown in the schedule for Outside Directorship Liability, which is part of — not in addition to — the Side A and Side B aggregates shown in Item 3 of the schedule.

Priority and Excess of Outside Entity Insurance — Double-Excess / Other Insurance Interaction

(a) Cover under this endorsement is excess of, and will not contribute with, any outside entity insurance available to respond to the claim, regardless of whether that outside entity insurance is itself written on a primary or excess basis.

(b) Where the outside entity maintains its own directors' and officers' liability insurance, that insurance responds first. This endorsement responds only to the extent non-indemnifiable loss or indemnifiable loss (as applicable) exceeds, or is not paid because of exhaustion, cancellation, avoidance, or insolvency of the insurer under, that outside entity insurance.

(c) Where the company (rather than the outside entity) has arranged or contributed to insurance covering the insured person's outside position — for example, under a general facility separate from this policy — that insurance also responds before this endorsement, on the same basis as (b).

(d) This clause creates a double-excess position: this endorsement sits excess of both (i) the outside entity's own insurance and (ii) any separate facility the company has arranged for outside positions. The Other Insurance provision is not affected by this clause and continues to apply to insurance other than outside entity insurance.

(e) Advancement of defence costs under the Advancement of Defence Costs extension is not delayed pending exhaustion of rights under outside entity insurance; advancement is made on the terms that already apply to advancement under this policy, subject to the repayment and allocation mechanics in the Advancement of Defence Costs extension and the Allocation provision.

Exclusion — Related For-Profit Entities

This endorsement does not cover claims arising from an outside position in an outside entity in which the company (together with its Subsidiaries) holds, directly or indirectly, more than 20% of the issued capital or an equivalent economic interest, unless the schedule specifically identifies that outside entity as a covered outside entity.

No Extension to the Outside Entity

For the avoidance of doubt, this endorsement does not extend Side C cover to the outside entity, and does not cover a claim by the outside entity against the company.

Notification

The company must notify us in writing of each outside position it requests an insured person to hold, within 90 days of the request being made, where the insured person's cover in that position is intended to be covered under this endorsement.

Schedule variables: Outside Directorship Liability sub-limit; covered outside entities.

E13 Employment Practices Liability (EPL)

Additional Definitions

This endorsement uses the definition of Employment claim. In addition: Third-party discrimination claim means a claim by a customer, client, supplier, or other third party who is not an Employee, alleging discrimination or harassment by an insured person or an Employee acting on behalf of the company, in connection with the company's business.

Insuring Clause — Company and Individual Cover

We will pay: (a) loss that the company becomes legally liable to pay in connection with an Employment claim (extending Side-C-equivalent cover to the company for this purpose only); and (b) non-indemnifiable loss and indemnifiable loss that an insured person becomes legally liable to pay in connection with an Employment claim (under Sides A and B), first made against the company and/or an insured person during the policy period.

Third-Party Discrimination Claims

If shown as operative in the schedule, cover under the Insuring Clause paragraph of this endorsement extends to a Third-party discrimination claim, subject to the sub-limit shown in the schedule for third-party discrimination claims.

Sub-limit and Retention

Cover under this endorsement is subject to: (a) the EPL aggregate sub-limit shown in the schedule (part of, not in addition to, the Overall Aggregate under Item 3(g)); and (b) the EPL retention shown in the schedule, which the company must bear as the first part of each Employment claim, applicable to the company's own loss under paragraph (a) of the Insuring Clause paragraph of this endorsement only — no retention applies to an insured person's non-indemnifiable loss under Side A, consistent with the No Excess on Side A clause.

Relationship to the Employer's Liability Exclusion

This endorsement supersedes the Employer's Liability exclusion to the extent of the cover this endorsement grants. Where this endorsement is not operative, the Employer's Liability exclusion applies, subject to the carve-backs it expressly preserves at paragraph (e) of the Insured Versus Insured exclusion and paragraph (a) of the Bodily Injury and Property Damage exclusion.

Exclusions Specific to This Endorsement

We will not pay: (a) an amount payable under a statutory redundancy, severance, or long-service-leave entitlement, to the extent payable regardless of any wrongful act; (b) a claim for workers' compensation, or for benefits under a workers' compensation, disability, or unemployment compensation law; (c) a civil penalty imposed under the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) or equivalent legislation, to the extent uninsurable at law (consistent with the Loss definition and the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition); and (d) an amount payable as wages, salary, commission, or other compensation earned but unpaid in the ordinary course, other than back pay or front pay awarded as compensatory damages in a covered Employment claim.

Aggregation

Employment claims by multiple claimants arising from the same, similar, or related policy, practice, or course of conduct are treated as a single Employment claim first made when the earliest was first made, consistent with the definition of Claim.
