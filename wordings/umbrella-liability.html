Umbrella Liability Insurance

Umbrella liability for Australian businesses: higher limits above public and products, motor and employers' liability policies, with drop-down when an underlying aggregate is used up and cover above a self-insured retention for liability the underlying policies do not cover.

About This Policy

This is an umbrella liability policy. It has two coverages.

The Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause pays loss above the underlying insurance shown in the schedule, for liability that the underlying insurance covers. It follows the terms of the underlying insurance, except where this policy says otherwise.

The Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause pays loss above a self-insured retention, for liability that no underlying insurance covers, on the terms of this policy alone.

This policy is written on an occurrence basis. It drops down when an aggregate limit of the underlying insurance is reduced or exhausted by payment of loss, as the Retained Limit and Exhaustion clause sets out. It does not drop down for any other reason, including the insolvency of an underlying insurer.

This policy, together with your schedule and any endorsements shown in it, forms your contract of insurance with us. Please read it together with the underlying insurance, and tell us if anything in the schedule is incorrect. Where the schedule and this wording differ, the schedule applies. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

This wording is written for businesses. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement. Definitions

What Is Covered

Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance

We will pay loss in excess of the retained limit for liability that is covered by the underlying insurance, up to the limit of liability.

Under this coverage, this policy follows the terms of the underlying insurance that applies to the loss as they are at the start of the policy period, including its insuring clauses, definitions, exclusions, conditions and claims trigger, subject to the terms of this policy. Where this policy and the underlying insurance differ, the narrower terms apply, except for the limits, the premium, the policy period and any term of this policy that is wider in the Defence Costs clause, the Individual Treatment of Insureds condition or the Your Duty of Disclosure condition.

Where the underlying insurance has a sub-limit for a particular liability, this coverage does not apply above that sub-limit unless the E07 Excess of Underlying Sub-limits endorsement applies.

Coverage B — Umbrella Cover

We will pay loss in excess of the self-insured retention that you become legally liable to pay because of personal injury, property damage or advertising injury that:

  • happens during the policy period within the geographical limits;
  • is caused by an occurrence in connection with the business; and
  • is not covered by any underlying insurance, and would not be covered by it if its limits had not been reduced or exhausted.

This coverage applies on the terms of this policy alone. It does not apply to liability that the underlying insurance covers, even if the underlying insurance does not pay it for a reason set out in the No Drop-down for Other Reasons clause.

Retained Limit and Exhaustion

This policy responds to a loss only once the retained limit has been reached for that loss.

Under the Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause, the retained limit is reached when the full underlying limit of each policy of underlying insurance that applies to the loss has been paid by any one or more of the underlying insurers, you, or any other insurer or person paying on your behalf. A payment counts only if it is a payment of loss that the underlying insurance would cover, disregarding any deductible or excess under it. It does not matter whether the payment was made by way of judgment, award or settlement. A settlement for less than an underlying limit does not prevent the retained limit being reached, as long as the difference is paid by you or on your behalf.

Where an aggregate underlying limit is reduced by payment of loss for personal injury or property damage that happens during the policy period, the retained limit is reduced by the amount of that payment. Where the aggregate underlying limit is exhausted by such payments, this policy responds in place of that underlying insurance for liability it would have covered, subject to the deductible or excess of that underlying insurance, which you bear. In both cases this policy covers nothing wider than the underlying insurance that was reduced or exhausted, and a payment of loss that happened before the start of the policy period does not reduce the retained limit.

Under the Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause, the retained limit is reached when you, or someone on your behalf, have paid the self-insured retention for that occurrence.

No Drop-down for Other Reasons

Apart from the reduction or exhaustion of an aggregate underlying limit described in the Retained Limit and Exhaustion clause, this policy does not drop down to replace any underlying insurance, and does not pay any part of a loss below the retained limit. That applies whatever the reason the underlying insurance does not pay, including:

  • the insolvency, administration or liquidation of an underlying insurer, or its inability to pay;
  • the cancellation, non-renewal, expiry, avoidance or reduction of any underlying insurance;
  • a breach of the underlying insurance by you; or
  • a refusal by an underlying insurer to pay all or part of a loss, for any reason.

In each of these cases you bear the amount below the retained limit that the underlying insurance does not pay.

Defence Costs

We pay defence costs for a claim for loss covered by this policy in addition to the limit of liability, except as this clause says.

Under the Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause, we do not pay defence costs that an underlying insurer pays or must pay. Once the underlying limits that apply to the claim have been exhausted, we pay defence costs as they are incurred. We may take part, at our own cost, in the defence of any claim that is likely to involve this policy.

Under the Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause, we have the right to defend the claim. Defence costs count towards the self-insured retention, and we pay them once the self-insured retention has been paid.

For a claim brought in the USA and Canada, or to enforce a judgment made there, defence costs are part of, and reduce, the limit of liability.

How the Exclusions Apply

Under the Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause, the exclusions of the underlying insurance that applies to the loss also apply, and the exclusions of this policy that apply to both coverages apply even where the underlying insurance would cover the liability. Where the underlying insurance has a write-back, carve-back or sub-limit for a matter excluded by this policy, it does not extend this policy unless this policy or the schedule says so.

The knowledge or conduct of one insured is not imputed to another insured for the purpose of any exclusion, as the Individual Treatment of Insureds condition sets out.

How We Pay

Limit of Liability

The most we will pay for loss from any one occurrence, under both coverages together, is the limit of liability.

The most we will pay in the policy period, in total, is the limit of liability:

  • for all loss arising from your products; and, separately,
  • for all loss under the Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause; and, separately,
  • for all other loss under the Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause to the extent the underlying insurance that applies to it has an aggregate limit.

Each aggregate is reduced by every payment of loss counted in it. Defence costs paid in addition under the Defence Costs clause do not reduce the limit of liability or any aggregate.

One Occurrence

All personal injury, property damage and advertising injury arising from the same, similar or related causes, or from continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same general conditions, is one occurrence, however many people are injured, things damaged or claims made.

An occurrence that results in personal injury or property damage over more than one policy period is treated as happening when the personal injury or property damage first happens, and the limit of liability, retained limit and terms of the policy period in which it first happens apply to all of it.

Which Coverage Applies

A loss is paid under one coverage only. The Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause applies to liability that any underlying insurance covers, or would cover but for the reduction or exhaustion of its limits. The Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause applies only to liability that no underlying insurance covers.

Where one occurrence gives rise to liability under both coverages, each part of the loss is paid under the coverage that applies to it, the limit of liability applies once to the whole occurrence, and you bear the self-insured retention only once for the occurrence.

Self-insured Retention

For each occurrence under the Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause, you bear the self-insured retention, which applies to compensation and defence costs together. We pay only the part of the loss that exceeds it. The self-insured retention does not apply under the Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause.

You may not insure the self-insured retention unless the schedule says so. If you cannot pay the self-insured retention because you are insolvent, we will not pay any part of it, but we will pay the loss above it as if you had paid it.

Other Insurance

This policy is written as a true excess layer above the underlying insurance. It is excess of, and does not contribute with, the underlying insurance, and pays only after the retained limit is reached.

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy that is not underlying insurance, this policy is excess of that other insurance, unless the other insurance is written specifically to be excess of this policy. Where the other insurance is written on the same basis as this policy, we contribute with the other insurer in the proportion that our limit of liability bears to the total limits.

Recoveries

Any amount recovered from a third party in respect of a loss paid under this policy and the underlying insurance, after deducting the reasonable costs of the recovery, is applied first to any layer above this policy, then to this policy, then to the underlying insurance, and lastly to you, including for the self-insured retention. Each receives no more than it paid. This provision does not apply to the extent that a different order is required by law.

We may take over and conduct, in your name, any right of recovery you have for a loss we pay. You must not give up any right of recovery after an occurrence without our written consent, but you may agree before an occurrence, in a written contract, to waive rights of recovery against another party.

Your Obligations

Maintain the Underlying Insurance

You must keep the underlying insurance in force throughout the policy period, at the limits and on the terms shown in the schedule, except for the reduction or exhaustion of an aggregate underlying limit by payment of loss.

If you do not, this policy still applies as if the underlying insurance had been kept in force, and the retained limit does not change. We are not liable for any loss that the underlying insurance would have paid had it been kept in force.

Tell Us About Occurrences and Claims

You must tell us as soon as reasonably practicable when you become aware of:

  • an occurrence or claim for which the loss, including reserves for defence costs, is likely to exceed half of the underlying limit for any one occurrence;
  • an occurrence that results in death, brain or spinal injury, amputation, serious burns or loss of sight; or
  • an occurrence or claim that may involve the Coverage B — Umbrella Cover clause.

A notice given to an underlying insurer under its terms is notice to us as long as we also receive a copy within a reasonable time. Notice of circumstances that may give rise to a claim has the effect provided in s.40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Cooperate With Us

You must give us the information, documents and help that we reasonably ask for about any occurrence or claim that may involve this policy. We may take part, at our own cost, in the investigation, defence and settlement of any such claim, and you must keep us informed of significant developments in it.

Settlement Consent

You must not admit liability for, settle, or agree to any judgment on, a claim that is likely to involve this policy, without our written consent. We will not unreasonably withhold or delay our consent.

Tell Us About Changes

You must tell us as soon as reasonably practicable if:

  • any underlying insurance is cancelled, not renewed, changed, or reduced other than by payment of loss;
  • an underlying insurer becomes insolvent or enters administration or liquidation;
  • the number of heavy vehicles you operate increases by more than five; or
  • you begin to export products to, or carry out work in, the USA and Canada.

General Conditions

Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth)

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts or modifies the operation of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where this policy is inconsistent with the Act, the Act prevails.

If this policy would otherwise let us refuse to pay a claim, in whole or in part, because of something you did or did not do after this policy was entered into, section 54 of the Act applies. We may not refuse to pay the claim only because of that act or omission. Our liability is reduced by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced. Where the act or omission could reasonably be regarded as capable of causing or contributing to the loss, we may refuse to pay only the part of the claim that the act or omission caused or contributed to. We may not refuse any part that you prove the act or omission did not cause. This applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation in this policy, not only to the claims conditions.

Your Duty of Disclosure

This is a contract of insurance for a business. It is not a consumer insurance contract. Before you enter into this policy with us, you have the duty of disclosure in section 21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): you must tell us every matter that you know, or that a reasonable person in the circumstances could be expected to know, is relevant to our decision whether to accept the risk of the insurance and, if so, on what terms. You have the same duty before you renew, extend, vary or reinstate this policy. For a company that is an insured, what it knows is what a member of its control group knows (see the definition of Control Group).

You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk we insure you for, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive your duty to tell us about.

If you do not tell us something you are required to, we may reduce the amount we will pay you if you make a claim, or cancel this policy, or both, only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows. If your failure is fraudulent, we may refuse to pay a claim and treat this policy as if it never existed. Disclosure is assessed separately for each insured, as the Individual Treatment of Insureds condition sets out.

Individual Treatment of Insureds

Each insured is treated as having a separate policy for every provision of this policy that depends on what an insured knew, expected, intended, did or failed to do. This includes the Your Duty of Disclosure condition, the definition of Occurrence, and the Known Injury or Damage exclusion.

The knowledge, conduct or failure of one insured is not imputed to any other insured. The one exception is that the knowledge or conduct of a member of the control group of an entity is imputed to that entity. Where the underlying insurance gives an insured greater protection than this condition, this policy gives the same protection to the same insured under the Coverage A — Excess of the Underlying Insurance clause.

Including more than one insured does not increase the limit of liability.

Cancellation

You may cancel this policy at any time by telling us. We refund the premium for the unexpired period of insurance on a pro-rata basis, less any non-refundable government charges.

We may cancel this policy only as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows, by giving you written notice as that Act requires. We refund the premium for the unexpired period on a pro-rata basis.

The cancellation or expiry of any underlying insurance does not by itself cancel this policy.

Sanctions

We do not provide cover, pay any claim or provide any benefit under this policy to the extent that doing so would expose us to any sanction, prohibition or restriction under:

  • the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth), the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or regulations made under either Act; or
  • the trade or economic sanctions laws of the United Kingdom, the United States of America or the European Union, to the extent they apply to us.

This policy is governed by the law of the state or territory of Australia shown in the schedule for this purpose. If none is shown, it is governed by the law that governs the underlying public and products liability insurance at the start of the policy period.

Jurisdiction

You and we submit to the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of the state or territory whose law governs this policy under the Governing Law condition, and the courts that hear appeals from those courts.

GST

Assignment

You cannot transfer this policy to another person or entity without our prior written consent.

A reference in this policy, the schedule, or an endorsement to a clause by its heading (for example, "the Pollution exclusion") is a reference to the clause with that heading in the version of this policy that applies to you. Headings do not otherwise limit or extend the meaning of the clause they introduce.

How Umbrella Cover Works

This guide explains this policy. It does not change its terms.

An umbrella policy does two jobs. Coverage A adds limit above your primary liability policies. Coverage B covers some liabilities your primary policies do not cover at all, above a self-insured retention you pay first.

For example, where your public and products liability policy has a limit of $10 million and this policy has a limit of liability of $10 million:

  • for a public liability loss of $14 million, the primary policy pays $10 million (less its excess, which you pay), and this policy pays $4 million under Coverage A;
  • if your products aggregate of $10 million has already been reduced to $3 million by earlier product claims this year, and a new product claim costs $5 million, the primary policy pays $3 million and this policy pays $2 million under Coverage A, dropping down in place of the exhausted aggregate;
  • for a $200,000 advertising injury claim that your primary policy does not cover, with a self-insured retention of $10,000, you pay the first $10,000 and this policy pays $190,000 under Coverage B.

If your primary insurer cannot pay, for example because it is insolvent, this policy does not move down to replace it.
