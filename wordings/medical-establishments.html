Medical Establishments Insurance

About This Policy

This policy insures healthcare establishments (general practices and medical centres, allied health clinics, day surgeries and procedure centres, private hospitals, and aged care and community health facilities) for the civil liability of the establishment arising from the provision of healthcare services.

It covers:

  • the company's own civil liability for bodily injury or property damage arising from its healthcare operations;
  • the company's vicarious liability for the acts, errors and omissions of its employees, allied health practitioners, volunteers, students and locum tenens;
  • the company's vicarious liability for the acts, errors and omissions of medical practitioners, including visiting medical officers, practising at or for the company;
  • premises liability while healthcare services are being provided;
  • administrative and system errors: care-plan failures, medication management and dispensing errors, inadequate supervision, and triage and referral failures; and
  • the costs of responding to inquiries, investigations, and disciplinary and regulatory proceedings.

Medical practitioners. This policy never covers the personal civil liability of a medical practitioner. We will not pay the civil liability of a medical practitioner for any act, error or omission in the provision of healthcare services in their capacity as a medical practitioner. Every medical practitioner practising at or for the company must hold their own medical indemnity insurance that complies with the Medical Indemnity (Prudential Supervision and Product Standards) Act 2003 (Cth). What this policy does cover is the company's own liability, including its vicarious liability and its non-delegable duty of care, arising from a medical practitioner's conduct. See the Civil Liability for Healthcare Operations clause and the Vicarious Liability for Employees, Practitioners and Contracted Doctors clause.

Allied health practitioners. This policy covers an allied health practitioner only while acting for or on behalf of the company. It is not written to be, and does not claim to be, the professional indemnity insurance that the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law and a National Board's registration standard require of a registered practitioner for their own practice. It has no unlimited retroactive cover, and its run-off cover belongs to the company, not to the practitioner. Each practitioner should check with their National Board whether this policy satisfies the standard that applies to them. Claims made and notified. This policy covers only claims first made against you during the policy period and notified to us during the policy period. It does not cover claims first made after the policy period ends, even if the treatment or incident occurred during the policy period, subject to your statutory rights under s.40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), which the Notification of Circumstances clause explains. Before you enter into this contract, your insurer or its distributor must give you written notice of the effect of s.40(3), as s.40(1) of that Act requires.

This policy, together with your schedule and any endorsements, forms your contract of insurance with us. Please read all documents together and tell us if anything is incorrect. We enter into this contract, and each of us must act towards the other, with the utmost good faith, as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

This wording is written for businesses and professionals. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement. What Is Covered

Civil Liability for Healthcare Operations

If a claim is first made against you during the policy period and notified to us during the policy period, and the claim arises from your healthcare operations conducted on or after the retroactive date within the geographical limits, we will pay the loss you become legally liable to pay, and your defence costs, for any actual or alleged:

  • (i) death, bodily injury, mental injury, illness or disease of any person arising from the provision of, or failure to provide, healthcare services;
  • (ii) physical loss of or damage to tangible property (including resulting loss of use) arising from your healthcare operations;
  • (iii) breach of a duty of care, including a non-delegable duty of care, owed by the company in connection with healthcare services;
  • (iv) administrative or system error in the conduct of healthcare operations, including failures of care planning, clinical handover, triage, referral, recall, test-result follow-up, patient identification, credentialing, rostering or supervision, and errors in the storage, management, prescribing systems or dispensing of medication;
  • (v) liability of the company as occupier of a facility for bodily injury or property damage occurring while healthcare services are being provided or in connection with the conduct of healthcare operations;
  • (vi) breach of privacy or confidentiality in connection with healthcare services (see also the Privacy Complaints and Data Breach Notification Costs extension and the Serious Invasion of Privacy extension);
  • (vii) unintentional defamation committed in the course of providing healthcare services; and
  • (viii) any other civil liability arising from your healthcare operations,

unless excluded under the What Is Not Covered section.

Vicarious Liability for Employees, Practitioners and Contracted Doctors

Cover under the Civil Liability for Healthcare Operations clause includes the company's vicarious liability, and its liability for breach of a non-delegable duty of care, arising from the acts, errors and omissions of:

  • (a) any employee, including nurses, allied health practitioners, care workers and administrative staff;
  • (b) any allied health practitioner, volunteer, student or locum tenens acting for the company; and
  • (c) any medical practitioner, including a visiting medical officer or contracted doctor, while providing healthcare services at or for the company.

We will not pay the civil liability of a medical practitioner for any act, error or omission in the provision of healthcare services in their capacity as a medical practitioner. The cover in this clause is for the company's liability only. A medical practitioner is never an insured under this policy, has no rights under this policy, and must maintain their own compliant medical indemnity insurance.

Where the company is liable, vicariously or for breach of a non-delegable duty of care, for the conduct of a medical practitioner, we pay the company's whole liability for that conduct, even if the medical practitioner is also liable for the same loss. After we pay, we may seek contribution or recovery from the medical practitioner or their insurer (see the Preserve Our Recovery Rights obligation).

Inquiry and Regulatory Defence Costs

If, during the policy period, you are first notified in writing that you are required to attend, produce documents to, or respond to an inquiry, and you notify us during the policy period, we will pay the defence costs of your representation at, and response to, the inquiry, provided the subject matter of the inquiry arises from your healthcare operations conducted on or after the retroactive date. This includes, without limitation:

  • (a) coronial inquests and inquiries;
  • (b) Ahpra and National Board notifications, investigations, performance assessments, panel hearings and tribunal proceedings concerning the company or an insured person;
  • (c) proceedings before the Health Care Complaints Commission (NSW), the Office of the Health Ombudsman (Qld), or any equivalent health complaints entity;
  • (d) Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission investigations, audits, compliance monitoring, enforcement proceedings, and Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS) investigations;
  • (e) Medicare, Professional Services Review, PBS and NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission audits and investigations (see also the Medicare, NDIS and PBS Audits and Employee Fraud extension); and
  • (f) Royal Commissions and boards of inquiry, to the extent your involvement arises from your healthcare operations; and
  • (g) the legal costs of advice about, making or responding to a mandatory notification under the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law, including a notification the company or an insured person is legally required to make about a registered practitioner, and a notification made about the company or an insured person, where the obligation to notify arises, or you first receive notice of the notification, during the policy period.

We do not pay under this clause: fines or penalties (except as provided by the Statutory Liability extension); any compensation ordered by the inquiring body (which may instead be covered under the Civil Liability for Healthcare Operations clause); or your internal costs. This clause pays for the company and for insured persons. It does not pay for the own representation of a visiting medical officer or any other medical practitioner. The sub-limit for this clause in the Sub-limits provision applies.

Notification of Circumstances

If during the policy period you become aware of any fact, matter or circumstance that might reasonably be expected to give rise to a claim against you or an inquiry involving you, and you notify us of it in writing during the policy period, then any claim or inquiry that later arises from those facts, whenever it is made or commenced, will be treated as a claim or inquiry first made during this policy period and notified under this policy.

Your written notice should include: why you think a claim or inquiry might arise; details of the act, error, omission or incident involved; the identity of any potential claimant, patient or resident (if known); and an estimate of the potential value (if possible).

This clause reflects, and does not limit, your rights under s.40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), which provides that where the insured gave notice in writing to the insurer of facts that might give rise to a claim, as soon as was reasonably practicable after becoming aware of them and before the insurance cover expired, the insurer is not relieved of liability in respect of the claim, when made, by reason only that it was made after the cover expired.

Extensions of Cover

The following extensions are included automatically. Each is subject to the terms, conditions and exclusions of this policy and to the limit of liability. Where an extension refers to a sub-limit, the sub-limit in the Sub-limits provision applies unless the schedule states a different amount. Sub-limits are part of, not in addition to, the limit of liability.

Consumer Protection Legislation

We will cover your civil liability for loss and defence costs arising from a claim alleging a breach of the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth) (including the Australian Consumer Law), the Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001 (Cth), or any state or territory fair trading legislation, committed in connection with your healthcare services, including allegations of misleading or deceptive conduct and breach of consumer guarantees. We do not cover criminal liability, or pecuniary penalties except as provided by the Statutory Liability extension.

Privacy Complaints and Data Breach Notification Costs

We will cover:

  • (a) your civil liability for loss and defence costs arising from a claim alleging breach of privacy legislation, breach of confidence, or unauthorised use or disclosure of personal or health information, in connection with your healthcare operations;
  • (b) your defence costs in responding to a complaint, investigation or determination by the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner or a state or territory privacy or health records regulator; and
  • (c) your reasonable first-party costs, incurred with our prior written consent, of assessing and notifying an eligible data breach as required by Part IIIC of the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), including forensic investigation, legal advice on notification obligations, preparation of statements to the Commissioner, and notification to affected individuals. Because the Privacy Act requires you to complete your assessment within 30 days, our consent under this paragraph is deemed given for reasonable costs necessarily incurred to meet that statutory deadline, provided you notify us as soon as reasonably practicable.

Penalties under privacy legislation are covered only to the extent permitted by law and only under the Statutory Liability extension, subject to its sub-limit. The sub-limit for this extension applies.

Serious Invasion of Privacy

We will cover the company's civil liability for loss and defence costs arising from a claim under Schedule 2 of the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) (the statutory cause of action for serious invasions of privacy, inserted by the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (Cth) and in force from 10 June 2025), including the company's vicarious liability for the conduct of an insured person.

We will also cover an insured person (other than a medical practitioner) for such a claim, but only where the invasion of privacy is alleged to have been reckless rather than intentional, and only where the insured person was acting in the course of their duties for the company. We do not cover any person who is found by a court, or who admits, to have intentionally committed a serious invasion of privacy, and if defence costs have been advanced to that person, they must be repaid.

Good Samaritan Acts

We will cover the company's liability, and the liability of any insured person (other than a medical practitioner), arising from emergency medical treatment, first aid or assistance rendered in good faith and without expectation of payment to a person in urgent need at or near the scene of an accident, emergency or sudden illness, including outside a facility. This extension applies even though the treatment falls outside the healthcare services described in the schedule.

Medicare, NDIS and PBS Audits and Employee Fraud

We will pay:

  • (a) your defence costs in responding to an audit, investigation or review by Medicare, Services Australia, the Chief Executive Medicare, the Professional Services Review, the Department of Health and Aged Care, or the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, concerning claiming, billing or payments in connection with your healthcare services; and
  • (b) your defence costs where such an audit or investigation involves alleged fraud or dishonesty by an employee, but only for the benefit of the company and any insured person who did not commit, condone, participate in, or wilfully ignore the fraudulent or dishonest conduct.

We do not pay: repayments of benefits, rebates or payments incorrectly claimed or received; fines or penalties (except as provided by the Statutory Liability extension); or the defence costs of any person who committed or condoned the fraud or dishonesty (and any defence costs advanced to such a person must be repaid). The sub-limit for this extension applies.

Statutory Liability

We will pay your defence costs in defending a prosecution, civil-penalty proceeding or formal enforcement proceeding first brought against the company or an insured person during the policy period and notified to us during the policy period, under any Australian statute in connection with your healthcare operations, including proceedings under work health and safety legislation, privacy legislation, the Aged Care Act (including civil-penalty proceedings brought by the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission), and food safety, radiation safety, and drugs and poisons legislation.

We will also pay fines and civil pecuniary penalties imposed on the company or an insured person in such a proceeding, but only to the extent the law allows them to be insured, and never a criminal fine.

We do not pay any fine or penalty that the law does not allow to be insured. In particular, we do not pay a penalty imposed under work health and safety law in a State or Territory whose law prohibits insurance of it (including New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland), and we do not pay a criminal fine. In those jurisdictions this extension provides defence costs only for work health and safety proceedings.

Penalties are also not covered where the conduct was deliberate, dishonest, fraudulent or reckless, or where payment would be against public policy. Certain penalties, including penalties at the quantum available under the Aged Care Act and privacy legislation, may be uninsurable at law in whole or in part, and we pay only what the law permits.

The Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), sections 199A and 199B condition applies to this extension for an insured person. Where we advance defence costs in a civil-penalty or criminal proceeding against an insured person, we advance them as they are incurred, and the insured person must repay them if a Final adjudication, or a court's finding, establishes conduct that this policy or the law does not allow to be covered.

The sub-limit for this extension applies. This extension does not cover medical practitioners.

Molestation Defence Costs

Despite the Molestation exclusion, we will pay the defence costs of the company, and of any insured person who is not the alleged perpetrator, in defending a claim alleging molestation committed by an employee in connection with your healthcare operations, provided that:

  • (a) no member of the control group knew of, condoned or wilfully ignored the alleged conduct or any propensity of the alleged perpetrator;
  • (b) cover under this extension is limited to defence costs only: we will not pay any loss, compensation, settlement or damages in respect of molestation;
  • (c) the most we will pay under this extension for all claims combined during the policy period is the molestation defence costs sub-limit; and
  • (d) if it is established by judgment, adverse admission or other final adjudication that the molestation occurred and was committed, condoned or wilfully ignored by the person for whose benefit defence costs were paid, that person must repay to us all defence costs we advanced on their behalf.

This extension never covers the alleged perpetrator where the allegation is established, and never covers a medical practitioner.

Newly Acquired Subsidiaries and Facilities

If during the policy period the company acquires or creates a new subsidiary, or acquires or opens a new healthcare facility, the new entity or facility is automatically covered for 60 days from the date of acquisition or opening, provided that:

  • (a) its healthcare activities are the same as or similar to the healthcare services shown in the schedule;

Reason shown on the quote and schedule: Applies because you operate aged care or community care services. A current sanction, more than 50 memory care beds or more than 3 Priority 1 incidents in 12 months refers.
