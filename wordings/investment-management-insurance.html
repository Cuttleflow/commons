Investment Managers Indemnity Insurance

Professional indemnity, directors and officers, and crime cover for investment managers, responsible entities and trustees, with one aggregate.

About This Policy

The Professional Liability section: Professional Liability — claims by investors, clients, and regulators arising from your financial services activities.

The Directors' and Officers' Liability section: Directors' and Officers' Liability — personal liability of your directors and officers for management decisions.

The Crime section: Crime — direct financial loss from employee dishonesty, fraud, and theft.

By integrating these covers, this policy eliminates the gaps and overlaps that arise when you purchase separate PI, D&O, and crime policies — and provides a single aggregate to manage.

This is a claims-made and notified policy (the Professional Liability section and the Directors' and Officers' Liability section) and a discovery policy (the Crime section).

This policy, together with your schedule and any endorsements, forms your contract of insurance. Words in bold have special meanings — see the Definitions section.

Notice under s 40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): if, during the policy period, you give us written notice of facts that might give rise to a claim against you, we cannot later refuse to cover a claim arising from those facts merely because it is made after this policy expires. The Notification of Circumstances clause and the Notify Circumstances — Professional Liability and Directors' and Officers' Liability obligation explain how to give that notice. This notice is given to you before you enter into this contract, as required by s 40(1) of the Act.

Definitions

When the following words appear in bold in this policy, they have the meanings set out below. These definitions apply throughout this policy unless a section expressly states a different meaning for that section.

AFSL

An Australian Financial Services Licence issued under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth).

Claim

Any of the following:

(a) any written demand for compensation against an insured;

(b) any writ, summons, or legal proceeding against an insured;

(c) any regulatory proceeding;

(d) any complaint to AFCA that alleges financial loss;

(e) any derivative action; and

(f) any extradition proceeding against an insured person in connection with a wrongful act.

Claims are related if they arise from the same wrongful act, or from wrongful acts that are the same, similar, or related, or that have a common originating cause, act, omission, or set of facts or circumstances. Related claims are treated as a single claim, first made when the earliest of them was first made.

Company

The named insured and any subsidiary.

Compliance costs

The reasonable costs of engaging external compliance consultants or legal advisors to respond to an ASIC or APRA audit, review, or inquiry relating to your financial services activities — where the audit or inquiry is specifically directed at you (not an industry-wide review). See the Compliance Costs extension.

Defence costs

Reasonable legal costs and expenses incurred with our prior written consent to investigate, defend, settle, or appeal a claim. We will not unreasonably withhold or delay consent.

Derivative action

A proceeding brought under Part 2F.1A of the Corporations Act 2001, or by a unitholder or member of a fund seeking relief on behalf of the fund, for a wrongful act of an insured person.

Discovery

The point at which any responsible person first has reason to believe, on the information then available, that a loss covered by the Crime section has been or is likely to have been sustained, whether or not the amount, cause, or full circumstances of the loss are then known.

Dishonest act

An act committed by an employee with the clear intention to cause you or a fund direct financial loss and to obtain a personal financial benefit beyond their normal compensation.

Employee

Any natural person who works for the company under a contract of employment, including permanent, fixed-term, casual, and part-time employees. It includes:

(a) authorised representatives appointed under your AFSL;

(b) persons seconded to you and working under your direction;

(c) temporary and labour hire workers under your supervision; and

(d) volunteers and interns.

It does not include independent contractors, external auditors, custodians, or prime brokers.

Endorsement

A document attached to this policy that changes its terms.

Excess

The amount shown in the schedule that you must pay as the first part of each claim or loss.

Financial services activities

Any of the following:

(a) the provision of financial product advice (general and personal) under your AFSL;

(b) dealing in financial products under your AFSL;

(c) the operation, management, or administration of a fund;

(d) acting as trustee or responsible entity of a fund;

(e) the provision of custodial or depository services;

(f) portfolio management and investment management;

(g) investor reporting, unit pricing, and fund accounting;

(h) investor communication, marketing, and distribution of financial products;

(i) compliance, risk management, and governance activities in connection with your AFSL; and

(j) any other activity authorised under your AFSL or required by law in connection with your role as investment manager, trustee, or responsible entity.

Fund

Any managed investment scheme, unit trust, superannuation fund, pooled investment vehicle, separately managed account, or other investment structure operated, managed, or administered by you, including the funds named in the schedule and any fund established during the policy period.

Fund assets

The money, securities, financial instruments, digital assets, cryptocurrency, and other assets held in or on behalf of a fund, including assets held by a custodian on behalf of the fund. See the Digital Assets and Cryptocurrency extension for the digital-asset position and Endorsement E13 for enhanced terms.

Insured

(a) the company (for all sections);

(b) any insured person (for all sections); and

(c) any fund, for the Professional Liability section and the Crime section only, to the extent you manage or administer the fund. If a fund has separate insurance of its own that responds to the same loss, this policy applies excess of that other insurance (see the Other Insurance provision); the fund does not cease to be an insured merely because it holds separate insurance of its own.

Insured person

Any natural person who is, was, or during the policy period becomes:

(a) a director (including shadow and de facto directors) of the company;

(b) an officer of the company (including company secretary, CEO, CFO, CIO, chief compliance officer, portfolio managers, and any person who participates in investment or management decisions affecting the whole or substantial part of the business);

(c) a member of any investment committee, compliance committee, risk committee, or equivalent body;

(d) a trustee or member of the board of a fund (where the fund has its own governing body);

(e) an equivalent person in any subsidiary; and

(f) the spouse, domestic partner, estate, or legal representative of any of the above — but only for claims arising from that person's wrongful acts.

Limit of liability

The amounts shown in Item 4 of the schedule.

Loss

For the Professional Liability section and the Directors' and Officers' Liability section: compensatory damages and defence costs that an insured becomes legally liable to pay. For insured persons, loss also includes insurable fines and penalties, to the extent insurable at law.

For the Crime section: the direct financial loss you or a fund sustains as a result of a dishonest act. The Payment to Funds extension (Payment to Funds) states how we pay a fund's direct financial loss.

Loss does not include: taxes; the return of fees, commissions, or management charges to which you were not entitled (but see the Fees and Commissions exclusion); the return of improperly gained profits; amounts uninsurable at law; or indirect or consequential loss (for the Crime section).

Named insured

The entity shown in the schedule.

Policy period

The period shown in the schedule.

Regulatory proceeding

A formal investigation, examination, inquiry, enforcement action, infringement notice, or civil penalty proceeding brought by ASIC, APRA, the ACCC, the ATO, AUSTRAC, or any equivalent foreign regulator in connection with your financial services activities or the affairs of a fund.

Responsible person

Any director, officer, chief compliance officer, chief financial officer, or head of operations of the company.

Retroactive date

The date shown in Item 3 of the schedule. See Item 3 for the sections to which it applies.

Schedule

The document issued with this policy.

Subsidiary

Any entity in which the named insured holds more than 50% of voting rights or effective management control.

Third-party service provider

A custodian, administrator, prime broker, fund accountant, transfer agent, registry, or other service provider engaged by you or a fund under a written contract.

Unauthorised access

Access to a computer system or communication system without the actual or apparently authorised consent of the person entitled to control that access, including access obtained by a person who exceeds the scope of access actually granted to them.

We / us / our

The insurer named in the schedule.

Wrongful act

Any actual or alleged act, error, omission, misstatement, misleading statement, breach of duty (including fiduciary duty), breach of trust, breach of any statutory or regulatory obligation, or neglect, committed by an insured in connection with your financial services activities or in the management of the company.

You / your

The company and, where the context requires, any insured person.

Professional Liability

What Is Covered — Professional Liability

Financial Services Liability

If a claim is first made against an insured during the policy period for a wrongful act in connection with financial services activities performed on or after the retroactive date, we will pay:

(a) any amount the insured becomes legally liable to pay as compensation (including the claimant's costs); and

(b) the insured's defence costs,

for any actual or alleged:

(i) negligence, breach of duty of care, or failure to exercise reasonable care and skill in providing financial services

(ii) negligent misstatement, misrepresentation, or misleading advice about a financial product or investment

(iii) breach of fiduciary duty or trustee duty (including the duty to act in the best interests of investors or members)

(iv) breach of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), including AFSL obligations, the managed investments regime (Chapter 5C), design and distribution obligations (Part 7.8A), and the financial services conduct provisions

(v) breach of the ASIC Act 2001 (Cth), including misleading or deceptive conduct (s 12DA) and unconscionable conduct (s 12CA–12CC)

(vi) breach of the Australian Consumer Law in connection with financial services

(vii) breach of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 (Cth), including the best financial interests duty, the sole purpose test, and prudential standards (where you are a superannuation trustee)

(viii) breach of the Financial Accountability Regime Act 2023 (Cth) (FAR), including accountability obligations of accountable persons

(ix) failure to comply with anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing obligations under the AML/CTF Act 2006 (Cth), to the extent the failure arises from your financial services activities

(x) breach of confidence, misuse of confidential information, or breach of privacy in connection with your financial services activities

(xi) defamation arising from your research reports, investor communications, or marketing materials

(xii) infringement of intellectual property (copyright, trademark) in your publications or marketing

(xiii) any other civil liability arising from your financial services activities

Defence of Regulatory Proceedings

We will pay the insured's defence costs in responding to any regulatory proceeding, even before a claim has been made.

AFCA Complaints

We will pay the insured's defence costs in responding to complaints lodged with the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) in connection with financial services activities. We will also pay any determination, award, or settlement made through the AFCA process.

Notification of Circumstances

If during the policy period an insured becomes aware of any fact, matter, or circumstance that might reasonably be expected to give rise to a claim, and you notify us in writing during the policy period, any later claim arising from those circumstances will be treated as first made during this policy period. This clause implements s 40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) — see the Notify Circumstances — Professional Liability and Directors' and Officers' Liability obligation for the corresponding obligation.

Extensions — Professional Liability

Loss of Documents

Reasonable costs to restore or replace tangible or electronic documents necessary for your financial services activities, lost, damaged, or destroyed while in your possession.

Fund Assets — Third-Party Service Provider Failure

If a third-party service provider (such as a custodian, administrator, or prime broker) fails to perform its obligations under its contract with you or a fund, and a claim is made against you as a result, we will cover you as if you had performed the activity yourself — provided you exercised reasonable care in selecting, appointing, and monitoring the third-party service provider.

This extension does not cover the insolvency of a third-party service provider or the loss of fund assets held by a custodian — unless the loss results from the custodian's fraud or dishonesty (which may be covered under the Crime section).

Mitigation Costs

Reasonable costs incurred with our prior consent to prevent or reduce a loss that would otherwise be covered.

Continuous Cover

If a claim arises from a wrongful act that should have been notified under a previous policy and was not known, and continuous cover has been maintained, we will cover the claim under this policy's terms and limits — see the Continuous Cover condition for how this is capped.

Public Relations

Reasonable costs of a public relations consultant following a covered claim or regulatory proceeding.

Court Attendance

$500 per day for an insured person or employee attending court, a tribunal, or an AFCA hearing as a witness in connection with a covered claim, subject to a maximum of $10,000 in total for any one claim.

Joint Venture and Sub-Advised Funds

If you provide investment management or sub-advisory services to a fund managed by another entity under a sub-advisory or joint venture arrangement, we will cover claims arising from your services under that arrangement as if the fund were your own fund. “Joint venture” means an incorporated or unincorporated arrangement between you and another entity to jointly provide investment management or sub-advisory services, and covers claims arising in connection with the scope of that arrangement.

Compliance Costs

We will pay compliance costs incurred by you in responding to a covered ASIC or APRA audit, review, or inquiry.

Directors' and Officers' Liability

What Is Covered — Directors' and Officers' Liability

Personal Liability — Non-Indemnifiable Loss (Side A)

If a claim is first made against an insured person during the policy period for a wrongful act in their capacity as a director or officer of the company, committed on or after the retroactive date, and the loss is non-indemnifiable (because the company is legally prohibited, financially unable, or refuses to indemnify), we will pay the insured person directly.

No excess applies to Side A.

Company Reimbursement (Side B)

If the company has indemnified an insured person for indemnifiable loss, we will reimburse the company.

The Side B excess shown in the schedule applies.

Extensions — Directors' and Officers' Liability

Retired Directors and Officers

Cover continues for claims against retired insured persons for 72 months after the end of the policy period, at no additional premium.

Spouse, Estate, and Legal Representative

Covered for claims arising from the insured person's wrongful acts, including jointly held assets at risk.

ASIC and APRA Investigation Costs

Defence costs for formal ASIC and APRA investigations into the conduct of an insured person, even before a claim is made.

FAR Proceeding Costs

Defence costs for proceedings under the Financial Accountability Regime Act 2023 (Cth) against an insured person as an accountable person.

If the proceeding results in the forfeiture or clawback of the insured person's deferred remuneration, we will pay the forfeited amount — provided no final adjudication of dishonesty.

Preservation of Assets and Living Expenses

Legal costs to vary or discharge a freezing order; reasonable living expenses during asset restraint.

Bail and Civil Bond

Reasonable costs of a bail bond or civil bond required to secure an insured person's release or to avoid a freezing order, in connection with a covered claim.

Extradition Defence

Defence costs in responding to an extradition proceeding against an insured person, in connection with a wrongful act.

Loss of Earnings

$1,000 per day for an insured person attending court, a tribunal, an AFCA hearing, or a regulatory proceeding at our request, in connection with a covered claim, subject to a maximum of $20,000 in total for any one claim.

Crime

What Is Covered — Crime

Employee Dishonesty

You or a fund's direct financial loss caused by a dishonest act committed by an employee, first discovered during the policy period.

This covers:

(a) theft of money, securities, digital assets, cryptocurrency, or other fund assets;

(b) fraudulent transfers from your accounts or fund accounts;

(c) fraudulent alteration of records, valuations, or unit prices;

(d) deliberate misallocation of trades or investments; and

(e) embezzlement and misappropriation.

Third-Party Service Provider Dishonesty

You or a fund's direct financial loss caused by a dishonest act committed by an employee of a third-party service provider while performing services for you or the fund — provided the act was directed at you or the fund.

Forgery and Alteration

Your loss from forgery of, or fraudulent alteration to, any financial instrument, instruction, or authorisation, resulting in a payment from your accounts or fund accounts.

Computer Crime

Your loss from fraudulent entry, modification, or deletion of data in your computer system by a third party, resulting in the unauthorised transfer of money or fund assets.

Funds Transfer Fraud

Your loss from a fraudulent funds transfer instruction issued by a third party who has gained unauthorised access to your banking or communication systems.

Social Engineering Fraud

Your loss from a funds transfer or delivery of money made in good faith as a result of a fraudulent communication impersonating a genuine person or entity. Whether reasonable verification steps were followed is a factor we take into account in assessing the claim; it is not a condition precedent to cover under this clause.

Extensions — Crime

Forensic Investigation Costs

Reasonable costs of forensic accountants or fraud investigators.

Reconstitution of Records

Costs of reconstituting financial records destroyed to conceal a dishonest act.

Client / Investor Notification Costs

If an employee's dishonest act affects fund investors or your clients, we will pay reasonable costs of notifying affected persons.

Payment to Funds

Where this Section covers a fund's direct financial loss, we will pay you, for the benefit of the affected fund, unless we agree otherwise in writing with you and the fund. The definition of Insured and the Other Insurance provision (Other Insurance) state how this Section applies where the fund has separate insurance of its own.

Single Loss

All direct financial loss under this Section caused by, or involving, one dishonest act, or a series of dishonest acts that are the same, similar, or related, or that involve one or more employees acting alone or in collusion pursuant to a single scheme or course of conduct, is treated as one loss, first discovered when the earliest constituent act was discovered. A single loss that spans more than one policy period is treated as discovered in, and is subject to the limit, excess, and terms of, the policy period in which it was first discovered.

Digital Assets and Cryptocurrency

The definition of Fund assets includes digital assets and cryptocurrency. Loss of digital assets or cryptocurrency is covered under this Section on the same basis as other fund assets. Endorsement E13 (Cryptocurrency / Digital Asset Fund Management) provides enhanced terms — including custody, wallet-security, and valuation provisions — for funds with material digital-asset exposure; it does not remove or narrow the base-form cover in this clause.

What Is Not Covered — All Sections

Dishonesty and Fraud — Professional Liability and Directors' and Officers' Liability

Claims arising from deliberately dishonest, fraudulent, or criminal conduct — but only after final adjudication (a judgment or finding after exhaustion of all appeal rights, or a formal admission by the insured person concerned). Defence costs advanced until then, repayable if there is final adjudication. Severability applies (see the Severability and Non-Imputation condition).

Personal Profit

Personal profit to which the insured person was not entitled — after final adjudication.

Insured Versus Insured

Claims by one insured against another — except: claims by a liquidator/administrator/receiver; derivative actions by unitholders or shareholders; whistleblower claims (within the meaning of Part 9.4AAA of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)); third-party-funded claims; employment claims; and cross-claims.

Prior Known Matters

Claims arising from matters known before the retroactive date that the insured knew might lead to a claim. Knowledge assessed individually (see the Severability and Non-Imputation condition).

Prior and Pending Litigation

Claims arising from proceedings pending before the retroactive date, or from substantially the same facts.

Bodily Injury and Property Damage

Death, bodily injury, or physical damage — except mental injury in employment or regulatory claims, and defence costs in WHS proceedings. WHS fines and penalties are not insured to the extent insuring them is prohibited by law in the relevant State or Territory.

Fund Performance

Any claim alleging that the performance or return of a fund or investment was inadequate, or that an investment decision resulted in a loss — unless the claim also alleges a breach of duty, negligence, misleading conduct, or another wrongful act.

We do not guarantee investment performance. But if you breached your duty of care in making an investment decision, and an investor claims that breach caused their loss, that is covered.

Trading Losses — Crime

Direct investment or trading losses — whether authorised or unauthorised — except where the trading was conducted by an employee committing a dishonest act and qualifies under the Employee Dishonesty clause.

Fees and Commissions

The return of management fees, performance fees, commissions, or other charges that you were not entitled to receive — except where the obligation to return fees arises directly from a covered claim and the return is ordered by a court, tribunal, or AFCA as part of a compensation award.

Product Design and Distribution

Claims alleging that a financial product was inherently defective in its design, or that the product was distributed outside its target market in breach of the design and distribution obligations — except to the extent the claim alleges a wrongful act by an insured in the design, governance, or distribution process.

ASIC stop orders and product intervention orders are covered as regulatory proceedings under the Defence of Regulatory Proceedings clause.

War, Terrorism, Sanctions, Nuclear

War, terrorism, and nuclear risks: We do not cover any loss or liability arising directly or indirectly from (a) war (whether or not declared), invasion, hostilities, rebellion, revolution, insurrection, civil unrest, or the use or usurpation of military power, or any similar belligerent act; (b) any act of terrorism, meaning an act, or threat of an act, involving force, violence, or serious harm to people, property, or systems, committed for a political, religious, ideological, or similar purpose and intended to influence a government or to intimidate the public or a section of it; or (c) radioactivity, or radioactive, nuclear, or ionising-radiation material of any kind. This exclusion applies regardless of any other cause or event contributing concurrently or in any sequence to the loss.

Sanctions: we are not required to provide cover, or to make any payment, to the extent that doing so would expose us to enforcement action under the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth), the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or the trade or economic sanctions, laws, or regulations of the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, or Australia.

Cyber Event

First-party cyber losses (data restoration, business interruption, incident response). Third-party cyber liability is excluded except to the extent it arises from a breach of duty in your financial services activities (e.g., failure to protect client data in the course of providing financial services).

US / Canada Jurisdiction

Claims brought or maintained in the US or Canada — unless Endorsement E01 is operative.

Employer's Liability

Employment-related claims (unfair dismissal, discrimination, harassment). Covered under management liability or EPL (see Endorsement E12).

How Much We Will Pay

Section Limits and Overall Aggregate

Each section has its own per-claim and aggregate limit. The overall aggregate is the most we will pay across all sections combined.

Priority of Payment

First: the Directors' and Officers' Liability section Side A — personal non-indemnifiable loss and defence costs of insured persons.

Second: the Professional Liability section — professional liability claims and regulatory proceedings.

Third: the Directors' and Officers' Liability section Side B — company reimbursement.

Fourth: the Crime section — crime losses.

Defence Costs

Included within the limit of liability unless the schedule states otherwise. Advanced as incurred.

Allocation

If a claim involves covered and uncovered matters or parties, we will agree a fair and reasonable allocation having regard to the relative legal and financial exposure of covered and uncovered matters and parties. If we cannot agree, a Senior Counsel, mutually agreed or, failing agreement, nominated by the President of the relevant State or Territory Bar Association, will determine the allocation, and that determination is binding on the allocation question only.

No Excess on Side A

No excess for insured persons under the Directors' and Officers' Liability section Side A.

Cross-Section Application

If a single event gives rise to losses under more than one section (for example, an employee commits fraud — the Crime section — and investors bring a claim alleging negligent supervision — the Professional Liability section), each section responds to its own type of loss, but the overall aggregate limits the total we pay. Where the same loss is capable of being paid under more than one insuring clause, it is paid once, under the clause with the highest applicable limit, subject to one excess.

Other Insurance

This policy is excess over other valid insurance responding to the same loss, except the Directors' and Officers' Liability section Side A, which is always primary for non-indemnifiable loss. This clause states a priority rule; it does not relieve us of liability merely because other insurance exists (see s 45 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth)).

Multiple Funds — Limit Sharing

Where the schedule (Item 7) lists more than one fund, all funds share the section limits and the overall aggregate stated in Item 4 of the schedule; a loss involving, or a claim by or on behalf of, one fund reduces the limit available to every other fund insured under this policy. Item 7 of the schedule may instead state a per-fund sub-limit, or a stated sharing rule, for one or more named funds, in which case that sub-limit or rule applies to that fund in place of unrestricted sharing.

Your Obligations

Notify Claims — Professional Liability and Directors' and Officers' Liability

Tell us in writing as soon as reasonably practicable after a claim is made.

Notify Circumstances — Professional Liability and Directors' and Officers' Liability

Tell us during the policy period of any circumstance that might reasonably give rise to a claim. Later claims from notified circumstances are treated as made during this policy period. This is the notification referred to in the Notification of Circumstances clause and implements s 40(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Examples specific to investment management:

• An investor or group of investors has complained about fund performance and engaged lawyers.

• ASIC has issued a s912C direction or s33 notice relating to your AFSL obligations.

• A material valuation error has been identified in a fund that may require unit price correction.

• An authorised representative has provided advice outside the scope of their authority.

• A compliance breach report has identified a systemic issue in product distribution.

• APRA has commenced a targeted review of your prudential compliance (for superannuation trustees).

Notify Losses — Crime

Tell us within 60 days of discovery of a crime loss. Report to police.

Do Not Admit Liability

Do not admit liability or settle without our consent. Consent not unreasonably withheld.

Cooperate

Provide all information and assistance. Allow us to associate in defence and settlement.

Duty of Disclosure

The duty of disclosure under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) applies to the named insured as the party proposing this contract; this is a non-consumer contract. As between us and each insured person, disclosure and knowledge are assessed individually as a matter of contract, under the Severability and Non-Imputation condition (Severability and Non-Imputation).

General Conditions

Severability and Non-Imputation

Each insured person is treated as if they had a separate policy. Knowledge and conduct are not attributed between insured persons. This severability applies to every knowledge-based or conduct-based provision of this policy, including the Dishonesty and Fraud — Professional Liability and Directors' and Officers' Liability exclusion, the Personal Profit exclusion, the Prior Known Matters exclusion, the Prior and Pending Litigation exclusion, the Duty of Disclosure obligation, and the Non-Rescindable — Side A condition.

Non-Rescindable — Side A

Side A will not be avoided for any reason, except personal misrepresentation with intent to deceive by the specific insured person seeking cover, and we waive our right under s 28(3) of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) to reduce our liability to any other insured person for that misrepresentation.

Continuous Cover

As per the Continuous Cover extension, capped at the lower of this policy's limit and the prior policy's limit, and otherwise on the terms of whichever of the two policies is less favourable to you.

Extended Reporting Period

Automatic 90 days if this policy is not renewed or is cancelled (the Professional Liability section and the Directors' and Officers' Liability section). The Crime section has its own 60-day post-discovery notification period under the Notify Losses — Crime obligation. Extended periods for the Professional Liability section and the Directors' and Officers' Liability section beyond the automatic 90 days are available for purchase under Endorsement E08, for the durations shown in the schedule when that endorsement is operative.

Cancellation

By you: any time in writing. By us: we may only cancel this policy on grounds permitted by s 60 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where we are entitled to cancel, we will give written notice as required by s 59, and will refund the premium proportionally. Cancellation does not affect a claim or circumstance already notified.

Change of Control

If any person acquires more than 50% of the company's voting rights, this policy continues for the remainder of the policy period for pre-change-of-control wrongful acts only.

Newly Acquired Subsidiaries

Automatic cover for 90 days from acquisition (revenue up to $10M; similar activities). Notify within 90 days.

Governing Law, Jurisdiction, GST, Privacy, Disputes

Governing law: the law of the state or territory shown in Item 10 of the schedule, fixed as at the inception of the policy period.

Jurisdiction: each party submits to the non-exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of that state or territory, and courts competent to hear appeals from them.

GST: amounts payable under this policy are reduced by any input tax credit entitlement under A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth), and are grossed up for GST where required.

Privacy: information is handled in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth).

Disputes: if you are not satisfied with a decision we make, you may access our internal dispute resolution (IDR) process — details are available on request or in our Financial Services Guide. If you remain unsatisfied after IDR, you may lodge a complaint with the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) — www.afca.org.au | 1800 931 678.

Insurance Contracts Act — Savings

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts, or modifies the operation of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) (the Act). Where any provision of this policy is inconsistent with the Act, the Act prevails. Without limiting the foregoing, s 54 of the Act applies to any act or omission by an insured after this policy's inception in relation to a claim or circumstance, so that we may not refuse to pay, or reduce our liability in respect of, a claim by reason of that act or omission except to the extent permitted by s 54.

Fund Wind-Up Run-Off

If a fund named in the schedule (or established during the policy period) winds up, terminates, or is transferred to another responsible entity or trustee during the policy period, cover under the Professional Liability section, the Directors' and Officers' Liability section, and the Crime section continues in respect of that fund, on this policy's terms and within its limits, for wrongful acts and dishonest acts occurring before the wind-up, termination, or transfer, until the earlier of: (a) the expiry of the policy period (including any Extended Reporting Period under the Extended Reporting Period condition or discovery notification period under the Notify Losses — Crime obligation that applies); or (b) the fund obtaining its own separate insurance covering the same period.

This clause does not extend the policy period or create a separate limit for the affected fund; the Multiple Funds — Limit Sharing provision (Multiple Funds — Limit Sharing) continues to apply.
