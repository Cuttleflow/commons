Corporate Travel Insurance

Annual cover for an organisation's business travellers, in Australia and overseas: medical, accident, disruption, luggage, money, liability, kidnap and crisis, and rental vehicle excess, sold through brokers.

About This Policy

This policy covers the people your organisation sends on business trips, within Australia and overseas, for the medical, accident, disruption, property, liability and security events set out in it. It is an annual policy: it covers every business trip that starts during the policy period, so you do not need to arrange insurance for each trip.

You, the organisation named in the schedule, are the policyholder. The people covered are the insured persons described in the Who Is Covered clause. Some benefits are paid to you, and some to the insured person or their estate, as each section states.

This policy is not workers compensation, private health insurance or life insurance, and it does not replace any of them. It cannot pay any medical expense in Australia for which a Medicare benefit is payable (see the Medicare and Health Insurance Laws provision).

This policy, together with your schedule, your proposal and declarations, and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms your contract of insurance with us. Please read them together and tell us if anything is incorrect. Where the schedule and this wording differ, the schedule applies. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

Your duty when you apply. This policy is taken out by an organisation for its business, so it is not a consumer insurance contract. You have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. This includes the trips you expect your insured persons to take, where they will go, and what they will do there. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.

Schedule

(Completed separately for each policy. It contains your specific details.)

The schedule shows:

  • The policyholder's name, ABN and address, and the state or territory whose law governs this policy (see the Governing Law condition)
  • The policy period, with its start and end dates
  • The categories of insured persons included (see the Who Is Covered clause), and the number of insured persons declared
  • The declared trips: the number of return trips expected in the policy period for each destination region, and the expected trip lengths
  • The maximum trip duration (default 90 consecutive days)
  • The territory (default worldwide, subject to the Travel Against Government Advice exclusion and the Sanctions condition)
  • The limit and excess for each section, and the sub-limits where they differ from the defaults stated in this wording
  • The capital sum and weekly benefit for the personal accident section, and the accumulation limit (see the Accumulation Limit provision)
  • Whether the kidnap, extortion and crisis response section applies
  • The 24-hour emergency assistance telephone number
  • The premium, including stamp duty and GST
  • The insurer and its licence
  • The endorsements that apply, by code, with their variables

Definitions

In this policy, the words below have these meanings, wherever they are used and whether or not they start with a capital letter.

Accident

A sudden, unexpected and external event, not intended by the insured person, that happens at an identifiable time and place during a trip.

Accompanying Family Member

The spouse or domestic partner, or a dependent child under 25, of a director, officer or employee who is an insured person, while they travel with that person on a business trip and the schedule shows that accompanying family members are included.

Business Equipment

Laptops, tablets, mobile phones, cameras and presentation equipment that you own or lease, or for which you are legally responsible, and that an insured person carries for business on a trip.

Business Trip

A journey an insured person makes on your business, with your authority, that is more than the distance from their home and usual place of work shown in the schedule (default 100 kilometres) or that involves a flight, together with any leisure travel allowed by the When They Are Covered clause.

DFAT Advice

The travel advice level published on Smartraveller by the Australian Government Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade for a country or region. "Do not travel" is the highest level.

Endorsement

A written change to this policy that we issue and that is shown in the schedule. The available endorsements are listed in the Endorsement Library.

Epidemic or Pandemic

An outbreak of an infectious disease that the World Health Organization, the Australian Government or the government of the country where the insured person is has declared an epidemic, a pandemic or a public health emergency.

Excess

The amount shown in the schedule for a section that is deducted from each claim under that section. Only one excess applies to all claims by one insured person arising from one event under one section.

Home

The usual place of residence in Australia of an insured person, or for an insured person included under Endorsement E10, their usual place of residence in the country shown for them in the schedule.

Immediate Family

The spouse or domestic partner, parent, parent-in-law, step-parent, child, step-child, grandparent, grandchild, brother or sister of an insured person.

Injury

A bodily injury caused solely and directly by an accident, and not by a sickness or a gradual process. Injury includes a resulting medical condition that first shows itself within 12 months of the accident.

Insured Person

A person described in the Who Is Covered clause while they are on a trip.

Kidnap

The unlawful seizure, holding or taking away of an insured person during a trip by someone who demands a ransom from you, the insured person or their immediate family for their release. Detention by a government or a person acting for a government is not kidnap.

Known Event

An event or circumstance that, at the time a trip was booked or this policy started (whichever is later), had been reported in the media, was the subject of a warning by a government or carrier, or that you or the insured person knew, or a reasonable person in your position would have known, could disrupt the trip.

Luggage and Personal Effects

Suitcases and bags and their contents, clothing and articles an insured person wears or carries, and their business equipment, but not money, vehicles, watercraft, or items sent unaccompanied as freight.

Medical Practitioner

A person registered and practising as a medical practitioner or dentist in the country where treatment is given, who is not the insured person, a member of their immediate family, or employed by you.

Medicare-Eligible

An insured person who is eligible for a Medicare benefit under the Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth).

Money

Cash, bank notes, travellers' cheques, prepaid travel cards and corporate cards held by an insured person on a trip for your business.

Permanent Total Disablement

Disablement that has lasted 12 consecutive months and that, in the opinion of a medical practitioner we appoint, will prevent the insured person from ever working again in any occupation for which they are reasonably suited by education, training or experience.

Policy Period

The period shown in the schedule, or any shorter period if the policy is cancelled.

Pre-existing Condition

A condition, illness or injury of an insured person: for which they received treatment, medication or advice from a medical practitioner in the 12 months before a trip starts; or whose symptoms they were aware of, or a reasonable person in their circumstances would have been aware of, in the 3 months before the trip starts. A condition that we have accepted in writing, or that is accepted under Endorsement E01, is not a pre-existing condition.

Rental Vehicle

A car, campervan or motorcycle up to 250cc hired from a licensed rental company under a written agreement for a business trip.

Sickness

An illness or disease that first shows itself during a trip.

Temporary Total Disablement

Disablement caused by injury that wholly prevents the insured person from working in their usual occupation and for which they are under the regular care of a medical practitioner.

Terrorism

An act, including the use or threat of force or violence, by any person or group, whether acting alone or for or with any organisation or government, done for political, religious, ideological or similar purposes, including to influence a government or put the public in fear.

Trip

A business trip, together with any leisure travel this policy allows, from the time the insured person leaves their home or usual place of work to start it until they return there, or the end of the maximum trip duration, whichever is earlier.

Unattended

Left where it is not under the close personal supervision of the insured person or a person they know and trust, or left where it can be taken without the insured person being able to prevent it.

We, Us, Our

The insurer named in the schedule.

You, Your

The organisation named in the schedule as the policyholder, and its subsidiaries in Australia.

Who and What Is Covered

Who Is Covered

The following people are insured persons while they are on a trip:

  • your directors, officers and employees, including those you employ on a fixed-term or casual basis;
  • contractors travelling on your business, if the schedule shows that contractors are included and names them or describes them by category;
  • accompanying family members, if the schedule shows that they are included; and
  • consultants, board advisers and guests travelling on your business at your cost, if Endorsement E08 applies.

You must tell us within 30 days if the number of insured persons increases by more than 20% above the number declared in the schedule. This obligation is subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition.

When They Are Covered

Cover for each insured person starts when they leave their home or usual place of work to begin a trip that starts during the policy period, and ends when they return there.

Each trip is covered for up to the maximum trip duration shown in the schedule (default 90 consecutive days). We may agree in writing to cover a longer trip, or Endorsement E06 may apply. If a trip ends after the policy period, cover continues until the trip ends, subject to the maximum trip duration.

If the insured person's return is delayed by an event covered by this policy, or by the delay of scheduled transport, cover continues until they can reasonably return, for up to 30 days.

Leisure travel by an insured person immediately before or after a business trip is part of the trip for up to the number of days shown in the schedule (default 7 days for each trip, unless Endorsement E02 applies), provided the leisure part is no longer than the business part.

Cover applies anywhere in the territory shown in the schedule, subject to the Travel Against Government Advice exclusion and the Sanctions condition. For a trip within Australia, the medical expenses benefit in the Medical and Evacuation Expenses clause does not apply to a Medicare-eligible insured person.

Medical and Evacuation

Medical and Evacuation Expenses

If an insured person suffers an injury or sickness during a trip, we will pay the reasonable costs, incurred within 12 months of the injury or the sickness first showing itself, of:

  • medical, surgical, hospital, nursing, ambulance and prescribed medication expenses outside Australia;
  • emergency dental treatment to relieve sudden pain, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $5,000 for each insured person);
  • emergency transport to the nearest hospital able to give appropriate treatment, and repatriation to their home when we and the treating medical practitioner agree it is medically necessary, including a medical escort;
  • economy class travel and reasonable accommodation for one member of their immediate family to travel to be with them if they are hospitalised for more than 3 days or are dangerously ill, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $25,000); and
  • if the insured person dies during a trip, the return of their remains to their home, or a funeral or cremation where they died.

The limit is shown in the schedule. Unless the schedule shows a limit, there is no monetary limit, but we pay only reasonable costs.

We must approve medical expenses of more than $1,000, and every evacuation or repatriation, before they are incurred, except in a life-threatening emergency when the insured person or someone with them must contact the emergency assistance service as soon as practicable afterwards. If prior approval was not obtained, the Insurance Contracts Act condition applies and we may reduce our payment only by the extra cost that approval would have avoided.

We decide, after talking to the treating medical practitioner, whether and when evacuation or repatriation is medically necessary, and the method of transport. Where an insured person refuses a repatriation we consider medically appropriate, we will not pay further medical expenses after the date the repatriation could have taken place.

For an insured person whose home is outside Australia, medical expenses incurred in the country of their home are not covered. If such a person is returned to their home country instead of to Australia, ongoing medical expenses there are covered up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $50,000).

Travel Disruption

Cancellation, Curtailment and Delay

We will pay, up to the limit shown in the schedule for each insured person (default $25,000), for the following losses caused by an insured disruption event:

  • cancellation: non-refundable travel and accommodation costs paid or contracted before a trip is cancelled;
  • curtailment: reasonable additional travel and accommodation costs to return the insured person to their home early, and the unused part of prepaid costs that cannot be refunded;
  • replacement traveller: reasonable economy class travel and accommodation costs for one person to replace an insured person who cannot continue a trip, so the business purpose of the trip can be completed, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $10,000 for each event);
  • delay: reasonable meals, accommodation and essential items if the insured person's scheduled transport is delayed for 12 hours or more, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $250 for each full 12 hours, and $2,500 for each trip);
  • missed connection: reasonable additional travel and accommodation costs if the insured person misses a connecting flight because the earlier scheduled flight arrived late, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $2,500 for each insured person); and
  • detention or hijack: a daily allowance of the amount shown in the schedule (default $500 for each full day, and $10,000 for each trip) while the insured person is held by a hijacker, or detained by a government authority for a reason that is not their own act or omission.

An insured disruption event is:

  • the death, serious injury or serious sickness of the insured person, their travelling companion, or a member of their immediate family, certified by a medical practitioner;
  • a natural disaster, fire, flood, riot, strike or civil commotion at the destination, or the destination becoming unsafe because DFAT advice for it is raised after the trip was booked;
  • the insured person's home or usual place of work in Australia being made uninhabitable or unusable by fire, flood or storm;
  • the insured person being summoned for jury service or subpoenaed as a witness; or
  • the cancellation, delay or diversion of scheduled transport for a reason outside the insured person's control.

We do not pay under this section for a loss caused by:

  • your business decisions, including a meeting, contract or event being moved or cancelled, or a change in your plans;
  • the insured person deciding not to travel or to come home, other than because of an insured disruption event;
  • a known event;
  • an amount that is refundable, or for which compensation is payable, by a carrier, travel agent, accommodation provider or under a credit card or other scheme; or
  • an epidemic or pandemic, unless Endorsement E04 applies.

Luggage and Personal Effects

Luggage, Business Equipment and Documents

If an insured person's luggage and personal effects are lost, stolen or accidentally damaged during a trip, we will pay, at our option, the cost of repair, replacement with an item of similar quality, or the value of the item at the time of loss after an allowance for wear and depreciation. The limits for each insured person are shown in the schedule (defaults: $15,000 in total, $5,000 for any one item or set, and $10,000 for business equipment).

We will also pay, for each insured person:

  • the cost of essential replacement clothing and toiletries if their luggage is delayed for more than 12 hours on an outward journey, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $1,500), which we deduct from any later claim for the loss of that luggage;
  • the cost of replacing passports, visas and travel tickets lost or stolen during a trip, including reasonable travel costs to obtain them, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $5,000); and
  • the cost of replacing or recreating your business documents lost or stolen during a trip, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $5,000).

A pair or set of items is treated as one item. Where only part of a pair or set is lost or damaged, we pay for that part only, unless it cannot be replaced or repaired to match.

We do not pay for:

  • wear and tear, gradual deterioration, scratching, mechanical or electrical breakdown, or damage by insects or vermin;
  • an item left unattended in a public place or in a vehicle, or theft from an unlocked vehicle or unlocked accommodation;
  • valuables, cameras or business equipment checked in as luggage, unless they could not lawfully be carried in the cabin;
  • money (see the Money and Business Expenses clause), or sporting equipment while in use, unless Endorsement E03 applies;
  • an item confiscated or detained by customs or another government authority; or
  • an amount the carrier or accommodation provider pays or is liable to pay.

Business Expenses

Money and Business Expenses

We will pay, for each insured person and up to the sub-limits shown in the schedule:

  • money: the loss of money by theft or accidental loss during a trip, including fraudulent use of a corporate card after its theft, less any amount the card issuer bears (default $5,000);
  • emergency business purchases: essential items bought to complete the business purpose of a trip after the loss of luggage or business equipment covered by the Luggage, Business Equipment and Documents clause (default $5,000);
  • event fees: non-refundable conference, exhibition or event registration fees if the insured person cannot attend because of an insured disruption event under the Cancellation, Curtailment and Delay clause (default $10,000); and
  • communication costs: additional telephone and data charges incurred because of an event covered by this policy (default $1,000).

We do not pay for money that is not reported to the police within 24 hours of the loss being discovered, subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition, or for a shortage caused by error or a change in exchange rates.

Personal Liability

Personal Liability

If, during a trip, an insured person becomes legally liable to pay compensation for bodily injury to another person, or loss of or damage to another person's property, caused by an accident, we will pay that compensation and the reasonable legal costs we approve to defend the claim. The most we will pay for all claims arising from one event, including legal costs, is the limit shown in the schedule (default $2,500,000).

The insured person must not admit liability, or offer or agree to pay anything, without our written consent. We may take over the defence and settlement of the claim in the insured person's name. This obligation is subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition.

We do not pay for liability:

  • arising from your business, profession or employment, the work the insured person does on the trip, or the products or advice you supply (these are for your public, products and professional liability policies);
  • for injury to you, to the insured person's immediate family or travelling companions, or to anyone employed by you;
  • for property owned by, or in the care or control of, you, the insured person, their immediate family or their travelling companions, other than temporary accommodation they occupy;
  • arising from owning or using a motor vehicle, aircraft or watercraft (other than a non-motorised watercraft under 6 metres), or a firearm;
  • assumed under a contract, unless it would have existed without the contract;
  • for fines, penalties, or punitive, exemplary or aggravated damages; or
  • arising from a deliberate or reckless act of the insured person.

Kidnap, Extortion and Crisis Response

Kidnap, Extortion and Crisis Response

This section applies only if the schedule shows it applies.

If, during a trip, an insured person is the subject of a kidnap or an extortion demand, or is unlawfully detained, we will pay, up to the limit shown in the schedule for each event (default $250,000), for all of the following together:

  • the fees and expenses of the crisis response consultant we appoint;
  • a ransom or extortion payment made with our prior written consent, where paying it is lawful, including the loss of that payment in transit;
  • the insured person's salary for the period of the kidnap or detention, and reasonable travel, accommodation and medical costs on their release; and
  • counselling for the insured person and their immediate family, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $25,000 for each insured person).

Security evacuation. If, during a trip, DFAT advice for the country or region where an insured person is is raised to "do not travel", or civil unrest, war, a natural disaster or terrorism makes it reasonably necessary to leave, we will pay the reasonable costs of moving the insured person to the nearest safe place or to their home, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $50,000 for each insured person). This benefit applies whether or not the kidnap and crisis response cover above applies.

Terrorism response. We will pay the emergency medical and evacuation costs of an insured person injured in an act of terrorism at or near their location during a trip, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule (default $250,000 for each event). the War exclusion does not apply to this benefit or to the security evacuation benefit.

You must keep this section, and the fact that you hold it, confidential, unless disclosure is required by law or to us. If this obligation is breached, the Insurance Contracts Act condition applies.

We do not pay:

  • for a kidnap or extortion in a country that had a DFAT advice of "do not travel" when the trip began, unless we agreed in writing to cover the trip;
  • for a payment to a person or body that is a listed terrorist organisation, or that would be an offence under the Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth) or would breach the Sanctions condition; or
  • for a kidnap or extortion planned or carried out with the involvement of you, an insured person, or a member of their immediate family.

Rental Vehicle Excess

Rental Vehicle Excess

If a rental vehicle hired by an insured person for a business trip is damaged or stolen while they are in charge of it, we will pay the excess or damage liability they must pay under the rental agreement, up to the limit shown in the schedule for each rental agreement (default $5,000).

The insured person must have taken the rental company's standard comprehensive motor insurance, and must have driven in accordance with the rental agreement and while licensed. We do not pay for damage caused while the vehicle is driven on an unsealed road, other than a road to an accommodation provider or public attraction, or while the driver is affected by alcohol or drugs.

How We Pay

Limits and Excess

The most we will pay for each section is the limit shown in the schedule, and each sub-limit is part of, and not in addition to, the limit of the section in which it appears, unless this wording says otherwise. Where a limit is expressed for each insured person, it applies separately to each insured person.

We deduct the excess shown in the schedule for the section from each claim. Where one event gives rise to claims under more than one section for the same insured person, only the highest of the excesses applies. No excess applies to the medical and evacuation section, the personal accident section or the kidnap, extortion and crisis response section unless the schedule shows one.

One Benefit for One Loss

We will not pay the same loss more than once, even if it falls within more than one section. Where the same amount could be claimed under two sections, we pay it under the section that gives the insured person the greater benefit.

Only one personal accident capital benefit is payable for injuries from one accident, being the highest that applies. Any weekly benefit paid for the same injury is not deducted from a capital benefit.

Accumulation Limit

The most we will pay in personal accident capital benefits for all insured persons injured in one accident, or in a series of accidents arising from one event, is the accumulation limit shown in the schedule (default $5,000,000). If the total of the benefits would be more, we reduce the benefit for each insured person in proportion.

Medicare and Health Insurance Laws

The Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth), the National Health Act 1953 (Cth) and the Private Health Insurance Act 2007 (Cth) do not allow us to pay:

  • any medical expense for which a Medicare benefit is payable, or any gap between the Medicare benefit and the fee charged; or
  • any amount that would make this policy health insurance business under those Acts.

We will not pay any benefit, or part of a benefit, to the extent that paying it would breach those Acts. Emergency transport within Australia that is not a Medicare benefit, and the other benefits of this policy, are not affected.

Other Insurance and Recovery

If a loss under the luggage, business expenses, travel disruption, personal liability or rental vehicle sections is also covered by another policy, you or the insured person must tell us, and we will contribute to the loss in the proportion our limit bears to the total of the limits of all policies covering it, subject to s.45 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). This does not apply to the personal accident capital benefits.

After we pay a claim for an indemnity loss, we may recover the amount from anyone responsible for it, in your name or the insured person's name. You and the insured person must give us reasonable help to do this. We will not recover from an insured person, or from you, except where they acted fraudulently.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the contribution wording is a true other-insurance provision rather than one void under s45(1), given the policy is not expressed as an excess layer.]

Who We Pay

We pay the personal accident capital benefits to the insured person, or if they have died, to their estate. We pay other benefits to whoever incurred the cost, whether you or the insured person, or directly to the provider of the service. Payment to any of them discharges our liability for that amount. All amounts are paid in Australian dollars, at the exchange rate on the date the cost was incurred.

What Is Not Covered

These exclusions apply to every section, in addition to the exclusions in each section.

Pre-existing Conditions

We do not pay any claim under the medical and evacuation, personal accident or travel disruption sections arising from a pre-existing condition of the insured person, unless we have accepted it in writing or Endorsement E01 applies to it.

This exclusion is subject to s.47 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth): we will not rely on it for a condition the insured person was not aware of, and that a reasonable person in their circumstances could not have been expected to be aware of, when the trip started.

Travel Against Government Advice

We do not pay any claim arising in a country or region for which DFAT advice was "do not travel" when the trip, or that part of it, began, unless we agreed in writing before the trip began to cover it. This exclusion does not apply to the security evacuation benefit in the Kidnap, Extortion and Crisis Response clause for an insured person who was already there when the advice was raised.

War

We do not pay any claim caused by war, invasion, act of a foreign enemy, hostilities (whether war is declared or not), civil war, rebellion, revolution or insurrection, except as the Kidnap, Extortion and Crisis Response clause provides. This exclusion does not apply to an insured person who is injured in an act of terrorism they took no part in, or to an insured person in a country when war unexpectedly breaks out there, for 14 days after it breaks out.

Nuclear, Biological and Chemical

We do not pay any claim caused by ionising radiation or contamination by radioactivity from any nuclear fuel, nuclear waste or nuclear weapon, or by the use of a biological or chemical weapon, except as the terrorism response benefit in the Kidnap, Extortion and Crisis Response clause provides, up to its sub-limit.

Epidemic or Pandemic

We do not pay any claim under the travel disruption or business expenses sections caused by an epidemic or pandemic, or by government action taken because of one, unless Endorsement E04 applies. This exclusion does not stop us paying medical, evacuation or repatriation expenses for an insured person who contracts the disease during a trip.

Known Events

We do not pay any claim caused by a known event, except under the medical and evacuation and personal accident sections.

Self-Injury and Intoxication

We do not pay any claim caused by the insured person's intentional self-injury, or by their being under the influence of alcohol or a drug, unless the drug was taken as prescribed by a medical practitioner.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the self-injury exclusion as drafted is consistent with the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth) for injury resulting from a mental illness.]

Illegal Acts

We do not pay any claim caused by the insured person's illegal act, or by their being arrested or detained because of their own illegal act.

Hazardous Activities

We do not pay any claim caused by the insured person taking part in professional sport, motor racing, mountaineering or rock climbing using ropes or guides, skydiving, hang-gliding, paragliding, base jumping, bungee jumping, scuba diving deeper than 30 metres or without a qualified instructor or certification, off-piste snow sports, or flying other than as a fare-paying passenger on a scheduled or licensed charter aircraft, unless Endorsement E03 applies.

Manual Work

We do not pay any claim under the medical and evacuation or personal accident sections caused by the insured person doing manual work on a construction, mining, oil or gas, or engineering site, or working underground or offshore, unless Endorsement E05 applies. Supervising, inspecting or visiting such a site is not manual work.

Pregnancy

We do not pay medical expenses for normal pregnancy or childbirth, or for any pregnancy-related condition after the 26th week of pregnancy, unless the condition is a complication a medical practitioner certifies as unforeseen.

[WP REVIEW: confirm the 26-week threshold is consistent with the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth) and its exemption for insurance based on actuarial or statistical data.]

Elective and Deferrable Treatment

We do not pay for treatment the insured person travelled to obtain, cosmetic or elective treatment, or treatment that a medical practitioner certifies could reasonably be delayed until they return home.

Your Obligations

Emergencies

In a medical emergency, kidnap, extortion or security event, the insured person or someone with them must contact the 24-hour emergency assistance service on the number shown in the schedule as soon as practicable. In a life-threatening emergency, they should first call local emergency services. These obligations are subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition.

Making a Claim

You or the insured person must tell us as soon as reasonably practicable about any event that may lead to a claim, and give us the information and documents we reasonably need to assess it, including original receipts, medical reports, carrier reports and, for theft or loss of property or money, a police report obtained within 24 hours of discovering the loss or as soon as practicable after that.

You and the insured person must take reasonable steps to prevent or reduce loss, and must not dispose of damaged property until we have had a chance to inspect it. These obligations are subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition.

Declarations and Adjustment

The premium is based on the trips you declared for the policy period. Within 60 days after the end of the policy period, you must tell us, if we ask, the number of return trips your insured persons took to each destination region. If the actual number is more than the declared number by more than the tolerance shown in the schedule (default 25%), we will charge, and you must pay, additional premium for the excess trips at the rates used to calculate the premium. We will not reduce the premium if the actual number is lower.

Failure to give a declaration does not affect a claim, subject to the Insurance Contracts Act condition.

General Conditions

Insurance Contracts Act

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts or modifies any right you or an insured person have under the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth).

Where you or an insured person fail to do something this policy requires, or do something it does not allow, after the policy is entered into, s.54 of that Act applies. We will not refuse a claim only because of that act or omission. We may reduce our payment by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced. We may refuse the claim only where the act or omission could reasonably be regarded as capable of causing or contributing to the loss, and then only as that section allows.

Cancellation

You may cancel this policy at any time by telling us in writing. We will refund the premium for the unexpired policy period in proportion.

We may cancel this policy only on a ground allowed by the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), by giving you written notice as s.59 of that Act requires. We will refund the premium for the unexpired policy period in proportion.

Cancellation does not affect cover for a trip that started before the cancellation took effect, until that trip ends.

Renewal

Each renewal is a new contract. Before this policy ends, we will tell you whether we offer to renew it and on what terms, as s.58 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires. We are not obliged to offer renewal.

Fraud

If a claim is made fraudulently, we may refuse to pay it, as s.56 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows.

Sanctions

We will not provide cover, pay any claim or provide any benefit under this policy to the extent that doing so would expose us, or any of our related bodies corporate, to any sanction, prohibition or restriction under the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth), the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or the trade or economic sanctions laws or regulations of the United Nations, the European Union, the United Kingdom or the United States of America.

GST

The premium includes GST where it applies, under A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth). Insurance for travel outside Australia is GST-free to the extent the law allows, and the schedule shows how the premium is divided between overseas and domestic travel. Where you are entitled to an input tax credit for a cost we pay, we pay the cost less that credit. The personal accident benefits are fixed amounts and are not adjusted for GST.

Privacy

We collect, use and disclose personal information about you and insured persons, including health information, to assess your proposal, administer this policy, provide emergency assistance and manage claims, in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and our privacy policy. You confirm you will tell insured persons that you have given us their information and where they can read our privacy policy. Our privacy policy explains how to access and correct information and how to complain about a privacy matter.

Disputes and Complaints

If you or an insured person are unhappy with our service or a decision, contact us at the details in the schedule. We will acknowledge your complaint within one business day and give you our decision within 30 calendar days.

If you are not satisfied with our response, or we have not responded within 30 calendar days, you may take your complaint to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA), a free and independent dispute resolution service, if it falls within AFCA's rules: www.afca.org.au, 1800 931 678, info@afca.org.au.

Governing Law

This policy is governed by the law of the Australian state or territory shown in the schedule. If the schedule does not show one, it is governed by the law of New South Wales.

Jurisdiction

You and we submit to the jurisdiction of the courts of the state or territory whose law governs this policy, and of any court that may hear appeals from them.

Headings and References

A reference to a clause by its heading is a reference to that clause as it appears in this wording. A reference to an endorsement by its code is a reference to that endorsement in the Endorsement Library, and it applies only if the schedule shows it.

How to Make a Claim

  1. In an emergency, call the 24-hour emergency assistance number shown in the schedule.
  2. Otherwise, contact your broker, or contact us at the details shown in the schedule, as soon as reasonably practicable (see the Making a Claim obligation).
  3. Send us the claim form with original receipts, medical certificates, carrier and police reports, and evidence of the trip.
  4. Tell us about any other insurance, carrier compensation or card scheme that may cover the loss.
  5. We will acknowledge your claim, and keep you informed, as the General Insurance Code of Practice requires where the insurer subscribes to it.
