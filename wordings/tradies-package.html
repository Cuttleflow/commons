Tradies Package Insurance

Liability, tools, mobile plant, small-job contract works, tax audit, payment fraud and vehicle downtime cover for sole traders and small trade contractors, with exclusions set by trade.

About This Policy

This policy insures a trade business: a sole trader or a small trade contractor working on other people's homes, buildings, sites and gardens. It covers the business's liability to others, its tools, its mobile plant, the works it carries out under small contracts, a tax audit, payment fraud and the time a work vehicle is off the road.

It has seven sections. Public and Products Liability always applies. The other sections apply only if the schedule shows them.

SectionWhat it coversBasis
Public and Products LiabilityYour legal liability for injury and property damage caused by your work or your productsOccurrence during the policy period
Tools and Portable EquipmentLoss of or damage to your tools, including theft from a locked vehicleLoss or damage during the policy period
Mobile PlantLoss of or damage to mobile plant you own, and to mobile plant you hire inLoss or damage during the policy period
Annual Contract WorksDamage to the works you carry out under a construction contract up to the maximum contract valueDamage before practical completion of a contract that starts during the policy period
Tax AuditProfessional fees for a tax audit of the businessAudits first notified during the policy period
Payment Fraud and CyberMoney lost to a fraudulent payment instruction, and the cost of restoring your email and devicesLoss first discovered during the policy period
Vehicle DowntimeA daily amount while a work vehicle insured under a linked motor policy is off the roadDamage or theft during the policy period

Motor insurance for your vehicles, and personal accident and illness insurance for you, are not part of this policy. They may be arranged as separate policies, which the schedule may show as linked to this policy. This policy does not provide workers compensation insurance or statutory home warranty insurance.

This policy, together with your schedule, your proposal and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms your contract of insurance with us. Please read them together and tell us if anything is incorrect. Where the schedule and this wording differ, the schedule applies. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

Who this policy is for. This wording is written for trade businesses that buy it for the business. Its sections are liability, business property, contract works, tax audit, payment fraud and business interruption covers, which are not among the kinds of insurance the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) and its regulations treat as retail when bought for a small business. Most buyers will therefore be wholesale clients, and this wording is not a Product Disclosure Statement. A linked motor policy or a personal accident and illness policy may be a retail product, and has its own Product Disclosure Statement and Target Market Determination.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that Tools and Portable Equipment cover bought by a sole trader for tools used in the trade is not personal and domestic property insurance under reg 7.1.12 of the Corporations Regulations 2001 (Cth), so that the package stays a wholesale product.]

Who is insured. You are the person, partnership, company or trust named in the schedule. Each section says who else it insures.

Your duty when you apply. Before the policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). You must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows.

If this policy is a consumer insurance contract under that Act, you instead have a duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to us, and the Act sets out what we may do if you do not.

Your trade matters. We set the cover and the endorsements by the trade you carry on. Tell us every trade and activity in the business, and whether you work at height, excavate, do hot work, waterproofing, roof work, gas fitting or tree work, use subcontractors, or do residential building work for which the law requires statutory insurance. Tell us during the policy period when the business changes, as the Tell Us About Changes obligation requires.

What this policy does not replace. This policy does not provide workers compensation insurance, which each state and territory requires an employer to hold through its own scheme. It is not statutory home warranty insurance (also called home building compensation, domestic building insurance or home warranty), which the law requires for residential building work above a value set by each state and territory. It does not insure advice or design you give for a fee, or your vehicles on the road.

Cooling-off period. You may cancel this policy within 21 days of the date it starts, and within 21 days of each renewal, by telling us in writing. If you have not made a claim, we will refund the full premium paid for that period, less any non-refundable government charges we cannot recover. After that, you may still cancel under the Cancellation condition.

Commission and fees. The schedule or the quote shows any commission or fee paid to your broker in connection with this policy.

Financial Claims Scheme. If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, you may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.

Your Schedule

(Completed separately for each policy. It contains your specific details.)

The schedule shows:

  • Your name and ABN, and your main state or territory of operation
  • The trade and the business, and any licence number
  • The policy period, with its start and end dates
  • The sections that apply, with the limit or sum insured and the excess for each
  • The liability limit, and the principals named under the E12 Named Principals endorsement
  • The tools sum insured, the per item limit and any specified items
  • The owned mobile plant and the sum insured of each item, and the hired-in plant limit
  • The maximum contract value for Annual Contract Works
  • The limits of Tax Audit and Payment Fraud and Cyber
  • The daily benefit, the waiting period and the maximum days for Vehicle Downtime, and the linked motor policy and its work vehicles
  • The limits of the extensions, where they differ from the defaults stated in them
  • Any interested party, such as a financier
  • The premium, including stamp duty, any emergency services levy, and GST
  • Any commission or fee paid to your broker
  • The insurer and its licence, and the state or territory whose law governs this policy (see the Governing Law condition)
  • The endorsements that apply, by code, with their variables

Definitions

These words have the meanings below wherever they appear in this policy, with or without capital letters.

Business

The trade shown in the schedule, and work incidental to it, carried on by you in Australia.

Computer System

The computers, phones, tablets and other devices, the software on them, and the email, accounting, banking and cloud accounts that you use in the business.

Construction Contract

An agreement, written or oral, under which you carry out work in the business for a principal for a price, including a contract under which you supply and install materials.

Contract Value

The total price of a construction contract when it is made, including materials, variations agreed before work starts and GST, and the value of materials the principal supplies for you to install.

Contract Works

For each construction contract:

  • the permanent and temporary works you carry out, including materials, fixtures and fittings to be built into them; and
  • materials and supplies for incorporation into the works while at the site, in transit to it within Australia, or stored away from it within Australia, including materials the principal supplies to you.

Contract works do not include your tools, your mobile plant, your vehicles, the existing buildings and structures into or onto which the works are built, or property of the principal other than materials supplied for the works.

Cyber Event

A person accessing or using your computer system without the permission of its owner, a person taking control of one of your email or messaging accounts without your permission, or malicious software in your computer system.

Damage

Sudden and accidental physical loss of, or physical damage to, property, including theft. Damaged has a corresponding meaning.

Employee

A person who works for you under a contract of service or apprenticeship, or who is supplied to you by a labour hire business and works under your direction, while working in the business.

Endorsement

A written change to this policy that we issue and that is shown in the schedule. The available endorsements are listed in the Endorsement Library.

Event

One occurrence, or a series of occurrences arising from one original cause. All damage caused by one storm, flood, bushfire or earthquake within any 72 hour period is one event.

Excess

The amount you pay towards each claim, as shown in the schedule for the section that applies, or as an endorsement sets.

Forcible Entry

Entry to a building, a vehicle, a locked toolbox or a locked compartment of a vehicle, by force or violence that leaves visible marks of the entry, or entry to a locked vehicle using a key or device obtained by theft or deception.

Fraudulent Instruction

An instruction, request or invoice, sent by email, text message, messaging application, letter or telephone, that appears to come from your client, your supplier, your bank or someone in the business, but that was sent by another person intending to deceive you or your client into paying money to an account that person controls.

Hire Agreement

A written agreement under which you hire mobile plant from its owner, including the owner's terms and conditions that form part of it.

Hired-in Plant

Mobile plant you hire from another person under a hire agreement, without an operator, for a period of no more than 12 months, while it is in your care, custody or control.

Linked Motor Policy

A motor vehicle insurance policy for your vehicles that the schedule shows as linked to this policy, for the period it is in force.

Mobile Plant

Machinery and equipment used in the business that is self-propelled, towed, or designed to be lifted onto a vehicle by a crane or ramp, such as excavators, skid steer loaders, elevating work platforms, trailers, generators, compressors and concrete mixers, with their attachments and buckets. Mobile plant does not include tools or a vehicle registered for use on a public road other than a trailer.

Occurrence

An event, including continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same conditions, that results in personal injury or property damage that you did not expect or intend. All personal injury and property damage arising from one source or original cause is one occurrence.

Personal Injury

Bodily injury, death, sickness, disease, disability, shock, fright, mental anguish or mental injury, and false arrest, wrongful detention, malicious prosecution, defamation and invasion of privacy.

Policy Period

The period shown in the schedule, unless this policy is cancelled earlier.

Pollutant

Any solid, liquid, gaseous or thermal irritant or contaminant, including smoke, vapour, soot, fumes, acids, alkalis, chemicals and waste.

Practical Completion

The time when the works under a construction contract are complete except for minor defects that do not prevent their use, or the time when the principal takes possession of the works or starts to use them, whichever happens first.

Principal

The person or organisation for whom you carry out work under a construction contract, including a head contractor where you are a subcontractor.

Property Damage

Physical damage to or destruction of tangible property that you do not own, including the loss of its use, and the loss of use of tangible property that is not physically damaged.

Subcontractor

A person or business, other than an employee, that you engage to carry out part of your work in the business.

Sum Insured

The amount shown in the schedule as the most we pay for the property it applies to. Each sum insured includes GST to the extent the GST and Sums Insured provision states.

Tools

Hand-held and portable tools, instruments and equipment used in the business that one person can carry, with their batteries, chargers, carry cases and accessories, and the laptops, tablets and phones you use in the business, that you own or for which you are responsible. Tools do not include mobile plant, stock, materials for a job, contract works, vehicles or their fixed accessories, or cash.

Vehicle

A motor vehicle, including a motorcycle, and a trailer or caravan.

We, Us, Our

The insurer named in the schedule.

Work Vehicle

A vehicle used in the business that is insured under a linked motor policy and listed on it.

You, Your

The person, partnership, company or trust named in the schedule as the insured. Where more than one is named, each of them. Where the insured is a trustee, the insured in that capacity. Where the insured is a partnership, each partner while acting in the business.

Your Products

Goods, including materials you install and the work you have completed, that you sold, supplied, installed, erected, repaired, altered or serviced in the business, after they have left your possession or control.

Public and Products Liability

This section always applies.

Liability for Injury and Damage

We will pay the amount you become legally liable to pay as compensation for personal injury or property damage that happens during the policy period in Australia and is caused by an occurrence in connection with the business, including by your products.

Who is insured under this section. You, each of your employees while working in the business, and each principal to the extent the Principals extension provides, are insured as if each were named in the schedule.

Limit. The most we pay for all compensation arising from one occurrence is the liability limit shown in the schedule. For personal injury and property damage caused by your products, the liability limit is also the most we pay for all occurrences in the policy period.

Defence costs. In addition to the limit, we pay the reasonable legal costs we incur or approve in defending or settling a claim covered by this section, and interest on a judgment up to the date we pay or offer to pay it.

Settlement. We may take over and conduct the defence or settlement of any claim in your name. We may at any time pay you the limit, less any amount already paid, and then we have no further obligation for that occurrence except for defence costs already incurred.

More than one insured. This section applies to each insured as if a separate policy had been issued to each, but the limit is not increased.

Principals

Where a construction contract requires you to insure a principal, the principal is insured under this section for its liability for personal injury or property damage arising from your work under that contract, to the extent the contract requires and on no wider terms than this policy provides. This extension does not insure the principal for its own negligence beyond what the contract requires you to insure, and does not increase the limit.

Where a principal is named in the schedule under the E12 Named Principals endorsement, that endorsement also applies.

Property in Your Care

The Your Own Property exclusion does not apply to property damage to property of others in your care, custody or control, including the building or room in which you are working. The most we pay under this extension for all property damage arising from one occurrence is $250,000, or the amount shown in the schedule, within the liability limit.

This extension does not pay for damage to the part of any property on which you are working that results from that work, which the Your Own Work exclusion deals with, or for damage to hired-in plant, which Mobile Plant covers if the schedule shows it.

Underground Services

This section covers property damage to underground pipes, cables, mains and other services, and the loss of use of them, caused by your work. Where the schedule shows the E04 Underground Services Conditions endorsement, the conditions in that endorsement apply.

Injury to Your Workers

We do not pay for personal injury to an employee that arises out of their employment, where you are required by law to insure that liability under a workers compensation scheme, or would be but for an exemption.

Liability Under Contracts

We do not pay for liability you have taken on under a contract or agreement, unless you would have had it without the contract or agreement, or the Principals extension covers it, or it arises under a lease of premises, an agreement with a public authority or utility for access to a site, or a hire agreement for hired-in plant.

Your Own Work

We do not pay the cost of repairing, replacing, redoing or completing your own work, or of correcting a defect in it, or the cost of withdrawing or recalling your products. This exclusion applies only to that work or product itself. It does not apply to personal injury, or to property damage to other property, that your work or your products cause.

Example: a tiler's waterproofing fails and water damages the floor and ceiling below. We pay for the damage to the floor and ceiling below. We do not pay to redo the waterproofing or the tiling.

Your Own Property

We do not pay for property damage to property you own or lease, or to property in your care, custody or control, except as the Property in Your Care extension provides.

Vehicles, Aircraft and Watercraft

We do not pay for liability arising from the ownership or use of a vehicle that is registered or required to be registered, or of an aircraft, a drone or a watercraft longer than 8 metres. This exclusion does not apply to:

  • personal injury for which no compulsory third party insurance or statutory scheme responds;
  • property damage caused by a vehicle, or its loading or unloading, beyond the limits of a public road, to the extent no motor vehicle insurance responds; or
  • the use of a vehicle, mobile plant or a trailer as a tool of trade at a work site.

Advice and Design for a Fee

We do not pay for liability arising from advice, design, a specification or a certificate you give for a separate fee. Advice you give without a separate fee, in the course of carrying out your work, is covered.

Gradual Pollution

We do not pay for liability arising from the discharge, dispersal, release or escape of a pollutant, or the cost of removing or cleaning up a pollutant, unless the discharge, dispersal, release or escape is sudden, identifiable, unintended and unexpected, and happens at a specific time and place during the policy period.

Asbestos

We do not pay for liability arising from asbestos, or from any material containing asbestos, including its removal, disturbance, encapsulation or disposal.

Fines and Penalties

We do not pay fines, penalties, or punitive, aggravated or exemplary damages, or any amount the law does not allow to be insured.

Tools and Portable Equipment

This section applies only if the schedule shows it.

Loss of or Damage to Tools

We will pay for damage to your tools that happens during the policy period anywhere in Australia, from any cause that is not excluded, including while the tools are:

  • in or on a vehicle, including in a locked toolbox or compartment fixed to it;
  • at a work site, at your premises or at your home; and
  • being carried, used or stored.

Limits. The most we pay for all damage from one event is the tools sum insured shown in the schedule. The most we pay for any one item that is not specified in the schedule is $5,000, or the per item limit shown in the schedule. A set of tools, such as a socket set, or a tool with its batteries and charger, is one item. Specified items are insured for the value shown for them in the schedule.

How We Settle Tools Claims

We choose whether to repair the damaged item, replace it, or pay you the cost of doing so. We may arrange the replacement through a supplier we nominate, and you may choose to be paid instead.

  • Items less than three years old, measured from the date the item was first bought new. We repair the item, or replace it with a new item of the same kind and specification, or the nearest equivalent available, without any deduction for wear and tear.
  • Items three years old or more. We repair the item, or pay its market value immediately before the damage, being the cost of a similar item of the same age and condition.

For a theft of items worth $5,000 or less in total, where you give us a police report number and evidence of the theft, we will tell you our decision within 2 business days of receiving them, and pay or arrange replacement within 2 business days after that. [WP REVIEW: confirm that the timed settlement promise can be given by the insurer and its claims administrator, and how a missed time is remedied.]

Tools in Vehicles

Theft of tools from a vehicle is covered at any time of day or night, including overnight, where:

  • the vehicle was locked and there was forcible entry to it or to a locked toolbox or compartment fixed to it; or
  • the vehicle itself was stolen; or
  • the tools were secured to the vehicle by a locked cable, chain or rack and the lock or its fixing was broken.

We do not apply a separate excess for tools in a vehicle. Where the same event also damages a work vehicle, the Excess provision applies.

Hire of Replacement Tools

After damage to your tools covered by this section, we pay the reasonable cost of hiring replacement tools so that you can keep working, from the day of the damage until the tools are repaired or replaced or we pay the claim, up to $1,000 for each event, or the amount shown in the schedule. This amount is paid in addition to the tools sum insured, and no excess applies to it.

Employees' Tools

Tools owned by an employee and used in the business are covered as if they were your tools, up to $2,500 for each employee for each event, within the tools sum insured. Where the employee has other insurance for the tools, this extension pays only the amount that insurance does not pay.

Tools Exclusions

Under Tools and Portable Equipment, we do not pay for:

  • wear and tear, rust, corrosion, gradual deterioration, or mechanical or electrical breakdown, unless it results from damage covered by this section;
  • damage to a tool caused by using it, unless the damage results from an external accident, such as a dropped or crushed tool;
  • theft from a vehicle that was unlocked and left unattended, or from an open tray or open trailer, unless the tool was secured as the Tools in Vehicles extension describes;
  • items that cannot be accounted for where there is no evidence of theft or damage, or a shortage found only on a stocktake;
  • tools while lent or hired to someone else, or while in the care of a repairer for repair of an existing fault;
  • batteries, blades, bits and other consumables, unless damaged together with the tool they belong to; or
  • mobile plant, which Mobile Plant covers if the schedule shows it.

Mobile Plant

This section applies only if the schedule shows it.

Plant You Own

We will pay for damage during the policy period to the mobile plant shown in the schedule, which you own or are buying under a finance agreement, anywhere in Australia, from any cause that is not excluded, including while it is:

  • at a work site, at your premises or in storage;
  • being loaded, unloaded or towed, or carried on a vehicle or trailer; and
  • being operated, maintained or left unattended.

The most we pay for an item is the sum insured shown for it in the schedule, and the most we pay for all owned mobile plant for one event is the owned plant sum insured shown in the schedule.

Plant You Hire In

If hired-in plant suffers damage during the policy period while in your care, custody or control, we will pay:

  • the amount you are legally liable to pay its owner for the damage, including liability you have taken on under the hire agreement;
  • the hire charges you must keep paying under the hire agreement while the plant is repaired or replaced, for up to 30 days; and
  • the owner's reasonable recovery, salvage and towing costs that you are liable to pay.

The most we pay for any one item of hired-in plant for one event, including the hire charges and costs, is $50,000, or the hired-in plant limit shown in the schedule.

How We Settle Plant Claims

For mobile plant you own, we choose whether to repair it or pay its market value immediately before the damage, being the cost of a similar item of the same age and condition, up to its sum insured. If the cost of repair is more than its market value, we pay the market value. Where we pay the market value or the sum insured, the damaged item becomes ours if we ask.

For hired-in plant, we pay the amount you are liable to pay under the Plant You Hire In clause, up to the hired-in plant limit.

Mobile Plant Exclusions

Under Mobile Plant, we do not pay for:

  • mechanical or electrical breakdown, wear and tear, rust, corrosion or gradual deterioration, unless it results from other damage covered by this section;
  • damage to tyres, tracks, buckets and other wearing parts, unless the item is damaged in the same event in other ways;
  • damage while the plant is being operated by a person who does not hold a licence or competency the law requires to operate it;
  • damage while the plant is underground, or in or on water, other than while being driven across a creek or ford;
  • damage while the plant is used for demolition, or for work outside the business;
  • damage while the plant is being driven on a public road as a vehicle, which motor insurance covers;
  • plant you hire to others, with or without an operator; or
  • liability under a hire agreement that makes you responsible for damage whatever its cause, to a standard beyond that of a reasonable person caring for the plant, unless you told us about the agreement before the damage.

Annual Contract Works

This section applies only if the schedule shows it.

Damage to the Works

We will pay for damage to the contract works of each construction contract that starts during the policy period, from any cause that is not excluded, while you are carrying out the works and until practical completion, or for 12 months from the start of the works if that is earlier. Cover for a construction contract continues after the policy period ends until that time.

Maximum contract value. A construction contract whose contract value is more than the maximum contract value shown in the schedule, or $250,000 if none is shown, is covered only if you tell us about it before work starts and we agree in writing.

Principals. The principal's interest in the contract works is noted on this policy for each construction contract, and we pay a claim for damage to the contract works to you and the principal as their interests appear.

Limit. The most we pay for damage to the contract works of one construction contract from one event is the contract value of that contract, up to the maximum contract value.

Removal of Debris

After damage to contract works covered by this section, we also pay the reasonable costs of removing debris from the site, demolishing and shoring the damaged contract works, and cleaning up, up to 10% of the claim for the damage, in addition to the limit.

How We Settle Contract Works Claims

We pay the reasonable cost of repairing or reinstating the damaged contract works to the condition they were in immediately before the damage, including the cost of labour and materials at the rates in the construction contract. We do not pay for improvements, or for work that would have been needed in any case to complete the construction contract.

Contract Works Exclusions

Under Annual Contract Works, we do not pay for:

  • the cost of repairing, replacing or redoing any part of the contract works that is defective in design, materials or workmanship. We do pay for damage to other parts of the contract works that the defect causes;
  • wear and tear, rust, corrosion, gradual deterioration, or mechanical or electrical breakdown;
  • damage after practical completion, other than damage you cause while returning to fix defects;
  • loss found only on a stocktake, or that cannot be explained;
  • penalties, liquidated damages, delay costs or loss of profit;
  • damage to existing buildings and structures, which Public and Products Liability may cover as property damage; or
  • damage to contract works caused by a cessation of work for more than 30 consecutive days, unless you protect and secure the site during the cessation.

Tax Audit

This section applies only if the schedule shows it.

Tax Audit Fees

If, during the policy period, the Australian Taxation Office or a state or territory revenue office first notifies you of an audit, review or investigation of the tax affairs of the business, including income tax, GST, PAYG withholding, payroll tax or superannuation guarantee, we will pay the reasonable fees of an accountant, tax agent or lawyer you appoint, with our consent, to deal with it. We will not unreasonably withhold consent.

The most we pay for all audits first notified during the policy period is $50,000, or the tax audit limit shown in the schedule.

Tax Audit Exclusions

Under Tax Audit, we do not pay for:

  • tax, interest, penalties or fines;
  • fees for preparing a return, an activity statement or other work you would have needed in any case;
  • an audit that results from fraud, a false statement or a deliberate failure to lodge by you or someone acting for you; or
  • an audit of which you were notified before the policy period, or which you knew was likely when this policy was entered into.

Payment Fraud and Cyber

This section applies only if the schedule shows it.

Fraudulent Payment Instructions

We will pay:

  • money you pay to another person because you, or someone in the business acting for you, relied in good faith on a fraudulent instruction, such as a supplier's invoice with changed bank details; and
  • money your client pays to another person, instead of to you, because the client relied in good faith on a fraudulent instruction that was sent from your computer system or that used your name, for which the client is not obliged to pay you again,

where the loss is first discovered during the policy period.

Restoring Your Systems

After a cyber event that you first discover during the policy period, we will pay the reasonable costs of an IT specialist you appoint, with our consent, to regain control of your email and other accounts, remove malicious software, and restore your data and software from your most recent backup or other source. We will not unreasonably withhold consent.

Payment Fraud Limits and Discovery

Limit. The most we pay under Payment Fraud and Cyber for all losses and costs first discovered during the policy period is $25,000, or the limit shown in the schedule.

One loss. All losses that arise from the same fraudulent instruction, or from fraudulent instructions sent by the same person or group of people, are one loss, and one excess applies. One loss is discovered when the first part of it is discovered, and is covered by the policy in force at that time only.

Discovery and notice. A loss is discovered when you, a partner or a director first becomes aware of facts that would cause a reasonable person to believe that a loss covered by this section has happened. Tell us as soon as reasonably practicable, and no later than 60 days after the policy period ends.

Checking changed payment details. We ask you to confirm any new or changed bank details by telephoning the person concerned on a number you held before the instruction arrived, before you pay. This is not a condition of cover. If the loss arises from a payment you made without that check, we pay 50% of the loss, unless you show that the check would not have prevented it.

Payment Fraud and Cyber Exclusions

Under Payment Fraud and Cyber, we do not pay for:

  • a loss caused by you, a partner or a director, or by anyone acting with them, acting dishonestly;
  • money lost on investments, loans, cryptocurrency or other digital assets, or through a payment card used without your authority where the card issuer is responsible for it;
  • goods or materials you supplied, or the value of work you did, other than money a client paid to another person as the Fraudulent Payment Instructions clause describes;
  • a ransom or extortion payment;
  • the cost of improving your computer system beyond its condition before the cyber event;
  • a failure or interruption of electricity, telecommunications, internet or other infrastructure not under your control; or
  • a loss or cyber event that you knew about before the policy period.

Vehicle Downtime

This section applies only if the schedule shows it and a linked motor policy is in force.

Daily Downtime Benefit

If a work vehicle cannot be used in the business because of damage or theft that happens during the policy period, and the insurer under the linked motor policy accepts a claim for it, we will pay the daily benefit shown in the schedule ($150 if none is shown) for each full day the work vehicle cannot be used after the waiting period shown in the schedule (48 hours if none is shown).

We pay until the first of these:

  • the work vehicle is repaired and available to you;
  • 14 days after the insurer under the linked motor policy offers to settle the claim as a total loss or a theft;
  • 30 days of benefit for that event, or the number shown in the schedule; or
  • 60 days of benefit in total in the policy period, or the number shown in the schedule.

This benefit is paid in addition to any hire vehicle or other benefit under the linked motor policy. It is paid for one work vehicle at a time for each event, unless the schedule shows otherwise.

Vehicle Downtime Exclusions

Under Vehicle Downtime, we do not pay for:

  • a vehicle that is not a work vehicle when the damage or theft happens;
  • mechanical or electrical breakdown, wear and tear, or servicing;
  • any day of delay caused by you, such as not authorising repairs, not collecting the repaired vehicle, or not giving the motor insurer what it reasonably asks for; or
  • any day after the linked motor policy ends, unless the damage or theft happened while it was in force.

How Claims Are Paid

These provisions apply to every section.

Excess

You pay the excess shown in the schedule for each claim, for the section that applies. An endorsement may set a special excess for the matter it describes; that excess replaces the section's excess for that matter.

One event, one excess. If one event gives rise to claims under more than one section of this policy, we deduct only the highest excess that applies.

Linked policies. If one event also gives rise to a claim under a linked motor policy or another policy issued by us and shown in the schedule as linked to this policy, and the excess under that policy is deducted, we do not deduct an excess under this policy for that event, unless ours is higher, in which case we deduct only the difference.

No excess applies to Vehicle Downtime; the waiting period applies instead.

One Loss, One Section

A loss is paid under one section only, even if more than one section could apply. Where it could be paid under more than one, we pay it under the section that gives you the higher amount, except that:

  • tools are paid under Tools and Portable Equipment whether they are damaged at a site or in a vehicle;
  • damage to contract works is paid under Annual Contract Works where the schedule shows it, and not as property damage; and
  • damage to hired-in plant is paid under Mobile Plant where the schedule shows it, and not as property damage.

GST and Sums Insured

Where you are entitled to an input tax credit for the GST on the cost of a repair or replacement, we pay the claim net of that credit. Each sum insured and limit in this policy includes GST to the extent you are not entitled to an input tax credit for it.

Other Insurance

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy, we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows. This does not apply where the schedule or an endorsement states that this policy is excess of a specified underlying policy, in which case this policy pays only after the limit of that specified policy is exhausted.

Where a principal's project insurance or a contract works policy arranged by a principal covers the same contract works, this policy pays only the amount that policy does not pay, including its excess where the construction contract makes you bear it.

Who We Pay

We pay you, or as you direct. Where an interested party is shown in the schedule, we pay as the schedule and the E13 Interested Party endorsement require. For contract works, we pay as the Damage to the Works clause provides.

General Exclusions

Nuclear

Terrorism

We do not pay for any loss, damage, liability or cost arising from an act of terrorism, meaning an act, including the use of force or violence or the threat of it, by any person or group, committed for political, religious, ideological or similar purposes, including the intention to influence a government or to put the public in fear. Cyber Events

Deliberate Acts

How to Make a Claim

  1. Make the site and the property safe, and prevent further damage.
  2. Contact your broker, or contact us directly at the details shown in the schedule.
  3. Tell the police about any theft, malicious damage or fraud, and get a report number. For payment fraud, also tell your bank and the bank that received the money straight away.
  4. Tell us what happened and when, and give us photographs, receipts, quotes and invoices.
  5. For a claim against you, send us the demand or court documents as soon as you receive them, and do not admit liability.
  6. For vehicle downtime, give us the claim number under the linked motor policy and the repairer's dates.
  7. We will acknowledge your claim, keep you informed, and tell you our decision as the General Insurance Code of Practice requires where the insurer subscribes to it.
