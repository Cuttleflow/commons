Decennial Liability Insurance (New South Wales)

Ten-year, first-resort cover for relevant defects in the building elements of the common property of a new NSW residential apartment building, taken out by the developer for the owners corporation under s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW). Not yet accepted by the Secretary as a form of decennial liability insurance.

About This Policy

This policy is decennial liability insurance for a new residential apartment building in New South Wales. The developer of the building takes it out. It insures the owners corporation of the strata scheme for the building. It pays to rectify a relevant defect in a building element of the common property for ten years from the date of the occupation certificate.

What it coversWho it protectsHow long it lasts
The cost of rectifying a relevant defect in a building element of the common property, and damage the defect causesThe owners corporation, for the benefit of the lot owners from time to timeTen years from the date of the occupation certificate

First resort. The owners corporation does not have to sue the builder or the developer, or show that either is insolvent, before it claims. It needs to show that there is a relevant defect, not who caused it. The First Resort clause explains this.

Strict liability. We pay whether or not anyone was at fault.

No cancellation. Once the certificate of currency for the occupation certificate application has been issued, nobody can cancel this policy. The No Cancellation condition explains this.

The law that requires this policy. This policy is intended to be decennial liability insurance under the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW). The Decennial Liability Insurance Under the Strata Schemes Management Act condition explains how the policy is read so that it meets that Act.

This policy, together with the schedule, the developer's proposal and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms the contract of insurance. Where the schedule and this wording differ, the schedule applies, except where the Decennial Liability Insurance Under the Strata Schemes Management Act condition requires otherwise. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

Version and Document History

This document is version-controlled. The version and date shown on the cover page and in the footer of each page govern this text. The version stated in the schedule when this policy is issued applies for the whole period of insurance and for any relevant defect notified during it, unless the schedule or an endorsement states otherwise.

Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where the insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

The statutory basis of this policy. This wording is drafted to meet the requirements for decennial liability insurance in s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW) and cll.55A to 55C of the Strata Schemes Management Regulation 2016 (NSW). A policy is decennial liability insurance for the purposes of that Act only if the Secretary accepts its form. The Secretary has not accepted this wording. An insurer that adopts it must obtain that acceptance before a developer relies on the policy instead of lodging a building bond.

Who this policy is for. The developer of a new residential apartment building in New South Wales takes out this policy for the benefit of the owners corporation. The developer's duty of disclosure. Before the developer enters into this policy, s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires it to tell us every matter it knows, or that a reasonable person in its circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure and on what terms. It does not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. Only the developer owes this duty. The owners corporation does not, and a failure by the developer does not reduce the owners corporation's cover: the Protection of the Owners Corporation condition explains this.

The owners corporation's right to claim. The owners corporation is the insured under this policy and may claim under it directly. It does not need the developer's consent or help to claim.

Terrorism. Where this policy is an eligible insurance contract under the Terrorism Insurance Act 2003 (Cth), the Terrorism exclusion has no effect for loss arising from a declared terrorist incident, and our liability may be reduced by any reduction percentage declared under that Act.

Financial Claims Scheme. If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, the owners corporation may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.

Schedule

(Completed separately for each policy. It contains the details of the building and the cover.)

The schedule shows:

  • The developer and its ABN
  • The insured: the owners corporation of the strata scheme for the building, identified by the building's address and, once registered, the strata plan number
  • The building: its address, lot and deposited plan numbers, the number of residential lots and of any other lots, the number of storeys, the development consent and construction certificate numbers, and the building class
  • The builder and its licence number, and the technical inspector
  • The final contract value, the sum insured and whether indexation applies, with its annual rate
  • The date of the occupation certificate and the period of insurance
  • The late notification period (default 90 days)
  • The excess for each relevant defect
  • The make-safe limit (default $100,000) and the investigation costs limit (default $50,000)
  • The time limits for deciding and settling claims, where they are shorter than those in the When We Decide and Pay provision
  • The premium, the deposit premium, the balance premium, stamp duty and GST
  • Any commission or fee paid to the developer's broker
  • The insurer and its licence
  • The endorsements that apply, by code, with their variables
  • The statement that the policy is issued as decennial liability insurance under s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW), and the date the Secretary accepted the form of this wording

Definitions

In this policy, the words below have these meanings wherever they appear, whatever their capitalisation. The singular includes the plural.

Building

The building or buildings at the address shown in the schedule, constructed by the building work, that form the parcel of the strata scheme.

Building Element

Has the meaning given in s.6 of the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 (NSW) as in force when this policy takes effect. At the date of this wording it means:

  • the fire safety systems for the building;
  • waterproofing;
  • an internal or external load-bearing component of the building that is essential to the stability of the building or a part of it, including foundations and footings, floors, walls, roofs, columns and beams;
  • a component of the building that is part of the building enclosure;
  • those aspects of the mechanical, plumbing and electrical services for the building that are required to achieve compliance with the Building Code of Australia; and
  • anything else the regulations under that Act prescribe.

For this policy, building element also includes lifts, escalators and other vertical transportation products that form part of the common property.

Building Work

The work to design and construct the building, carried out under the construction contract, the development consent and the construction certificate shown in the schedule.

Builder

The person shown in the schedule as the builder, and any person who replaces it to carry out the building work.

Certificate of Currency

The certificate we issue that sets out the matters cl.55C of the Strata Schemes Management Regulation 2016 (NSW) requires, for the developer to give to the Secretary before applying for the occupation certificate.

Claim

A written request to us to pay for, or arrange, the rectification of a relevant defect under this policy.

Common Property

The common property of the strata scheme, within the meaning of the Strata Schemes Development Act 2015 (NSW).

Construction Contract

The contract between the developer and the builder for the building work, including its variations.

Contract Value

The total price of the building work under the construction contract, including variations, the value of work and materials the developer supplies or pays for directly, and GST.

Developer

The person shown in the schedule as the developer, being the developer of the strata scheme within the meaning of Part 11 of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW). The developer takes out this policy and pays the premium.

Excess

The amount shown in the schedule that the owners corporation bears for each relevant defect, as the Excess provision sets out.

Final Technical Inspection Report

The report the technical inspector issues when the building work is complete and before the developer applies for the occupation certificate. It records the inspections carried out, confirms that the building work was inspected as the technical inspection plan requires, and lists any outstanding defects.

Lot

A lot in the strata scheme.

Lot Owner

The owner of a lot from time to time.

Occupation Certificate

The occupation certificate issued under the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (NSW) for the building. If more than one occupation certificate is issued for parts of the building, it means the first of them.

Owners Corporation

The owners corporation constituted for the strata scheme when the strata plan for the building is registered.

Period of Insurance

Ten years, starting on the date the occupation certificate is issued, as the When Cover Starts condition sets out.

Rectification Costs

The costs the Rectification Costs provision describes.

Related Defects

Two or more relevant defects that arise from the same or a related cause, including the same design, specification, building product, method of installation or trade. Related defects are treated as one relevant defect.

Relevant Defect

Has the meaning given in s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW) as in force when this policy takes effect. At the date of this wording it means a defect in a building element that is:

  • a failure to comply with the Building Code of Australia, a relevant Australian Standard or the approved plans and specifications, that causes or is likely to cause damage to the building, or a risk of death or serious injury;
  • defective design, workmanship or materials that causes or is likely to cause the building or a part of it to be uninhabitable or unusable for its intended purpose, its destruction or the threat of its collapse;
  • the use of a building product in contravention of the Building Products (Safety) Act 2017 (NSW); or
  • a defect in a lift, escalator or other vertical transportation product that causes or is likely to cause a risk of death or serious injury,

and any other defect the regulations under that Act prescribe.

Strata Scheme

The strata scheme for the building, registered under the Strata Schemes Development Act 2015 (NSW).

Sum Insured

The amount the Sum Insured provision sets out.

Technical Inspection Plan

The document we issue with our offer of insurance that lists the design review, the inspection stages and the hold points for the building work.

Technical Inspector

The person we appoint or approve to review the design and inspect the building work under the technical inspection plan. The schedule names the technical inspector.

We, Us, Our

The insurer named in the schedule.

You, Your

The owners corporation. Before the strata plan for the building is registered, the developer holds this policy for the owners corporation that will be constituted, and a reference to you is to the developer acting for it.

What We Cover

This section sets out the cover. Everything we pay under it, its extensions and the endorsements is paid within the sum insured.

Relevant Defects in the Common Property

We will pay the rectification costs of a relevant defect in a building element of the common property that is first discovered during the period of insurance, and that you tell us about as the Telling Us About Defects obligation requires.

We pay whether or not the relevant defect has caused damage yet. We also pay the cost of repairing physical damage to the building, including the fixtures and finishes in a lot, that the relevant defect causes.

We pay whether or not anyone was at fault, and whether or not the builder, the developer or anyone else is liable, insolvent, deregistered or can be found.

We pay as the How We Pay section sets out.

First Resort

You may claim under this policy without first making a claim, or bringing proceedings, against the builder, the developer, a designer, a certifier, a supplier, a manufacturer or anyone else. You do not need to show that any of them is insolvent, has died, cannot be found or has refused to rectify the defect.

We do not refuse or delay a claim because you have, or may have, rights against anyone else. Our right to recover from others after we pay is set out in the Our Right to Recover From Others condition.

Defects Discovered Near the End of the Period

If you first discover a relevant defect during the period of insurance but tell us about it after the period of insurance ends, we treat it as notified during the period of insurance, as long as you tell us within the late notification period shown in the schedule.

Rectification After the Period Ends

Once you have told us about a relevant defect within the time this policy allows, we continue to pay its rectification costs after the period of insurance ends, until it is rectified. This includes any later-discovered part of the same related defects.

Making the Building Safe

We pay the reasonable cost of urgent temporary work to make the building safe, or to prevent further damage, after a possible relevant defect is discovered. You do not need our agreement before urgent work starts, but you must tell us as soon as reasonably practicable.

If we later decide that the defect is not a relevant defect, we still pay the reasonable cost of urgent work done before we told you our decision, up to the make-safe limit shown in the schedule.

Investigation Costs

We pay the reasonable cost of investigating whether a defect is a relevant defect and how to rectify it, including engineering and building consultants' reports, testing and opening up.

Where the defect is a relevant defect, these costs are part of the rectification costs. Where it is not, we pay the costs we agreed in writing before they were incurred, up to the investigation costs limit shown in the schedule.

Access and Reinstatement

We pay the reasonable cost of removing and reinstating any part of the building, including fixtures and finishes in a lot, that must be removed to reach and rectify a relevant defect.

Professional and Statutory Fees

We pay the reasonable fees of architects, engineers, building consultants, project managers and certifiers, and the fees of public authorities, for designing, approving, supervising and certifying the rectification of a relevant defect.

Compliance With Current Building Law

Where the rectification of a relevant defect must comply with a law or standard in force at the time of rectification that differs from the one that applied to the building work, we pay the additional cost of complying with it, for the part of the building element being rectified.

How We Pay

This section sets out what we pay, how we settle a claim and how long we take.

Rectification Costs

The rectification costs of a relevant defect are the reasonable costs of repairing, replacing or redesigning the building element so that it is free of the relevant defect and complies with the Building Code of Australia and the approved plans, using materials and methods of a quality at least equal to those the approved plans required. They include the costs the Making the Building Safe extension, the Investigation Costs extension, the Access and Reinstatement extension, the Professional and Statutory Fees extension and the Compliance With Current Building Law extension describe, and GST as the GST provision sets out.

How We Settle a Claim

After we accept a claim, we agree a scope of rectification with you. Then, as you and we agree, we either:

  1. arrange and pay for the rectification ourselves, using contractors we appoint, in which case we are responsible for the quality of that work; or
  2. pay you the rectification costs of the agreed scope, as the work is done or as invoices are presented.

We pay a cash amount instead of rectification only if you ask for it and we agree.

If we arrange the rectification and the rectification work is itself defective, we treat the defect in that work as a relevant defect notified on the date it is discovered, for the longer of six years from the completion of the rectification and the rest of the period of insurance.

When We Decide and Pay

Unless the schedule shows shorter periods, we will:

  • acknowledge a claim within 10 business days after we receive it;
  • inspect the defect, or arrange an inspection, within 20 business days after we receive the claim, or within 2 business days where the defect creates a risk to safety;
  • tell you in writing whether we accept the claim within 60 days after we have the information we reasonably need, with our reasons if we do not accept it; and
  • agree the scope of rectification, or make you an offer, within 90 days after we accept the claim.

If we cannot meet a time limit because the investigation is complex, we will tell you before it ends why, and when we will decide. We may do this once for each time limit. Interest on any amount we pay late is payable as s.57 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) requires.

Sum Insured

The sum insured is the final contract value shown in the schedule. If the schedule shows that indexation applies, the sum insured for a claim is increased by the annual rate shown in the schedule, compounded, from the start of the period of insurance to the date you told us about the relevant defect.

The sum insured is the most we will pay for all claims under this policy during the period of insurance, including everything we pay under the extensions and endorsements. Each amount we pay reduces the sum insured available for later claims. The sum insured is not reinstated.

If the final contract value is more than the value the developer declared, the sum insured is still the final contract value, and the Premium and Its Adjustment condition applies.

Excess

The owners corporation bears the excess shown in the schedule for each relevant defect. Related defects are one relevant defect, so one excess applies to them. No excess applies to the costs the Making the Building Safe extension covers.

GST

Where the owners corporation is entitled to an input tax credit for the GST on a cost we pay, we pay net of that credit. The sum insured includes GST to the extent the owners corporation is not entitled to an input tax credit for it.

What We Do Not Cover

These exclusions apply to the whole policy. They do not reduce the cover s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW) requires: the Decennial Liability Insurance Under the Strata Schemes Management Act condition applies to each of them.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that each exclusion in this section is consistent with the strict liability cover s.211AA requires and is likely to be acceptable to the Secretary.]

Wear and Tear

We do not pay for wear and tear, or gradual deterioration from normal use and ageing. This exclusion does not apply where the wear or deterioration happens earlier, or is worse, than could reasonably be expected because of a relevant defect.

Lack of Maintenance

We do not pay for damage to the extent it is caused by a failure to maintain a building element as the building manual or the manufacturer's instructions given to the owners corporation required. This exclusion does not apply where the developer did not give the owners corporation the building manual or those instructions, and it does not apply to the relevant defect itself.

Alterations After Completion

We do not pay for a defect in work carried out after the occupation certificate by anyone other than the builder or a contractor we appointed, or for damage to the extent that work caused it.

Defects in Lot Property

We do not pay to rectify a defect in a part of a lot that is not common property. This exclusion does not apply to damage to a lot that a relevant defect causes, to the costs the Access and Reinstatement extension covers, or where an endorsement extends the cover to lot property.

Damage From External Events

We do not pay for damage caused by fire, lightning, explosion, storm, flood, earthquake, landslip, subsidence, impact, malicious damage or another sudden external event, unless a relevant defect caused the damage or made it worse. Where it did, we pay the rectification costs of the relevant defect and the cost of repairing the part of the damage the relevant defect caused or made worse.

Loss of Use and Other Financial Loss

We do not pay for loss of rent, loss of value, loss of use, business interruption, inconvenience or any other financial loss, except as an endorsement or the Making the Building Safe extension provides.

Deliberate Damage

We do not pay for damage deliberately caused by the owners corporation, a lot owner or an occupier of a lot.

Fines and Penalties

We do not pay fines, penalties or punitive, exemplary or aggravated damages.

War

We do not pay for any loss, damage or cost arising from war, invasion, act of foreign enemy, hostilities or warlike operations (whether war is declared or not), civil war, rebellion, revolution, insurrection, military or usurped power, or confiscation by a government or public authority.

Nuclear

We do not pay for any loss, damage or cost arising from ionising radiation, contamination by radioactivity from nuclear fuel or waste, or nuclear weapons material.

Terrorism

We do not pay for any loss, damage or cost arising from an act of terrorism, meaning an act, including the use of force or violence or the threat of it, by any person or group, committed for political, religious, ideological or similar purposes, including the intention to influence a government or to put the public in fear. This exclusion does not apply to the rectification costs of a relevant defect that existed before the act of terrorism, and it has no effect to the extent the Terrorism Insurance Act 2003 (Cth) requires cover to be provided.

What You and the Developer Must Do

These obligations are subject to the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition.

Telling Us About Defects

Tell us in writing as soon as reasonably practicable after the owners corporation, its strata committee or its strata managing agent becomes aware of a defect that may be a relevant defect. A lot owner may tell us on the owners corporation's behalf.

Give us the information and documents you have that we reasonably ask for, including the building manual, maintenance records and any reports about the defect. Let us inspect the defect before it is rectified, unless urgent work is needed to make the building safe or prevent further damage.

If you tell us late, we may reduce what we pay only to the extent that the delay prejudiced us, as the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition sets out.

Access to the Building

Give us, the technical inspector and the contractors we appoint reasonable access to the common property to investigate and rectify a relevant defect, and take reasonable steps to help arrange access to the lots that need to be entered.

Preventing Further Damage

Take reasonable steps to prevent further damage once you are aware of a defect that may be a relevant defect.

Helping Us Recover From Others

Give us the reasonable help we ask for, at our cost, to recover what we pay from anyone responsible for a relevant defect, including documents, access and witnesses.

Agreements With the Builder and Others

You may make a claim against the builder, the developer or anyone else for a defect, including a relevant defect, at any time. But do not release anyone from liability for a relevant defect that we have paid for, or are paying for, without our written agreement.

The Developer's Obligations Before Cover Starts

Before this policy takes effect, the developer must:

  • appoint the technical inspector, or allow us to appoint it, and give it the access, information and documents the technical inspection plan requires;
  • tell us promptly about any change to the design, the builder, the building products for the building enclosure and the waterproofing, the contract value or the construction programme;
  • arrange for the builder to rectify the defects the technical inspector reports during the building work, and give us the technical inspector's confirmation that they have been rectified;
  • give the owners corporation the building manual, the maintenance schedule and the final technical inspection report; and
  • declare the final contract value and pay the premium as the Premium and Its Adjustment condition sets out.

These obligations are the developer's. A failure by the developer to meet them does not reduce the owners corporation's cover once this policy has taken effect: the Protection of the Owners Corporation condition applies.

Conditions

Decennial Liability Insurance Under the Strata Schemes Management Act

This policy is intended to be decennial liability insurance for the purposes of s.211AA of the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW) and Division 3A of Part 8 of the Strata Schemes Management Regulation 2016 (NSW). If a term of this policy would mean the policy does not meet a requirement of that section or that Division, as in force when this policy takes effect, the term is read so that the policy meets the requirement. If it cannot be read that way, the term does not apply to the extent needed.

If the Secretary accepted the form of this policy on conditions, the policy is read as the accepted form.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that this read-down clause is effective, and whether the Secretary requires specific wording for it.]

Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth)

Nothing in this policy excludes, restricts or modifies the operation of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). Where this policy is inconsistent with the Act, the Act prevails.

If this policy would otherwise let us refuse to pay a claim, in whole or in part, because of something the owners corporation or the developer did or did not do after this policy was entered into, s.54 of the Act applies. We may not refuse to pay the claim only because of that act or omission. Our liability is reduced by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced. Where the act or omission could reasonably be regarded as capable of causing or contributing to the loss, we may refuse to pay only the part of the claim that the act or omission caused or contributed to. We may not refuse any part that the owners corporation proves the act or omission did not cause. This applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation in this policy.

Protection of the Owners Corporation

We will not refuse, reduce or delay a claim by the owners corporation because of:

  • a failure by the developer to comply with its duty of disclosure, or a misrepresentation or fraud by the developer;
  • a breach of this policy by the developer, or a breach of the construction contract by the developer or the builder;
  • a failure to pay premium after the certificate of currency has been issued; or
  • the insolvency, deregistration or death of the developer or the builder.

Where we pay a claim that we could have refused or reduced but for this condition, we may recover the amount from the developer to the extent the law allows. We will not recover it from the owners corporation or a lot owner.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that the insurer's remedies under ss.28 and 56 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) can be limited to the developer in this way, and how they interact with the owners corporation's rights as the insured.]

When Cover Starts

We issue this policy before the developer applies for the occupation certificate. It takes effect on the date the occupation certificate is issued, and the period of insurance starts on that date. Before that date this policy gives no cover for building work under construction; the builder's construction insurance covers that.

We issue the certificate of currency only when:

  • the developer has paid the premium in full; and
  • the technical inspector has issued the final technical inspection report.

If the occupation certificate is not issued within three years after we issue the certificate of currency, this policy does not take effect unless we agree in writing to extend that time. We then refund the premium, less the technical inspector's fees and the non-refundable government charges.

Once this policy has taken effect, it continues for the whole period of insurance.

No Cancellation

Once we have issued the certificate of currency, this policy cannot be cancelled by us, the developer, the owners corporation or anyone else, and no premium is refundable except as the When Cover Starts condition provides. We will not exercise any right the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) or any other law gives us to cancel this policy.

Before we issue the certificate of currency, the developer may withdraw from this policy by telling us in writing. We refund the premium paid, less the technical inspector's fees incurred and the non-refundable government charges.

[WP REVIEW: confirm how ss.59 and 60 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) interact with the requirement of cl.55A of the Strata Schemes Management Regulation 2016 (NSW) that the policy not permit cancellation, and confirm that collecting the whole premium before the certificate of currency is issued removes the non-payment ground.]

Premium and Its Adjustment

The premium is calculated on the contract value. The developer pays the deposit premium shown in the schedule when it accepts our offer, and the balance premium before we issue the certificate of currency.

Before we issue the certificate of currency, the developer must declare the final contract value. The balance premium is adjusted to the final contract value.

If, after this policy has taken effect, the final contract value is found to be more than the value the developer declared, the sum insured is still the final contract value. The developer must pay us the additional premium, and we may recover it from the developer. We will not reduce a claim by the owners corporation because the additional premium has not been paid.

The Owners Corporation as the Insured

This policy is taken out by the developer in favour of the owners corporation. The owners corporation becomes the insured when the strata plan for the building is registered. Until then, the developer holds this policy for the owners corporation that will be constituted, and may not deal with it in a way that reduces the owners corporation's rights.

A change in the lot owners does not affect this policy. The owners corporation acts for the lot owners from time to time.

Our Right to Recover From Others

After we pay for a relevant defect, we may take over the owners corporation's rights to recover the amount from anyone responsible for it, including the builder, the developer, a designer, a certifier, a subcontractor, a supplier or a manufacturer, and act in the owners corporation's name.

We will not seek to recover anything from the owners corporation, a lot owner, an occupier of a lot or the strata managing agent, except from a person who caused damage deliberately or carried out work the Alterations After Completion exclusion describes.

From any amount we recover, we first take what we paid and our reasonable costs of recovery. We pay any balance to the owners corporation.

Amounts You Recover From Others

If the owners corporation recovers money from anyone else for a relevant defect that we have paid for, it must repay us the part of that money that covers what we paid, up to the amount we paid. We do not reduce a claim because the owners corporation could have recovered from someone else.

Other Insurance

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy, including the building insurance the owners corporation holds under the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW), we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows.

Information for the Regulator

We may give the Secretary, Building Commission NSW and any other government agency that administers the Strata Schemes Management Act 2015 (NSW) the certificate of currency and information about this policy, the technical inspection, notifications and claims, as the law requires or allows. The developer and the owners corporation agree to this.

Records We Keep

We keep this policy, the technical inspection reports and the records of each claim for at least seven years after the period of insurance ends. We give the owners corporation a copy of the policy and the final technical inspection report on request.

Technical Disputes

If the owners corporation and we disagree about whether a defect is a relevant defect, or about the scope or cost of rectification, the owners corporation may refer the disagreement to an independent expert. The expert is a building consultant or engineer agreed by the owners corporation and us or, if we do not agree within 10 business days, nominated by the president of the Resolution Institute.

The expert decides as an expert and not as an arbitrator. The expert's decision binds us. It does not bind the owners corporation, which keeps its rights under the Disputes and Complaints condition and at law. We pay the expert's fees.

Fraud

If a claim is made fraudulently, we may refuse to pay it, as s.56 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) allows. Fraud by the developer, the builder or a lot owner does not affect the owners corporation's cover unless the owners corporation was a party to it.

Sanctions

We do not provide cover, pay any claim or provide any benefit under this policy to the extent that doing so would expose us to any sanction, prohibition or restriction under:

  • the Autonomous Sanctions Act 2011 (Cth), the Charter of the United Nations Act 1945 (Cth), or regulations made under either Act; or
  • the trade or economic sanctions laws of the United Kingdom, the United States of America or the European Union, to the extent they apply to us.

[WP REVIEW: confirm that naming the UK, US and EU regimes is appropriate for Australian capacity; this is a deliberate library-wide scope choice.]

Goods and Services Tax

The premium includes GST where it applies, under A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth). Claims are paid as the GST provision sets out. The owners corporation must tell us its entitlement to input tax credits when making a claim.

Privacy

We collect, use and disclose personal information about the developer, the owners corporation, lot owners and occupiers to assess the proposal, administer this policy and manage claims, in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and our privacy policy. Disputes and Complaints

If the owners corporation or the developer is unhappy with our service or a decision, contact us at the details in the schedule. We will acknowledge the complaint within one business day and give our decision within 30 calendar days.

If the complainant is not satisfied with our response, or we have not responded within 30 calendar days, the complaint may be taken to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA), a free and independent dispute resolution service, if it is within AFCA's rules: www.afca.org.au, 1800 931 678, info@afca.org.au.

How to Make a Claim

  1. If the defect creates a risk to safety, make the building safe and tell us straight away. The Making the Building Safe extension covers the reasonable cost.
  2. Contact us at the details shown in the schedule, or ask the strata managing agent or your broker to contact us.
  3. Tell us what the defect is, where it is, when it was found and what damage it has caused, and send photographs and any reports you have.
  4. Give us, and the people we appoint, access to inspect the defect.
  5. We will acknowledge the claim, inspect and decide within the time limits in the When We Decide and Pay provision, and keep you informed.
  6. If you disagree with our decision, you may use the Technical Disputes condition or the Disputes and Complaints condition.

E01 Alternative Accommodation

Where a lot cannot be lived in while a relevant defect is being rectified, we pay the reasonable cost of alternative accommodation of a similar standard for the occupiers of that lot, and the reasonable cost of moving and storing their contents, until the lot can be lived in again.

We pay up to the limit for each lot and the aggregate limit shown in the schedule, within the sum insured. We pay the lot owner, or the occupiers where the lot owner asks us to.

Where the lot is let, we pay the lot owner's loss of rent instead of the occupiers' accommodation costs, up to the same limits.

Schedule variables: limit for each lot (default $50,000); aggregate limit (default 10% of the sum insured).

E02 Waterproofing in Lots

The Relevant Defects in the Common Property clause also covers a relevant defect in the waterproofing of a wet area, balcony or planter box within a lot, whether or not that waterproofing forms part of the common property.

The Defects in Lot Property exclusion does not apply to that waterproofing. We pay the rectification costs to the lot owner or, where the owners corporation arranges the rectification, to the owners corporation.

Schedule variables: limit for each lot (default $100,000), within the sum insured.

E03 Outstanding Defects at Completion

The defects listed as outstanding in the final technical inspection report are set out in the schedule for this endorsement. The developer must arrange for the builder to rectify them, and the technical inspector must confirm in writing that each has been rectified.

For each listed defect, the Relevant Defects in the Common Property clause applies from the earlier of:

  • the date the technical inspector confirms that it has been rectified; and
  • the cover start date for listed defects shown in the schedule.

From that date, a listed defect that is a relevant defect is covered in the same way as any other relevant defect. This endorsement does not affect the cover for any defect that is not listed.

Schedule variables: the listed defects; cover start date for listed defects (default six months after the occupation certificate).

E04 Mixed-Use Buildings

The building contains lots that are not used for residential purposes. This policy covers relevant defects in the building elements of the common property of the whole building, including the parts that serve those lots.

We do not pay to rectify a defect in the fit-out of a non-residential lot, or for a non-residential lot owner's or occupier's loss of income, loss of rent or business interruption.

Schedule variables: the number of non-residential lots and their use.

E05 Modular and Prefabricated Construction

Some building elements are manufactured off site as volumetric modules or prefabricated panels. The following conditions apply to the building work:

  • the technical inspector inspects the manufacture of those building elements at the factory at the stages the technical inspection plan sets out;
  • the manufacturer gives the technical inspector its quality records and its certificates of compliance with the Building Code of Australia for those building elements; and
  • the technical inspector inspects the connections between modules or panels, and the waterproofing and fire separation at those connections, on site before they are closed in.

If a condition is not met, we may decline to issue the certificate of currency until the technical inspector is satisfied by other means. Once this policy has taken effect, the Protection of the Owners Corporation condition applies.

Schedule variables: the building elements manufactured off site; the manufacturer.
