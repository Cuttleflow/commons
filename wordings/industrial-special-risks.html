About This Policy

This policy insures the property and business income of medium and large commercial and industrial businesses in Australia. It is an industrial special risks policy: it covers physical loss of or damage to your property from any cause it does not exclude, and the loss of income that follows when that damage interrupts your business.

It follows the two-part structure the Australian market has used for industrial special risks policies since the late 1980s, written in plain English and updated for current law. It was drafted independently and does not reproduce the text of any other wording.

This policy, together with your schedule, your proposal and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms your contract of insurance with us. Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule. Where more than one insurer subscribes, the schedule shows each insurer's share. Where an insurer subscribes to the General Insurance Code of Practice, the schedule says so.

Who this policy is for. This wording is written for businesses with substantial property and business income to insure. Some buyers, such as small businesses, may be retail clients under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). Where a buyer is a retail client, the issuer must give a Product Disclosure Statement and make a Target Market Determination available. This wording is not itself a Product Disclosure Statement.

Your duty of disclosure. Before this policy is entered into, renewed, extended, varied or reinstated, you have the duty of disclosure in s.21 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth). You must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. If you do not comply, we may reduce or refuse a claim, or cancel the policy, as that Act allows. What your directors, officers and the person responsible for your insurance know is treated as known to you.

Terms to note. This policy:

  • covers Flood only up to the flood limit shown in the schedule for each Situation, and not at a Situation the schedule excludes (see the Flood exclusion);
  • reduces a claim where the Declared Value at the Situation of the loss is too low (see the Co-insurance provision);
  • does not cover earthquake, or theft without forcible entry, unless an endorsement adds that cover (see the Earth Movement exclusion and the Theft Without Forcible Entry exclusion);
  • does not cover the breakdown of a machine itself (see the Breakdown exclusion);
  • limits cover for cyber events and excludes communicable disease (see the Cyber Events exclusion and the Communicable Disease exclusion).

Commission and fees. The schedule or the quote shows any commission or fee paid to your broker in connection with this policy.

Financial Claims Scheme. If the schedule shows the insurer is an APRA-authorised general insurer, you may be entitled to a payment under the Financial Claims Scheme if the insurer becomes insolvent. Information is available at www.fcs.gov.au.

Definitions

In this policy, the words below have these meanings wherever they appear, whatever their capitalisation. The singular includes the plural.

Annual Turnover

The Turnover during the 12 months immediately before the Damage, adjusted as the Adjustments for Trend and Circumstances provision provides.

Business

The business described in the schedule, carried on at the Situations, including owning, managing and maintaining the premises and providing facilities for employees and visitors.

Damage

Physical loss, destruction or damage. Damaged has a matching meaning. Loss of use, loss of market, delay, or a fall in value without physical loss, destruction or damage is not Damage.

Declared Value

The value you declare, as shown in the schedule, for the Insured Property at each Situation by category of property, or for each item of the Business Interruption section. Declared Values are used to calculate premium and to apply the Co-insurance provision and the Measuring Business Interruption Loss provision. They are not limits.

Deductible

The amount shown in the schedule that you bear, as the Deductible provision provides.

Endorsement

A written change to this policy that we issue and that is shown in the schedule. The available endorsements are listed in the Endorsement Library.

Event

One occurrence, or a series of occurrences arising from one originating cause. All Damage by earthquake, volcanic eruption, storm, cyclone, hail, bushfire or Flood within any 72 consecutive hours is one Event. You may choose when each 72-hour period starts, but no two periods may overlap.

Flood

The covering of normally dry land by water that has escaped or been released from the normal confines of a lake, river, creek or other natural watercourse (whether or not it has been altered or modified), or of a reservoir, canal or dam. It includes water from any of those sources that reaches the land, or enters a building, through a stormwater drain, sewer or other pipe. It does not include action of the sea, storm surge or tsunami. Rainwater and stormwater that has not escaped from one of those sources is not Flood.

Gross Profit

The amount produced by adding Turnover and the value of closing stock and work in progress, and subtracting the value of opening stock and work in progress and the Specified Working Expenses. Stock and work in progress are valued on the basis your auditors normally use.

Gross Rentals

The rent, licence fees and recoverable outgoings paid or payable to you by tenants and other occupiers of the Situations.

Indemnity Period

The period starting when the Damage happens and ending no later than the number of months shown in the schedule for the relevant Situation and item, during which the results of the Business are affected by the Damage.

Insured Property

All real and personal property of every kind that you own, that you are responsible for, or that you agreed in writing before the Damage to insure, other than the property listed in the Property Not Insured exclusion. It includes property you acquire during the Period of Insurance, as the Capital Additions extension provides.

Limit of Liability

The amount shown in the schedule as the most we pay, as the Limit of Liability provision provides.

Money

Cash, bank notes, coins, cheques, money orders, postal orders, unused postage and revenue stamps, credit card sales vouchers and negotiable instruments, belonging to you or held by you.

Period of Insurance

The period shown in the schedule, and any further period for which we accept premium.

Pollutant

Any substance that contaminates or irritates, whether solid, liquid, gas or heat, including smoke, soot, vapour, fumes, acids, alkalis, chemicals and waste. Waste includes material to be recycled, reconditioned or reclaimed.

Rate of Gross Profit

The Gross Profit earned on Turnover during the financial year immediately before the Damage, as a percentage of that Turnover, adjusted as the Adjustments for Trend and Circumstances provision provides.

Situation

Each location listed in the schedule, and any location added during the Period of Insurance under this policy or an endorsement.

Specified Working Expenses

The expenses listed as specified working expenses in the schedule. If the schedule lists none, they are purchases, less discounts received.

Standard Turnover

The Turnover during the part of the 12 months immediately before the Damage that corresponds with the Indemnity Period, adjusted as the Adjustments for Trend and Circumstances provision provides.

Turnover

The money paid or payable to you for goods sold and delivered, and for services rendered, in the course of the Business at the Situations.

Value at Risk

The cost, at the time of the Damage, of reinstating all the Insured Property at a Situation on the basis of settlement that applies to it.

We, Us, Our

The insurer or insurers named in the schedule. Where there is more than one, each is liable only for its own share as the schedule shows, and not jointly with the others.

You, Your

The insured named in the schedule and each of its subsidiaries, as defined in the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), at the start of the Period of Insurance. It also includes an entity that becomes such a subsidiary during the Period of Insurance, for 90 days after it does so, or longer if we agree in writing, but not for Damage that happened before it became a subsidiary. Each is insured for its own interest. The entity named first in the schedule acts for all of them in giving and receiving notices, paying premium and receiving claim payments.

Material Damage

This section applies to all Insured Property at the Situations. The schedule shows the Declared Values, the Limit of Liability, the sub-limits and the Deductibles.

Material Damage Cover

We cover Damage to Insured Property at a Situation that happens during the Period of Insurance, from any cause this policy does not exclude.

We pay as the Basis of Settlement provision provides, up to the Limit of Liability and any sub-limit, after the Deductible.

Property Not Insured

The Material Damage section does not cover:

  • Money, except up to the Money sub-limit shown in the schedule;
  • vehicles registered for road use, and their accessories, while away from a Situation;
  • property in transit, except movement within a Situation and property covered by the Temporary Removal extension;
  • land, water, roads and dams, and the cost of the land component of any reinstatement;
  • livestock, growing crops and standing timber;
  • aircraft and watercraft, except small watercraft the schedule shows;
  • data and software as such, except as the Data Restoration extension provides. Physical Damage to the media holding them is covered.

Basis of Settlement

We pay the cost of reinstatement. That is the cost of repairing the Damaged property, or of rebuilding or replacing it with property of equivalent function and capacity, as new, without any deduction for wear or depreciation. Reinstatement does not have to be in the same form or on the same site, as long as our cost is not increased.

  • Reinstatement must start and be carried out with reasonable speed. Until it is carried out we pay no more than the indemnity value, and if it is not carried out we pay only the indemnity value.
  • The indemnity value is the cost of reinstatement at the time of the Damage, less an allowance for wear, tear and depreciation, and not more than the property's market value immediately before the Damage.
  • Stock and work in progress are valued at their cost of replacement at the time of the Damage.
  • We may choose to pay the cost of reinstatement, or to repair, rebuild or replace the property ourselves.

Co-insurance

We test whether the Declared Values are adequate separately for the Situation where the Damage happens, at the time of the Damage.

  • If the Declared Value of the Insured Property at that Situation is at least the co-insurance percentage shown in the schedule (85% if none is shown) of its Value at Risk, we pay the loss in full, subject to the Deductible and limits.
  • If it is less, we pay this proportion of the loss: Declared Value divided by (co-insurance percentage multiplied by Value at Risk). We then deduct the Deductible.
  • The test does not apply where the loss at that Situation, before the Deductible, is less than the minor-loss percentage shown in the schedule (10% if none is shown) of the Declared Value for that Situation.
  • The test does not apply to Money, or to any amount the schedule shows as a first-loss amount.
  • the Measuring Business Interruption Loss provision contains its own test for the Business Interruption section.

Worked example. The Value at Risk at the Situation is $10,000,000 and the co-insurance percentage is 85%, so the threshold is $8,500,000. The Declared Value is $7,000,000 and the loss is $1,000,000. We pay 7,000,000 divided by 8,500,000, which is 82.35% of the loss, or $823,529, less the Deductible. Had the loss been $600,000, which is under 10% of the $7,000,000 Declared Value, we would pay it in full less the Deductible.

Limit of Liability

The most we pay for all Damage, and all loss under the Business Interruption section, arising from one Event at one Situation is the Limit of Liability shown in the schedule. The schedule states whether this limit is combined for both sections or separate for each.

  • Where one Event causes Damage at more than one Situation, the Limit of Liability applies separately at each Situation, unless the schedule shows a limit for any one Event across all Situations. If it does, that limit is the most we pay for the Event.
  • The Limit of Liability includes every extension and sub-limit, unless the schedule says a particular amount is paid in addition.
  • Declared Values are used to test the adequacy of values and to calculate premium. They do not increase the Limit of Liability.

Removal of Debris

We pay the reasonable costs you incur, following Damage covered by the Material Damage section, to demolish, dismantle, shore up or prop up the Damaged property; to remove, store and dispose of its debris; and to clean up Pollutants at the Situation released by that Damage. We do not pay to clean up Pollutants beyond the boundary of the Situation, or costs an authority requires where there is no Damage covered by this policy. The most we pay is the removal of debris sub-limit shown in the schedule.

Extra Cost of Reinstatement

We pay the additional cost of reinstating Damaged property to comply with laws, regulations, building codes and the requirements of any authority in force when the reinstatement is carried out, including work on undamaged parts of a Damaged building that is needed for the reinstated building to comply. We do not pay for a requirement you were notified of before the Damage; fines or penalties; or any cost if the property is not reinstated, unless reinstatement is prevented by law. The most we pay is the extra cost of reinstatement sub-limit shown in the schedule.

Professional and Government Fees

We pay the fees of architects, surveyors, engineers, quantity surveyors, project managers and lawyers necessarily incurred in reinstating Damaged property, and the fees and contributions payable to a government or local authority for approvals needed to reinstate it, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule. Fees for preparing a claim are paid only under the Claims Preparation Costs extension.

Capital Additions

Property that you buy, build or install at a Situation during the Period of Insurance is insured up to the capital additions sub-limit shown in the schedule for any one Situation, until it is included in the Declared Values. Declare it to us by the end of the Period of Insurance. We charge premium at the rate shown in the schedule from the date of the addition.

Temporary Removal

Insured Property temporarily removed from a Situation to another location in Australia for cleaning, servicing, repair, renovation or a similar purpose is covered while at that location and in transit to and from it, up to the temporary removal sub-limit shown in the schedule.

Fire Fighting and Mitigation Costs

We pay charges made by a fire or emergency service for attending a fire or other emergency that threatens Insured Property at a Situation, the cost of refilling or replacing fire extinguishing equipment used, and the reasonable cost of measures you take to prevent imminent Damage to Insured Property, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule.

Temporary Protection and Repairs

We pay the reasonable cost of temporary repairs, fencing, security and protection of Damaged property at a Situation needed after Damage covered by the Material Damage section, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule.

Locks and Keys

If keys, access cards or combinations for a Situation, or for a safe or strongroom at a Situation, are stolen, or are lost through Damage covered by the Material Damage section, we pay the cost of replacing or recoding the locks and replacing the keys or cards, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule.

Undamaged Foundations and Parts

Where undamaged parts of a Damaged building, including its foundations, cannot lawfully or practically be used in reinstating it, we pay their value and the cost of removing them as part of the cost of reinstatement.

Data Restoration

Where Damage covered by the Material Damage section destroys or damages data storage media, we pay the reasonable cost of restoring data and software from back-ups or source documents, up to the data restoration sub-limit shown in the schedule. We do not pay for the value of the data itself, or for anything the Cyber Events exclusion excludes.

Claims Preparation Costs

We pay the reasonable fees of your accountants, auditors, engineers, valuers and other professionals for producing the information we ask for to assess a claim under either section, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule. We do not pay fees for disputing a claim with us, the fees of anyone paid by a share of the claim, or the cost of your own employees unless the schedule says so.

Business Interruption

This section applies only if the schedule shows it. The schedule shows the basis (gross profit, gross revenue or gross rentals), the Declared Value of each item, the Indemnity Periods and the waiting period.

Business Interruption Cover

We cover the loss you suffer because the Business is interrupted or interfered with during the Indemnity Period as a result of Damage that happens during the Period of Insurance and is:

  • Damage to Insured Property at a Situation that is covered by the Material Damage section, or would be covered but for the Deductible; or
  • Damage at a Situation to property you use in the Business but do not own and are not responsible for insuring, such as a building owned by your landlord, from a cause the Material Damage section would cover if that property were Insured Property.

We pay as the Measuring Business Interruption Loss provision provides, up to the Limit of Liability and any sub-limit.

Measuring Business Interruption Loss

The schedule shows which basis applies. Each figure is adjusted as the Adjustments for Trend and Circumstances provision provides.

Gross profit basis. We pay the Rate of Gross Profit applied to the amount by which Turnover during the Indemnity Period falls short of Standard Turnover because of the Damage. We also pay the additional expenditure you necessarily and reasonably incur to avoid or reduce that shortfall during the Indemnity Period, up to the Rate of Gross Profit applied to the shortfall it avoids. From the total we deduct any savings during the Indemnity Period in charges and expenses payable out of Gross Profit that stop or reduce because of the Damage.

Gross revenue basis. We pay the amount by which Turnover during the Indemnity Period falls short of Standard Turnover because of the Damage, and additional expenditure to avoid or reduce that shortfall up to the amount of the shortfall it avoids, less savings in charges and expenses that stop or reduce because of the Damage.

Gross rentals basis. We pay the amount by which Gross Rentals during the Indemnity Period fall short of the Gross Rentals for the corresponding period in the 12 months before the Damage, and additional expenditure to avoid or reduce that shortfall up to the amount it avoids, less savings in charges and expenses that stop or reduce because of the Damage.

Additional increased cost of working. We also pay reasonable additional expenditure you incur during the Indemnity Period to keep the Business running or to reduce the interruption, beyond the amounts above, up to the separate amount shown in the schedule for this item.

Underinsurance. The annual amount at risk is the Rate of Gross Profit applied to the Annual Turnover, the Annual Turnover, or the Gross Rentals for the 12 months before the Damage, according to the basis. Where the longest Indemnity Period is more than 12 months, the annual amount at risk is increased in proportion. If the Declared Value of the item is less than the business interruption co-insurance percentage shown in the schedule (85% if none is shown) of the annual amount at risk, we pay only the proportion of the loss under that item that the Declared Value bears to that percentage of the annual amount at risk.

Adjustments for Trend and Circumstances

Before applying the Rate of Gross Profit, Annual Turnover, Standard Turnover or Gross Rentals, we adjust them for anything that would have changed the results of the Business during the Indemnity Period if the Damage had not happened, such as growth or decline, seasonal patterns, new contracts or the loss of customers, whether it arose before or after the Damage. The aim is to measure what the Business would actually have earned.

An accounting term used in the Business Interruption section that this policy does not define has the meaning your auditors give it in your financial statements.

If, during the Indemnity Period, you or someone acting for you sells goods or provides services for the benefit of the Business at a place other than the Situations, the money paid or payable for them counts as Turnover during the Indemnity Period.

Special Cases in Business Interruption

Departments. If the Business has separate departments whose results can be identified, and you ask us to, we apply the calculations in the Measuring Business Interruption Loss provision separately to each department affected by the Damage.

New business. If the Business had traded for less than 12 months before the Damage, we use the average figures for the period it traded, adjusted to a 12-month basis.

Accumulated stock. We make a fair allowance for any shortfall in Turnover caused by the Damage that is postponed because Turnover was maintained during the Indemnity Period from stock built up before the Damage.

Salvage sales. If you hold a sale of salvaged stock during the Indemnity Period, the Turnover from that sale is not counted when measuring the shortfall in Turnover, and we reduce what we pay by the gross profit actually earned on the sale.

Waiting Period

Where the schedule shows a waiting period for this section or an extension, it is a threshold, not a deduction. If the interruption lasts longer than the waiting period, we measure the loss from the start of the interruption. If it does not, we pay nothing for that interruption. The schedule may instead apply the TXSD Time Excess (Deducted) endorsement, under which the first part of the loss is deducted.

Worked example. The waiting period is 3 days and a fire interrupts the Business for 20 days. Because the interruption lasted more than 3 days, we measure the loss for all 20 days.

Prevention of Access

We cover loss under the Business Interruption section when access to or use of a Situation is prevented or hindered by Damage, from a cause the Material Damage section would cover, to property in the vicinity of the Situation (or within the distance shown in the schedule, if one is shown), whether or not the property is yours. The most we pay is the prevention of access sub-limit shown in the schedule, after the waiting period shown.

Public Utilities

We cover loss under the Business Interruption section caused by the accidental failure of the supply of electricity, gas, water or telecommunications to a Situation, arising at the supplier's premises or anywhere in the network that supplies the Situation, where the failure lasts longer than the waiting period shown in the schedule. The most we pay is the public utilities sub-limit shown.

Suppliers and Customers

We cover loss under the Business Interruption section resulting from Damage, of a kind the Material Damage section would cover, at the premises in Australia of your suppliers and customers. The most we pay is the sub-limit shown in the schedule for suppliers and customers named in the schedule, and the lower sub-limit shown for those not named. Suppliers and customers not named means those you deal with directly, not their own suppliers or customers.

Computer Installations

We cover loss under the Business Interruption section resulting from Damage, of a kind the Material Damage section would cover, to computer installations and data storage media in Australia that the Business uses, wherever they are, up to the sub-limit shown in the schedule. The Cyber Events exclusion applies.

What We Do Not Cover

These exclusions apply to both sections. Where an exclusion removes cover for a kind of Damage, the Business Interruption section does not cover loss resulting from that Damage either.

War

We do not pay for any loss, Damage or cost arising from war, invasion, act of foreign enemy, hostilities or warlike operations (whether war is declared or not), civil war, rebellion, revolution, insurrection, military or usurped power, or confiscation by a government or public authority.

Nuclear

We do not pay for any loss, Damage or cost arising from ionising radiation, contamination by radioactivity from nuclear fuel or waste, or nuclear weapons material.

Terrorism

In this exclusion, an act of terrorism is an act, or a threat of an act, involving force, violence or damage to property, done for a political, religious, ideological or similar purpose, including to influence a government or to put the public, or part of it, in fear.

  • We do not pay for Damage or loss caused by an act of terrorism, or by any action taken to control, prevent or respond to one, except as this exclusion provides.
  • Where the Terrorism and Cyclone Insurance Act 2003 (Cth) applies to this policy and loss arises from a declared terrorist incident under that Act, this exclusion has effect only to the extent that Act allows. We pay that loss on the terms of this policy, subject to any reduction in our liability that the Act permits. [WP REVIEW: confirm the treatment of reductions announced under the Act, and of incidents declared after a claim has been declined.]
  • If the schedule shows a terrorism sub-limit, we also cover Damage and loss from an act of terrorism that is not a declared terrorist incident, up to that sub-limit in the aggregate for the Period of Insurance.

Cyber Events

In this exclusion, a computer system is any computer, device, network, software, cloud service, industrial or process control system, and the data stored in or processed by any of them, whether owned by you or anyone else. A cyber event is an unauthorised, malicious or criminal act involving a computer system, or an error, omission, failure or malfunction of a computer system.

We do not pay for loss of, damage to, or loss of use or function of data or software, however caused, except as the Data Restoration extension provides. We do not pay for Damage or loss caused by a cyber event, except as set out in the option shown in the schedule. If the schedule shows no option, Option A applies.

  • Option A, fire and explosion. Damage by fire or explosion that results from a cyber event is covered, and so is loss under the Business Interruption section resulting from that Damage.
  • Option B, physical damage. Physical Damage to property other than data or software that results from a cyber event is covered, and so is loss under the Business Interruption section resulting from that Damage.

Communicable Disease

In this exclusion, a communicable disease is any disease that can be transmitted from one person, animal or thing to another by any means, including by a virus, bacterium, parasite or other organism.

We do not pay for loss, cost or expense arising from a communicable disease, or the fear or threat of one, including any order, direction or restriction by an authority relating to it, or the cost of cleaning, disinfecting or decontaminating property because of one. This exclusion does not apply to Damage by fire, explosion or another physical cause that is not a communicable disease, or to cover provided by the INCD Incidents at the Premises endorsement or the COMB Communicable Disease Buy-back endorsement, to the extent and limits they state. [WP REVIEW: confirm the breadth of the definition and its interaction with food poisoning under the INCD endorsement.]

Pollution

We do not pay for Damage or loss caused by pollution or contamination by a Pollutant, unless it results from a sudden, identifiable, unintended and unexpected incident that happens entirely at a specific time and place during the Period of Insurance.

Gradual Causes

We do not pay for the cost of making good property affected by wear and tear, gradual deterioration, inherent vice, latent defect, rust, oxidation, corrosion, or the action of light or the atmosphere. Damage to other property, or other Damage, that results from any of these and is not otherwise excluded is covered.

Faulty Design and Workmanship

We do not pay the cost of correcting faulty or defective design, materials or workmanship. Damage to other property that results from it is covered.

Consequential Loss

The Material Damage section does not cover loss of use, delay, loss of market or any other consequential loss. The Business Interruption section covers some of these losses, as it states.

Breakdown

We do not pay for Damage to a machine or electrical installation caused by its own mechanical or electrical breakdown or derangement. Damage to other property that results from the breakdown is covered. The MBRK Machinery Breakdown endorsement, the ELEC Electronic Equipment Breakdown endorsement or the FUSN Fusion endorsement, where the schedule shows them, add breakdown cover.

Earth Movement

We do not pay for Damage caused by earthquake, volcanic eruption, subterranean fire, subsidence, settlement, landslip, erosion, or shrinkage or expansion of the ground, unless the earth movement itself results from a cause this policy covers. The EQKE Earthquake: 72-Hour Event and Deductible endorsement, where the schedule shows it, adds cover for earthquake, volcanic eruption and subterranean fire.

Theft Without Forcible Entry

We do not pay for theft or attempted theft unless it involves forcible and violent entry to or exit from a building at a Situation. The THFT Full Theft endorsement, where the schedule shows it, adds wider theft cover.

Flood

We cover Damage by Flood at a Situation, and loss under the Business Interruption section resulting from it, only up to the flood limit shown in the schedule for that Situation, after the flood Deductible. There is no cover for Flood at a Situation for which the schedule shows no flood limit, or at a Situation or postcode the schedule lists as excluded.

Paying Claims

These provisions apply to both sections.

Deductible

You bear the Deductible shown in the schedule for each Event at each Situation. Where the schedule shows a separate Deductible for a peril, an extension or an endorsement, that Deductible applies to Damage from that cause instead. Where more than one Deductible could apply to Damage from one Event at one Situation, only the highest applies.

Limits and Sub-limits

  • Where more than one limit or sub-limit could apply to the same Damage or loss, the higher one applies, not their total. A sub-limit that the schedule marks "most we pay" applies instead, whatever other provision might apply.
  • A sub-limit stated "per Event" applies once to all Damage and loss from one Event. A sub-limit stated "in the aggregate" is the most we pay for all such Damage and loss during the Period of Insurance.
  • A sub-limit stated "in addition" is paid on top of the Limit of Liability. Every other sub-limit is part of it.

GST on Claims

Where you are entitled to an input tax credit for the GST on the cost of a repair, replacement or other expense, we pay the claim net of that credit. Each Declared Value, sum insured and limit in this policy includes GST to the extent you are not entitled to an input tax credit for it. This reflects the A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999 (Cth).

Other Insurance

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another insurance policy, we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows. This does not apply where the schedule or an endorsement states that this policy is excess of a specified underlying policy, in which case this policy pays only after the limit of that specified policy is exhausted.

Who We Pay

We pay you, or the interested party the schedule names for its interest. A payment to an interested party discharges us to the extent of the amount paid.

Your Obligations

The Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) condition applies to every obligation in this section.

Tell Us About a Claim

When Damage happens that may lead to a claim:

COIS Co-insurance by Situation

This endorsement replaces the co-insurance (average or underinsurance) provisions of the Base Wording for the Material Damage cover.

  • The test is made separately for the Situation where the Damage happens, at the time of the Damage.
  • If the Declared Value of the property at that Situation is at least the co-insurance percentage shown in the Schedule of its value at risk, we pay the loss in full, subject to the Deductible and limits.
  • If it is less, we pay this proportion of the loss: Declared Value ÷ (co-insurance percentage × value at risk). We then deduct the Deductible.
  • The test does not apply where the loss at that Situation, before the Deductible, is less than the minor-loss percentage shown in the Schedule of the Declared Value for that Situation.
  • Value at risk means the cost, at the time of the Damage, of reinstating all the insured property at that Situation on the basis of settlement that applies to it.
  • The Business Interruption cover keeps its own co-insurance provision in the Base Wording, unless the Schedule says otherwise.

Worked example. Value at risk at the Situation is $10,000,000 and the co-insurance percentage is 85%, so the threshold is $8,500,000. The Declared Value is $7,000,000 and the loss is $1,000,000. We pay 7,000,000 ÷ 8,500,000 = 82.35% of the loss, which is $823,529, less the Deductible. Had the loss been $600,000, which is under 10% of the $7,000,000 Declared Value, we would pay it in full less the Deductible.

The courts of that State or Territory, and the Federal Court of Australia, have non-exclusive jurisdiction. This does not limit your right to complain to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority.
