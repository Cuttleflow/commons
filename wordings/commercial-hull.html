Commercial Hull Insurance

Hull and machinery cover for Australian domestic commercial vessels on the Institute Time Clauses Hulls, with war and strikes, optional protection and indemnity for passengers, pollution and wreck removal, and Insurance Contracts Act protections given by agreement.

About This Policy

This policy insures a commercial vessel: a vessel used in a business and operated under the Marine Safety (Domestic Commercial Vessel) National Law. It is built on the Institute hull clauses, which are the standard hull terms of the London and Australian markets. This wording does not repeat those clauses. It names the clauses that apply, amends them for Australian law and practice and for small domestic vessels, and adds what they do not say: the vessel and its operational area, cover for your liabilities as owner or operator, additional benefits, the excesses, and the protections of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) that we give you by agreement.

It has two sections. The schedule shows which apply.

SectionWhat it coversBasis
Hull and MachineryLoss of or damage to the vessel, and the collision liability, general average, salvage and sue and labour costs the Institute clauses provide for, with war and strikes coverAccidents and occurrences during the policy period
Protection and IndemnityYour legal liability as owner or operator of the vessel for death, injury, property damage, wreck removal and pollution, including liability to passengersOccurrences during the policy period

The schedule shows the vessel, its service category and operational area, its agreed value, whether the Hull and Machinery section is on full conditions or total loss only, the limit for the Protection and Indemnity section, and the excesses.

This policy, together with your schedule, your proposal, the Institute clauses named in it and any endorsements shown in the schedule, forms your contract of insurance with us. Please read them together and tell us if anything is incorrect. The Order of Precedence condition says which part applies where they differ. Words with special meanings are listed in the Definitions section.

Important Information

Who issues this policy. The insurer, its Australian Financial Services Licence and its contact details are shown in the schedule.

Who this policy is for. This wording is designed for businesses that own or operate a domestic commercial vessel in Australia: charter and tour boats, water taxis, small ferries, workboats, barges, fishing vessels and similar vessels. It is not for a vessel owned by individuals and used only for private recreation, which a pleasure craft policy insures.

Which law applies. A contract of marine insurance is governed by the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth). The Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) does not apply to it of its own force (s.9(1)(d) of that Act). This policy gives you, by agreement, the protections of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) that the Marine Insurance Act and Insurance Contracts Act condition lists. If that Act applies to this policy by law, for example because the vessel operates only on inland waters, it applies in full and nothing in this policy reduces it.

Your duty of disclosure. Before you enter into, renew, extend, vary or reinstate this policy, you must tell us every matter you know, or that a reasonable person in your circumstances could be expected to know, that is relevant to our decision whether to insure you and on what terms. You do not need to tell us anything that reduces the risk, that is common knowledge, that we know or should know as an insurer, or that we waive. The Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) sets a stricter duty and lets an insurer avoid the contract for any breach of it. By agreement, we do not rely on that Act's remedies. The Non-Disclosure and Misrepresentation condition sets out what we may do if you do not tell us something.

Wholesale insurance. Insurance to which the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) applies is not a prescribed kind of general insurance for retail clients under the Corporations Regulations 2001 (Cth). This wording is not a Product Disclosure Statement, and no Target Market Determination has been prepared for it. General Insurance Code of Practice. The Code of Practice does not apply to marine insurance. Where the insurer applies it to this policy voluntarily, the schedule says so.

Commission and fees. The schedule or the quote shows any commission or fee paid to your broker in connection with this policy.

Definitions

These words have the meanings below wherever they appear in this policy, in the singular or the plural. The Institute clauses keep their own meanings for the words they use, except where the Amendments to the Institute Clauses clause changes them.

Agreed Value

The value of the vessel shown in the schedule, which you and we agree is the value of the vessel for this policy, including for deciding whether it is a total loss.

Certificate of Operation

The certificate of operation issued for the vessel under the national law, which states its service category and operational area.

Certificate of Survey

The certificate of survey issued for the vessel under the national law, or an exemption from the need to hold one that applies to the vessel under the national law.

Crew

The master, and any other person employed or engaged by you to work on board the vessel.

Cyclone Season

The period from 1 November in one year to 30 April in the next.

Employee

A person employed by you under a contract of service, or a person who works for you and is treated as your worker under a workers' compensation law.

Endorsement

A clause from the endorsement library that applies to this policy because its code is shown in the schedule.

Excess

The amount you bear for each claim, as the schedule shows and the Excess provision sets out: the basic excess, the machinery excess, the protection and indemnity excess, the passenger excess, or a special excess shown for an endorsement.

Institute Clauses

The clauses listed in the Institute Clauses That Apply clause, and any other Institute clause an endorsement names, by number and date, as amended by this policy.

Lay-up

A period shown in the schedule during which the vessel is out of commission, carries no passengers or cargo, and stays at the place named in the schedule, except for movement within the port for repair, maintenance or safety.

Marina Berth

A pen, berth or pile mooring at a marina or boating facility that is managed by its operator and has controlled access.

Master

The person in command of the vessel.

Named Cyclone

A tropical cyclone that the Bureau of Meteorology has named, from the time the Bureau first issues a warning for it until the time it cancels the last warning.

National Law

The Marine Safety (Domestic Commercial Vessel) National Law in Schedule 1 to the Marine Safety (Domestic Commercial Vessel) National Law Act 2012 (Cth), and the regulations, marine orders, standards and exemptions made under it.

Northern Waters

Any place, on water or on land, north of latitude 26 degrees south.

Occurrence

An event, including continuous or repeated exposure to substantially the same conditions, that results in death, injury, illness, or loss of or damage to property that you did not expect or intend. All death, injury, illness, loss and damage arising from one source or original cause is one occurrence.

Operational Area

The operational area shown on the vessel's certificate of operation and in the schedule: B (within 200 nautical miles of the coast), C (restricted offshore, including C-restricted), D (partially smooth waters) or E (smooth waters), as the national law defines them.

Passenger

A person on board the vessel, or boarding or leaving it, who is not crew and is not carried in the course of your business to work on board.

Protection and Indemnity Limit

The most we pay under Protection and Indemnity for all claims arising from one occurrence, as shown in the schedule.

Policy Period

The period of insurance shown in the schedule.

Pollutant

Oil, fuel, sewage, garbage, chemicals or any other solid, liquid or gaseous substance that contaminates or harms water, land, air or living things.

Safety Management System

The safety management system the national law requires for the vessel and its operations.

Service Category

The vessel use category and the operational area shown together on the vessel's certificate of operation, such as 1D or 2C. The use categories are 1 (passenger vessel), 2 (non-passenger vessel), 3 (fishing vessel) and 4 (hire and drive vessel).

Swing Mooring

A mooring buoy attached to a block, chain or anchor on the seabed or riverbed, other than a marina berth.

Tender

A dinghy or other small boat, with its motor, carried on or towed by the vessel and used in its operations, up to the value shown in the schedule for it.

Total Loss

An actual total loss or a constructive total loss of the vessel, as the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) and the Institute clauses define them.

Vessel

The vessel named in the schedule, including its hull, machinery, equipment, fittings, furniture, stores and fuel, and its tender. The vessel does not include cargo, catch, the personal effects of any person, or fishing gear except as endorsement E14 provides.

We, Us and Our

The insurer named in the schedule.

You and Your

The named insured shown in the schedule, and any subsidiary or related entity named in the schedule.

Hull and Machinery

This section applies to every policy issued on this wording.

What We Insure

We insure the vessel during the policy period on the Institute clauses that the Institute Clauses That Apply clause lists for the conditions shown in the schedule, as amended by the Amendments to the Institute Clauses clause.

We pay for loss of or damage to the vessel caused by a peril those clauses insure, and the collision liability, general average and salvage contributions, and sue and labour costs those clauses provide for, subject to their exclusions, this wording and the schedule.

The most we pay for loss of or damage to the vessel from one accident or occurrence is the agreed value. The Institute clauses say which other amounts are paid in addition to the agreed value. A total loss is settled as the Settling a Claim for the Vessel provision sets out.

Institute Clauses That Apply

The Institute clauses that apply depend on the conditions shown in the schedule for this section.

Conditions in the scheduleInstitute clauses
Full conditionsInstitute Time Clauses Hulls CL280 1/10/83; Institute Additional Perils Clauses Hulls 1/10/83, with the Institute Machinery Damage Additional Deductible Clause dated 1/10/83
Total loss onlyInstitute Time Clauses Hulls Total Loss Only (including Salvage, Salvage Charges and Sue and Labour) CL289 1/10/83

The following also apply under either conditions:

  • Institute War and Strikes Clauses Hulls–Time CL281 1/10/83, as the War and Strikes clause sets out;
  • Institute Radioactive Contamination, Chemical, Biological, Bio-chemical and Electromagnetic Weapons Exclusion Clause CL370 10/11/03; and
  • Institute Cyber Attack Exclusion Clause CL380 10/11/03.

CL370 and CL380 apply to every section of this policy, not only to this section.

An endorsement may add another Institute clause: endorsement E09 for lay-up and endorsement E11 for disbursements and increased value.

The Text of the Institute Clauses condition explains where to find the text of these clauses.

Amendments to the Institute Clauses

Each Institute clause applies to this policy as amended below.

  1. Collision liability. Where the Institute clauses that apply include a collision liability clause, it applies as if it insured four-fourths of your liability instead of three-fourths. The added fourth is paid on the same terms as the rest, and within the same limit.
  2. Law and practice. Each provision of the Institute clauses that makes the insurance subject to English law and practice, or to the jurisdiction of the English courts, is replaced by the Australian Law and Practice condition and the Jurisdiction condition.
  3. Deductible. The deductible in the Institute Time Clauses Hulls is the basic excess shown in the schedule, and the deductible in the Institute Machinery Damage Additional Deductible Clause is the machinery excess. No excess applies to a claim for a total loss.
  4. Classification. The vessel is not required to be classed. Every provision of the Institute clauses about a classification society, the vessel's class or a class recommendation does not apply to a vessel that the schedule does not show as classed. The Certificates and Survey obligation applies in its place.
  5. Notice of claim and tenders. The provision of the Institute Time Clauses Hulls that reduces a claim by a percentage if you do not give notice of an accident or let us take tenders for repairs does not apply. The Tell Us About Loss obligation applies in its place.
  6. Change of ownership, flag, management or charter. Where the Institute clauses end this insurance automatically on a change of ownership, flag or management of the vessel, or on a bareboat charter, the Cancellation condition decides any return of premium.
  7. Negligence of repairers and charterers. To the extent the Institute Time Clauses Hulls do not already insure it, loss of or damage to the vessel caused by the negligence of a repairer or charterer of the vessel who is not insured under this policy is a peril insured, on the same terms as the perils that are subject to the due diligence proviso of those clauses.
  8. Returns of premium. A return of premium for lay-up applies only where endorsement E09 applies. Any other return is decided by the Cancellation condition.
  9. Warranties. Every warranty in the Institute clauses is a condition of this policy, as the Marine Insurance Act and Insurance Contracts Act condition sets out.

War and Strikes

The Institute War and Strikes Clauses Hulls–Time CL281 1/10/83 apply to the vessel. They insure the war, strikes, terrorism and malicious acts perils that the Institute Time Clauses Hulls exclude, subject to their own exclusions.

The provisions of CL281 that allow notice of cancellation, and that end its cover automatically on the events it names, apply to the cover CL281 gives and to nothing else. The rest of this policy continues.

No premium for the Australian Reinsurance Pool Corporation's terrorism cover is charged, because the Terrorism Insurance Act 2003 (Cth) does not apply to a contract of marine insurance. Additional Benefits

These benefits apply where the Hull and Machinery section is on full conditions. Each is paid in addition to the agreed value, up to the amount stated, or the amount shown in the schedule if it differs. No excess applies to them unless the benefit says so.

Equipment and Parts Ashore

We pay for loss of or damage to equipment and parts of the vessel while temporarily removed from it and stored ashore in Australia, or while carried in Australia to or from the vessel, caused by fire, flood, storm, theft following forcible entry, or a collision or overturning of the vehicle carrying them. The most we pay for one occurrence is 10% of the agreed value or $25,000, whichever is less. The basic excess applies.

Crew Personal Effects

We pay for loss of or damage to the clothing and personal effects of the crew while on board the vessel, caused by an accident that this section covers for the vessel, or by theft following forcible entry. The most we pay is $1,000 for each person and $5,000 for one occurrence. We do not pay for cash, cards, jewellery, or property of passengers.

Fire Extinguishing Costs

We pay the reasonable cost of recharging or replacing fire extinguishers and fixed fire-fighting systems on the vessel that were used to fight, or to prevent, a fire on board or near the vessel. The most we pay for one occurrence is $5,000.

Cyclone Movement Costs

If the Bureau of Meteorology issues a warning for a named cyclone that includes the place where the vessel is kept, we pay half of the reasonable cost of moving the vessel to a safer place or hauling it out of the water, and of returning it, whether or not the vessel is then damaged. The most we pay in the policy period is $5,000.

Protection and Indemnity

This section applies only if the schedule shows it.

What We Pay

We pay the amounts you become legally liable to pay, as owner or operator of the vessel, arising from an occurrence during the policy period in connection with the vessel or its operation, for:

(a) death of, or bodily injury or illness to, any person, including a passenger;

(b) loss of or damage to property, including another vessel and its cargo, a wharf, jetty, pontoon, mooring, buoy, navigation aid, aquaculture structure or fishing gear, and the loss of use of that property;

(c) collision with another vessel, to the extent your liability is more than Hull and Machinery pays for it, or is a liability that the collision liability clause of the Institute clauses does not insure;

(d) the cost of raising, removing, destroying, lighting or marking the wreck of the vessel, where a law or a public authority requires it or the wreck is a danger to navigation, less the value of anything recovered from it;

(e) pollution: liability, and the reasonable cost of preventing or cleaning up pollution, caused by a pollutant discharged or escaping from the vessel, or threatening to, where the discharge or escape is sudden and accidental, including costs a public authority recovers from you under a law; and

(f) the reasonable cost of diverting the vessel to land a sick or injured person, a stowaway or a person rescued at sea, including port charges, fuel and wages for the extra time.

We also pay the reasonable legal costs and expenses incurred with our written consent to investigate, defend or settle a claim under this section, and costs awarded against you. These costs are part of, not in addition to, the protection and indemnity limit.

This section does not pay a liability that Hull and Machinery pays.

Limits

The most we pay for all claims arising from one occurrence, including costs, is the protection and indemnity limit.

Within that limit, the most we pay for one occurrence is:

  • for pollution under paragraph (e) of the What We Pay clause: $1,000,000, or the amount shown in the schedule, and never less than endorsement E12 requires where it applies; and
  • for diversion costs under paragraph (f) of the What We Pay clause: $25,000.

Where you are entitled to limit your liability under the Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims Act 1989 (Cth) or any other law, we pay no more than your liability as limited, and the reasonable cost of establishing the limitation, within the protection and indemnity limit. We may ask you to claim limitation where you are entitled to, and you must not give up a right to limit without our written consent.

Defence and Settlement

We may take over and conduct the defence or settlement of any claim under this section in your name. You must give us the information and help we reasonably ask for.

We may at any time pay you the protection and indemnity limit for an occurrence, less any amount already paid for it, or any lower amount for which the claim can be settled. We then have no further obligation for that occurrence, except for costs we agreed to before that payment.

Employees

We do not pay for liability for the death of, or injury or illness to, your employee arising out of their employment, where it is covered, or required by law to be covered, under a workers' compensation law or scheme, including the Seafarers Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1992 (Cth). This exclusion does not apply to a liability that no such law requires you to insure.

Property You Own or Carry

We do not pay for liability for loss of or damage to property owned by, leased to or hired by you, or in the care, custody or control of you or your crew, including goods carried on the vessel. Your liability as a carrier of goods is not covered by this policy.

This exclusion does not apply to the clothing, baggage and personal effects of passengers, up to $1,000 for each passenger.

Liability Under Contract

We do not pay for liability you have only because you agreed to it under a contract, and that you would not have without the contract. This exclusion does not apply to a contract with a port, harbour, wharf or marina operator, on its standard terms, for the use of its facilities.

Fines and Penalties

We do not pay any fine, penalty, civil penalty or infringement notice imposed on you or your crew, or any punitive, aggravated or exemplary damages.

Swimming, Snorkelling and Diving

We do not pay for liability for the death of, or injury or illness to, a person in the water while swimming, snorkelling or diving from the vessel as an activity you organise, offer or allow, unless endorsement E04 or E05 applies to that activity. This exclusion does not apply to a person who falls or is thrown overboard, or whom the crew is rescuing.

Towage and Charter

We do not pay for liability arising while the vessel tows another vessel or object under a contract, unless endorsement E13 applies, or while the vessel tows a vessel in distress to save life or property. We do not pay for liability you have as charterer of another vessel, or to a charterer of the vessel.

War

We do not pay under this section for liability arising from war, invasion, act of foreign enemy, hostilities or warlike operations (whether war is declared or not), civil war, rebellion, revolution, insurrection, or military or usurped power, or from a mine, torpedo, bomb or other weapon of war. This section covers liability arising from strikes, riots, civil commotion, malicious acts and terrorism.

General Exclusions

These exclusions apply to every section, in addition to the exclusions in the Institute clauses. The Acts and Omissions After the Policy Starts condition applies to each of them.

Operations Not Insured

We do not pay for loss, damage, liability or cost arising while the vessel is:

  • performing salvage or towage under a contract made before the service was needed, except as endorsement E13 allows;
  • supporting offshore oil or gas exploration or production;
  • carrying oil, chemicals or gas in bulk as cargo, or dangerous goods as cargo for reward;
  • used for a thrill ride, for parasailing, or for towing people on inflatables or skis for reward;
  • bareboat or demise chartered to another person, other than a hire under endorsement E15;
  • under construction, before its delivery to you;
  • racing or in a speed trial; or
  • used outside Australia's territorial sea and exclusive economic zone, or on a voyage to or from a place outside Australia.

Deliberate Acts

We do not pay for loss, damage or liability caused deliberately by you, or by a person acting with your consent or at your direction. This exclusion does not apply to a deliberate act of the crew that you did not know of or consent to, where a peril insured by the Institute clauses covers it.

Illegal Use

We do not pay for loss, damage or liability arising while the vessel is used for an illegal purpose, including fishing that the law does not allow, or for the seizure, forfeiture or detention of the vessel, its gear or its catch by a public authority because of a breach of the law.

Your Obligations

These obligations apply to every section. They are conditions of cover, not warranties, and the Acts and Omissions After the Policy Starts condition applies to each of them.

Certificates and Survey

You must keep the vessel's certificate of operation and certificate of survey current, carry out the surveys and inspections the national law requires by their due dates, and comply with any direction, detention or prohibition notice that the Australian Maritime Safety Authority or a marine safety inspector gives for the vessel.

Operational Area

You must operate the vessel only within its operational area, except to save life, to escape danger, or to reach the nearest safe haven or place of repair. If the vessel goes outside its operational area for any other reason, tell us as soon as you know. We may charge an additional premium for the time it was outside.

Master and Crew

The master must hold the certificate of competency the national law requires for the vessel and its operations, and the vessel must carry the crew its certificate of operation requires whenever it is under way.

Passengers

You must not carry more passengers than the vessel's certificate of operation allows, and you must keep a record of the number of people on board for each voyage that carries passengers.

Safety Management System

You must keep, follow and update the vessel's safety management system, including its risk assessments, procedures, and drug, alcohol and fatigue management, as the national law requires.

Tell Us About Loss

You must tell us as soon as reasonably practicable about any accident, loss, damage, occurrence or demand that may give rise to a claim under this policy. Theft and malicious damage must be reported to the police. You must report marine incidents to the Australian Maritime Safety Authority as the national law requires.

Before repairs start, except emergency repairs, you must give us a reasonable chance to inspect the damage, and to obtain tenders for the repairs if we ask. We pay the reasonable cost of a survey we ask for. You must keep damaged parts available for inspection until we agree they may be disposed of.

Reduce the Loss

After an accident or occurrence, you and your crew must take reasonable steps to prevent or reduce loss, damage or liability, including salvaging and protecting the vessel. We pay the reasonable costs of doing so as the Institute clauses provide for the vessel, and as part of the protection and indemnity limit for a liability under Protection and Indemnity.

Admissions

You must not admit liability for, offer to settle, or settle any claim under Protection and Indemnity without our written consent, which we do not unreasonably withhold. This does not stop you from giving first aid or reporting the facts of an accident to an authority.

Tell Us About Changes

You must tell us within 30 days if, during the policy period, the vessel's service category or operational area changes, the vessel is modified in a way that requires a new survey or stability assessment, you start a new kind of operation with the vessel, the vessel is laid up for more than 30 days, or its home port moves to another state or territory. We may change the premium or terms for the rest of the policy period, in the way the Marine Insurance Act and Insurance Contracts Act condition allows.

How We Pay Claims

This part applies to every section.

Settling a Claim for the Vessel

If the vessel is a total loss, we pay the agreed value, without any excess, together with any other amount the Institute clauses provide for. The Institute clauses and the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) decide when the vessel is a constructive total loss, using the agreed value as the repaired value.

For partial loss or damage, we pay the reasonable cost of repairs, as the Institute clauses provide, less the excess.

If the schedule names a financier, we pay a claim for a total loss to the financier to the extent of its interest, and the rest to you.

Excess

For each claim you bear the excess that applies:

  • the basic excess, for each accident or occurrence under Hull and Machinery, except a total loss;
  • the machinery excess, for a claim to which the Institute Machinery Damage Additional Deductible Clause applies, as that clause provides;
  • the protection and indemnity excess, for each occurrence under Protection and Indemnity, including costs;
  • the passenger excess, instead of the protection and indemnity excess, for a claim for the death of, or injury or illness to, a passenger, where endorsement E03 applies; and
  • a special excess shown for an endorsement, where that endorsement says it applies.

Where one accident or occurrence gives rise to claims under both sections, you bear only the higher excess.

GST

Where you are registered for GST, we pay claims without GST to the extent you are entitled to an input tax credit for the cost. Tell us your entitlement when you make a claim. The agreed value, the protection and indemnity limit and the other amounts in this policy include GST.

Interest on Claims

If we do not pay a claim within the time that s.57 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) would allow if that Act applied, we pay interest on the amount at the rate that section and its regulations set, from the date it would require.

Other Insurance

If a loss covered by this policy is also covered by another policy, we will not refuse or reduce our payment because of that other insurance. We may seek contribution from the other insurer as the law allows. This does not apply where the schedule says this policy is excess of a named underlying policy.

General Conditions

These conditions apply to every section.

Marine Insurance Act and Insurance Contracts Act

This policy is a contract of marine insurance governed by the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth). The Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) does not apply to it of its own force.

By agreement, we give you these protections as if that Act applied:

  • the duty of utmost good faith, binding us as well as you (the Utmost Good Faith condition);
  • the duty of disclosure in s.21, and remedies limited as s.28 limits them (the Non-Disclosure and Misrepresentation condition);
  • the treatment of acts and omissions after the policy is entered into in s.54 (the Acts and Omissions After the Policy Starts condition);
  • interest on claims as s.57 allows (the Interest on Claims provision);
  • refusal of a fraudulent claim only as s.56 allows (the Fraud condition); and
  • cancellation only for the reasons s.60 allows (the Cancellation condition).

The obligations in this policy, including those in an endorsement, and every warranty in the Institute clauses, are conditions of cover. They are not warranties. We will not rely on a breach of any warranty, express or implied by the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth), to be discharged from liability. A breach of an implied warranty is treated as a breach of a condition, and the Acts and Omissions After the Policy Starts condition applies to it.

If the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) applies to this policy by law, it applies in full, and where this policy is inconsistent with it, that Act prevails.

Utmost Good Faith

You and we must each act towards the other with the utmost good faith in every matter arising under this policy, including in making, handling and paying claims. A failure by us to do so is a breach of this policy.

Non-Disclosure and Misrepresentation

If you did not comply with the duty of disclosure in the Important Information section, or made a misrepresentation to us, before this policy was entered into, and the failure or misrepresentation was not fraudulent, we may reduce our liability for a claim to the amount that would place us in the position we would have been in if you had complied or had not made it. We may not avoid this policy.

If the failure or misrepresentation was fraudulent, we may avoid this policy from its beginning.

The provisions of the Marine Insurance Act 1909 (Cth) that let an insurer avoid a contract for non-disclosure or misrepresentation do not apply, except for fraud.

Acts and Omissions After the Policy Starts

If this policy would otherwise let us refuse to pay a claim, in whole or in part, because of something you or another insured did or did not do after this policy was entered into, we may not refuse to pay the claim only because of that act or omission. Our liability is reduced by the amount that fairly represents the extent to which our interests were prejudiced.

Where the act or omission could reasonably be regarded as capable of causing or contributing to the loss, we may refuse to pay only the part of the claim that the act or omission caused or contributed to. We may not refuse any part that you prove the act or omission did not cause.

This applies to every condition, exclusion, limit and obligation in this policy, in an endorsement and in the Institute clauses, as s.54 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) would if that Act applied.

Order of Precedence

Where parts of your contract of insurance differ, they apply in this order: an endorsement shown in the schedule, then the schedule, then this wording, then the Institute clauses.

Two exceptions apply. CL370 and CL380 prevail over every other part of this policy, as they state. Nothing in an Institute clause, the schedule or an endorsement reduces a protection that the Marine Insurance Act and Insurance Contracts Act condition gives you.

Jurisdiction

Cancellation

You may cancel this policy at any time by telling us. We refund the premium for the unexpired policy period on a pro-rata basis, less any non-refundable government charges.

We may cancel this policy only for a reason for which s.60 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) would allow an insurer to cancel if that Act applied, by giving you at least 30 days' written notice. We refund the premium for the unexpired policy period on a pro-rata basis. The cover under CL281 may be cancelled on the shorter notice that CL281 allows, with a pro-rata refund of its premium.

If this insurance ends automatically under the Institute clauses, we refund the premium for the unexpired policy period on a pro-rata basis.

No refund is due if we have paid, or agreed to pay, a claim for a total loss of the vessel.

Cancellation does not affect a claim for an accident or occurrence that happened before it took effect.

Our Rights of Recovery

After we pay a claim, we may take over your rights to recover from anyone else, in your name, and you must cooperate with us. You must not give up or limit a right of recovery without our written consent, except under a contract with a port, harbour, wharf or marina operator on its standard terms.

We will not seek recovery from your employee or crew, unless the loss arose from their serious or wilful misconduct, or from a subsidiary or related entity of yours that charters the vessel from you.

Fraud

If a claim is made fraudulently, we may refuse to pay it, as s.56 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) would allow if that Act applied.

How to Make a Claim

  1. Make the vessel, its passengers and crew safe, and take reasonable steps to prevent further loss (see the Reduce the Loss obligation).
  2. Contact your broker, or contact us directly at the details shown in the schedule.
  3. Report the marine incident to the Australian Maritime Safety Authority as the national law requires, and report any theft or malicious damage to the police.
  4. Tell us what happened and when, and give us photographs, the vessel's logbook entries, the passenger count and any survey or incident report.
  5. Do not start repairs, other than emergency repairs, until we have had a chance to inspect the damage (see the Tell Us About Loss obligation).
  6. For a claim against you, send us the demand as soon as it is received, and do not admit liability (see the Admissions obligation).
  7. We will acknowledge the claim, keep you informed, and tell you our decision.
